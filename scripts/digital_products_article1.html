Building a full business from scratch takes time most people living and working in Korea simply don't have. But a smaller category of side income — digital products you build once and keep selling — fits around a normal work schedule because the product does the selling for you after the first version is done. Here are five that consistently work, along with what makes each one realistic to start.

1. Checklists for a specific, stressful task

A checklist sells well because it replaces anxiety with a clear sequence of steps. The strongest examples are narrow and specific — "Alien Registration Card renewal checklist" or "apartment move-out checklist for tenants in Korea" — rather than a generic productivity checklist that competes with thousands of free versions. Keep it to one page, add short explanations under each line item, and price it low enough that buying it is an easy decision.

2. Spreadsheet calculators

A well-built Google Sheets or Excel calculator solves a math problem someone doesn't want to redo by hand: a rent-versus-buy comparison, a severance pay estimate, or a monthly budget split across won and a foreign currency. The value is in the formulas, not the design — test the calculator with real numbers before selling it, and include a short instructions tab so a buyer isn't stuck guessing which cells to edit.

3. Notion templates for recurring life admin

Notion templates work when they organize something people already have to track anyway — visa document expiry dates, a job search pipeline, or a household budget shared between two people. A template earns repeat interest when it's genuinely reusable month after month, not just a one-time setup. Duplicate the template into a fresh workspace before publishing it, to confirm every linked database and formula still works for someone who has never seen it before.

4. Short guided workbooks (PDF)

A workbook goes one step further than a checklist: it walks the reader through a decision, with space to fill in their own answers. Examples that hold up well include a workbook for comparing job offers side by side, or one for planning a first year of freelance income and taxes. Because it is a PDF, it can be resold indefinitely once it's written and lightly designed.

5. Presentation templates

PowerPoint or Google Slides templates sell to a narrower but consistent audience — people preparing a specific kind of presentation repeatedly, such as a monthly team report, a client proposal, or a study group summary. The template needs a genuinely useful structure, not just a color scheme; buyers are paying to skip the layout work, so the slide order and placeholder text should already reflect how the presentation is normally organized.

What these five have in common

Each one solves a specific, recurring problem instead of trying to be broadly useful. None of them require ongoing delivery once they are built, and all five can be tested for free first — post the checklist or workbook outline in a relevant community, see if anyone asks for it, and only then invest the time in polishing the paid version.

This is general information for planning purposes, not financial or tax advice; confirm anything specific to your own situation with a licensed professional.
