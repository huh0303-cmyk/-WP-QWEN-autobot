The first five build-once-sell-repeatedly ideas covered checklists, calculators, templates, workbooks and slide decks. These next five lean more on a specific skill or professional background, which means less competition and a higher price per sale — but they also take more work to put together properly.

1. Job-transition packages

A job-transition package bundles a resume template, a cover-letter template, and a short guide on how to fill both out for a specific market — for example, applying to English-teaching jobs, or moving from an in-house role to freelance consulting. What makes this sell is specificity: a generic resume template competes with free ones everywhere, but "resume and cover letter templates formatted for E-7 visa job applications" solves a problem a much smaller, much more motivated group is actively searching for.

2. Toolkits for online sellers

Anyone running a small online storefront needs a repeatable set of tools: a product description formula, a pricing spreadsheet that accounts for fees and shipping, and a simple content calendar for promotional posts. Packaged together as a seller toolkit, these save a new seller the weeks it usually takes to figure out the same system by trial and error. The strongest version of this product comes from someone who has actually run a store and can point to what worked.

3. Short workbook-style mini-courses

Not every course needs video. A mini-course built as a structured PDF or a short Notion-based lesson series — with a clear before/after outcome, like "go from zero to your first freelance client in four weeks" — is faster to produce than video and just as sellable if the steps are genuinely actionable rather than motivational filler.

4. Stock photo or graphic packs

For anyone with a decent camera or basic design skills, a themed photo or graphic pack — Korean street food, minimalist workspace shots, seasonal social media templates — can be licensed repeatedly to small businesses and content creators who need visuals but don't want to shoot or design their own. Consistency of style across the whole pack matters more than the size of the pack.

5. B2B document templates

Contracts, proposals, and standard operating procedure templates are less exciting to build than creative products, but businesses pay well for documents that save a lawyer's or consultant's hourly rate. A freelance contract template written for a specific type of work, or an SOP template for onboarding a new employee, are both examples that a small business owner will pay for once and reuse for years.

Putting the ten together

Across both lists, the pattern holds: the products that keep selling are narrow, solve a real recurring task, and come from someone who has actually done the task themselves. Before building any of these, it's worth checking whether the specific problem is one people are already asking about in forums or communities — that's a faster, free way to validate demand than building the full product first.
