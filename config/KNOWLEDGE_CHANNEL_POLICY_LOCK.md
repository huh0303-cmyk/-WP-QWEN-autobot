# Knowledge channels — owner instructions, 2026-09-07

This policy supersedes older wordless-silent-film and fixed-length instructions.

All five channels: collect actual reusable footage, inspect actual frames, write each scene against its footage, synthesize an ElevenLabs documentary narrator, then pair each scene with its own narration and timed captions. No source loops, unrelated filler, or silent TTS fallback. Save scene_manifest.json with source records, narration, alignment and timing. Private upload only; public release remains the owner's decision.

1. NASA Science: space topics from NASA institutional footage. Third-party credits must be checked; a NASA host alone is not a rights grant.
2. Today in World History: the content date in KST, several sourced world events on that month/day; newest year to oldest. Keep the date and year visible for each event. Select interesting supported events, omit events without relevant footage. About 10–15 minutes when useful; shorter is acceptable.
3. Invention: engaging “Did you know?” stories about telephones, electrical technology, cars, computers, the Internet, lamps, clocks, coffee and other everyday origins. Distinguish inventions, discoveries and incremental contributions. Roughly 3–15 minutes, no minimum padding.
4. Old Hollywood (existing silent_era channel identity): silent and black-and-white cinema with high-quality documentary narration. Chaplin or early animation only when the particular film/version/recording has documented reusable rights; a character name or age alone is insufficient.
5. Retro USA (existing retro_reels identity): American everyday life in the 1960s–2000s: homes, washing machines, children, sweets, disco and social routines. Actual archive footage first, specific grounded nostalgia second.

Source discovery priorities: NASA, Library of Congress, US National Archives, Smithsonian, institutional university collections and item-level rights-cleared archive films. Government/university hosting does not automatically imply permission. Existing automated connectors cover NASA, Internet Archive and LOC; other institutional connectors are not yet implemented.

Thumbnail: minimal words, a clear relevant subject, no misleading historical reconstruction. Short opening hook; natural runtime capped at 15 minutes rather than stretched to a target. Existing private-upload receipt verification remains mandatory.
