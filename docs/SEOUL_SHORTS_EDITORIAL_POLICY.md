## Thumbnail / opening-frame design for Shorts

Shorts discovery commonly serves the video in a vertical feed; the opening frame therefore carries the visual hook. Design a distinct, readable first frame and cover image where the publishing surface permits:
- 9:16 vertical composition, face or relevant scene large and recognizable.
- Use a genuine, editorially relevant photograph or licensed/authorized footage as the background; preserve visual context and avoid unrelated stock imagery.
- One short Korean headline, ideally 3–8 words; emphasize the key quote or question. Keep text away from interface overlays and leave safe margins.
- High contrast, restrained palette, clean typography, no tiny body copy or clutter.
- Use distinct visuals and headline phrasing for every upload. Do not repeat the same image/card across different Shorts.
- Choose a compelling but accurate frame. Do not imply a person said or did something absent from the source.
- For YouTube Shorts, do not assume a separately uploaded custom thumbnail is available on every device or workflow. Preserve a strong opening frame and select the cover frame during upload if the current YouTube interface supports it.

## Short-form edit structure (adapt, do not clone)

1. 0–2s: Start on the real moment, a direct question, or a specific consequence; put the key phrase on-screen immediately.
2. 2–8s: Identify who, what happened, and when; show relevant person/event imagery.
3. Middle: Deliver one core fact and a brief second perspective or missing context; use short caption beats and purposeful visual changes.
4. Ending: Give a concrete takeaway or an open, neutral question. Avoid fabricated cliffhangers and repetitive “watch to the end” filler.
5. Keep duration story-driven rather than fixed. Remove pauses; retain enough context for claims and quotations to be understood.

## Voice, footage, and political accuracy

- Use the public figure's voice only when it is the authentic audio of a sourced recording and its reuse is authorized or otherwise cleared for the intended use. Attribute the speaker and preserve meaning/context.
- Do not clone, synthesize, or imitate a real politician's voice to make new statements. Never make generated audio appear to be a genuine quote.
- If authentic, cleared audio is unavailable, use a clearly editorial narrator voice (news-anchor/documentary style), not an imitation of the person.
- Use properly licensed, user-owned, permissioned, or otherwise cleared photos and footage. A public figure's fame does not itself grant reuse rights.
- Separate confirmed fact, allegation, opinion, and analysis in narration and captions. Add date/source context for fast-moving political news; check both relevant claims and responses.
- Avoid one-sided editing that changes a quote's meaning. Label AI-generated or materially altered visuals/audio when required by platform policy.

## Before publishing

- Verify the event and source links; check the person's identity, date, quote, and any countervailing context.
- Confirm the still/background is relevant and usable; add attribution where needed.
- Preview vertical safe areas, caption spelling, contrast, opening-frame readability, and audio synchronization.
- Check that title and cover promise exactly what the Short delivers.
- Record performance by channel and topic; compare early retention and viewed-vs-swiped-away alongside views. A single breakout does not guarantee future reach.
