# 서울저널 · 서울지수 쇼츠 썸네일 스타일 가이드

Status: Approved production standard  
Applies to: Seoul Journal and Seoul Jisoo vertical Shorts, 9:16 video cover and opening frames.

## Reference direction

Use the supplied reference screenshots as the visual baseline: Korean political/news Shorts and newsroom explainers with a real, recognizable central figure; high-contrast editorial color; short, oversized Korean copy; and a clear visual hierarchy. Study the public packaging approach of 이재명TV, 주진우TV, and 한동훈TV for direct-to-camera political framing, recognizable speakers, punchy but factual phrasing, and readable captions. This is visual/editorial reference only; do not copy their logos, exact layouts, wording, or footage.

## Five rotating templates

1. **속보 인물 클로즈업** — large real photo of the principal political figure, red “속보” badge in a top corner, two-line central headline with white/yellow emphasis, dark gradient behind text.
2. **발언 대 반박** — two relevant figures or one figure plus a source image, split composition, cyan/white main quote and yellow/red counterpoint; clearly attribute each statement.
3. **쟁점 숫자·정책 브리핑** — figure on one side, a single verified number/chart/document on the other; large central keyword and concise supporting phrase.
4. **국회·현장 리포트** — relevant real scene as background with a cutout/portrait of the key figure; “현장/브리핑/국회” label and high-contrast central summary.
5. **투표형 쟁점 카드** — main figure(s) at top or sides; central question in large text; lower prompt “찬성 vs 반대” or named options, used as the visual lead-in to the closing audience poll.

Choose randomly among the five for each new video, while avoiding the same template on consecutive videos when run history is available.

## Person and image rules

- Political coverage must feature the actual principal public figure(s) when a suitable, legally usable photograph is available. Prioritize major figures relevant to the story, including 이재명, 한동훈, 장동혁, 오세훈, 김민석, 정청래, 조국 and other central figures named in verified reporting.
- Prefer article-provided, official, or clearly licensed/attributed images. Never fabricate a politician’s photograph, imply a file photo depicts the current scene, or use an unrelated person as a stand-in.
- Where suitable imagery cannot be verified, use a clearly labeled document/venue graphic and stop treating it as a photo of the person.
- Keep attribution and license/source information in the video description/package. Do not place long credits over the headline.

## Central copy and captions

- The principal headline/caption must sit in the central safe area of the 9:16 frame, around 35–65% of frame height; do not bury all copy at the bottom.
- Use 1–3 short lines, normally 8–18 Korean characters per line, sized for older viewers and small phone screens. Prefer bold Noto Sans CJK; use black outline/shadow and a translucent dark backing when the image is busy.
- Use no more than two highlight colors in addition to white. Editorial palette: breaking red + yellow/white; briefing navy + cyan/white; debate black/navy + yellow/cyan.
- Keep key faces unobstructed. Respect YouTube Shorts UI safe margins at top and bottom.
- In-video narration captions should be centered, high contrast, synchronized by phrase, and large enough to read. Avoid a static poster held for the entire Short when source imagery supports multiple relevant shots.

## Editorial integrity

- Hook with the verified core dispute, not an unsupported accusation.
- Attribute claims to the speaker/source; distinguish allegations, rebuttals, confirmed facts, and unknowns.
- For political debate, structure: 핵심 발언 → 상대 반박 → 확인된 사실 → 시청자 투표.
- Always finish with a neutral audience poll, such as “여러분은 찬성입니까, 반대입니까?” or a balanced set of relevant named options.
- The thumbnail headline, spoken script, and source article must agree. No misleading implication that a still photo is current-event footage.
- Seoul Journal is political/news editorial. Seoul Jisoo uses the same five visual templates and legibility rules, adapted to its selected topic.
