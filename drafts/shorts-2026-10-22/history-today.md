**Narration**
On October 22, 1962, U.S. President John F. Kennedy went on television with a message that raised the stakes of the Cold War.

He announced that American intelligence had identified Soviet missile sites in Cuba and ordered a naval quarantine to stop additional offensive weapons from reaching the island.

The word “quarantine” mattered: the administration chose it instead of “blockade,” which carried a stronger implication of an act of war.

The announcement did not end the crisis. The following days brought military alert, tense exchanges, and urgent diplomacy before a settlement was reached.

The lesson is not that one speech solved everything. It is that words, military decisions, and private negotiations can all shape the path away from catastrophe.
