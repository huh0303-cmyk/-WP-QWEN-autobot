A Korean wedding costs about 21.39 million won on average, according to a Korea Consumer Agency (KCA) survey of 351 wedding venues reported by Seoul Economic Daily, and the largest single item is the meal. For international couples, the practical points beyond cost are the contract's cancellation terms and the paperwork that proves the marriage. This article lays out the figures reported, the refund standards that apply, and a short list of questions for venues.

What the survey found

Seoul Economic Daily's report of the KCA survey covers venues in 14 regions. Besides the 21.39 million won average, it gives a median venue rental of 3.5 million won and a studio, dress and makeup package of 2.94 million won. Meals were 119,000 won per person for course meals, 62,000 won for buffet and 55,000 won for Korean traditional set meals. The survey also lists minimum guest numbers: 218 for course meals, 199 for Korean set meals and 194 for buffet. In top-tier regions such as Seoul, the meal was 120,000 won per person with 224 minimum guests, and in lower-tier regions such as Busan it was 102,000 won with 102 minimum guests.

The Korea Times, forecasting costs, reports an average catering price of 65,000 won per person nationally, 77,000 won in the capital region and 96,000 won in Gangnam, and says catering is about 60 percent of total expenses. Its overall averages differ from the KCA figure because it uses a different measure, so compare numbers only within the same source.

Timing and season

The Korea Times forecast projects September costs at 21.58 million won and October at 20.62 million won, which suggests season affects the price. Ask venues whether dates in different months or on weekdays cost less, and compare quotes for the same guest count and meal type, because a lower hall fee can be offset by a higher meal price.

Why the guest minimum matters

A minimum guest count can raise the bill beyond your actual guest list. If a hall requires a minimum of about 200 guests at 62,000 won for a buffet and you invite 80, you may still be charged for the minimum. Ask every venue to state in writing whether the minimum is binding, and what happens if fewer people come. This point is especially relevant for international couples whose overseas relatives may not be able to attend.

Cancellation and deposit rules

The Financial News (fnnews) reports that the Korea Fair Trade Commission's consumer dispute standards say that even if a wedding contract is cancelled due to the consumer's fault, the deposit should in principle be refunded if notice is given at least 150 days before the wedding date. The same article reports that wedding-related relief applications rose from 281 to 720 cases over a few years, and that 81.4 percent of cumulative cases concerned contract cancellation and penalty disputes. It advises couples to review cancellation penalties and be cautious if terms are more burdensome than the standards.

The English edition of the standards on the KCA website also lists wedding planning and wedding photography services. For wedding planning, it lists a refund minus 10% of the total charge when the consumer cancels before service begins. For photography, it lists the business keeping 10% of the fee when the consumer is at fault. That edition is dated, and the current Korean standards may differ, so ask the KCA which version applies to your contract.

Paperwork for an international couple

A Korean consulate page on the F-6 marriage migrant visa states that the marriage must be registered in both Korea and the home country, and lists a translated marriage certificate among the documents. Because a wedding ceremony and a legal registration are separate things, confirm what your consulate needs, and do not assume a ceremony alone creates the legal record.

Questions to put to every venue

  1. What is the price per person, and what does it include?
  2. What is the minimum guest number, and is it binding?
  3. How much is the deposit, and what is the refund schedule if we cancel?
  4. What happens to our deposit if the venue changes the date or terms?
  5. Are studio, dress, makeup and photography included, and are they a separate contract?
  6. Is there an interpreter or English-speaking coordinator?

Keep every quote, message and receipt in one folder. If a dispute arises over a deposit or a change in terms, dated records are the first thing a consumer counseling body will ask to see, so ask the venue to confirm any verbal change by message or email.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Comparing averages from different sources. They measure different things.
  • Paying a deposit before reading the refund terms. Compare them with the consumer standards.
  • Missing the 150-day point. The standards reported tie refunds to notice time.
  • Confusing the ceremony with the legal registration.

Rules change: confirm with the official bodies

This is general information, not legal advice. Consumer standards, prices and visa rules change. Confirm the current standards with the Korea Consumer Agency or the Fair Trade Commission, and visa documents with the Korean consulate, and read any contract fully, with a translator if needed, before you sign.
