Start with an official starting point such as the Study in Korea job pages or the government's Work24 portal, then follow each lead to the employer's own posting before you apply. Study in Korea, the government portal for international students, lists several recruitment platforms for foreigners and links to Work24's job search, while Work24 itself offers searches by occupation, region and theme. This article shows how to move from a listing to a real application and what to check along the way.

The official starting points

Study in Korea has a "Job Opportunities" page that combines integrated job listings with a feed from Work24, the Ministry of Employment and Labor's portal. Its filters include location, occupation (for example IT, marketing, finance, design, education and languages), employment type (full-time, contract, intern, dispatched, freelance, part-time) and an "E-7 Visa Support" option. That last filter is useful because a foreign applicant often needs an employer willing to support a work visa.

Work24 is the Korean-language portal. Its main page has a recruitment information section with detailed search, searches by occupation and region, a themed search, a recruitment calendar, jobs overseas and nearby listings. It also hosts job fairs and employment support programmes. If your Korean is limited, start on Study in Korea and use Work24 once you can read the listing fields or have someone to help.

Other platforms named on Study in Korea

The portal's employment page lists a range of resources. According to that page:

  • KOTRA Contact Korea is described as a global talent sourcing service.
  • Seoul Global Center offers multilingual counselling and job fairs for foreign workers.
  • Busan Job Information Network supports international students in Busan, including visa linkage and regional-specific visa projects.
  • KoMate by Saramin provides a matching service with foreigner authentication and translations into 30 languages.
  • Wanted Lab Global is a recruitment service for foreign talent in digital jobs.
  • KOWORK is described as a foreigner-exclusive platform with a Korean-style résumé generator and visa support guidance.
  • Incruit provides recruitment information from internships to senior roles.

The page also says to check the websites of companies you are interested in, city and provincial offices, and university employment support centres. A survey quoted there (156 international students, multiple answers) reported that 60 percent used recruitment platforms and 32.3 percent used university career offices. Treat this as one small survey, not a national statistic.

From listing to employer: a four-step check

  1. Record the basics. Write down the employer, the work location, the closing date and the original application route.
  2. Find the employer's own posting. A platform listing can be a summary or a copy, so look for the same role on the company's recruitment page.
  3. Read the duties, not only the title. The portal's own list of job types shows how broad a category can be: marketing, for example, covers multilingual content, overseas market research and customer support.
  4. Check the visa position. Study in Korea explains that students can begin job seeking after converting a student visa to a job-seeking visa, and that foreigners must check the employment qualification information for their status before working. Ask the employer directly whether they support it.

The recruitment process you are entering

Study in Korea describes five typical stages: reviewing the job announcement, submitting the application, document screening, interviews, and final selection. It notes that interviews are based on the contents of your documents, so your cover letter should show how your abilities match the duties. Companies assess academic background, experience, certifications and language skills at the screening stage, and the interview can include a technical interview, a personality interview, a presentation or an executive interview, depending on the company.

For a résumé, the portal recommends using the company's own format if one is provided, listing education from the highest level downward, listing experience such as internships and projects in reverse chronological order, and mentioning Korean proficiency where relevant. For email applications it suggests a subject line in the form "[Job Application] Company Name/Job Title_Your Name". It also warns that after you submit through a company's recruitment site you may not be able to view your application, so save your content first.

Language level and documents

The portal says most Korean companies require a certain level of Korean for daily communication, and states that TOPIK level 4 or above is typically required. It also lists English tests and job-specific certificates as useful. Because requirements differ by employer, treat this as a general guide and read each posting. Before accepting any offer, the portal's pre-employment checklist includes opening a bank account, checking enrolment in the four major insurances (National Pension, Health Insurance, Employment Insurance and Industrial Accident Compensation Insurance) and reviewing the written work contract.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Applying from a repost. Confirm the vacancy on the employer's own page and check the closing date.
  • Ignoring visa support. A role that cannot support your visa status wastes both sides' time.
  • Using one résumé for every role. Match two or three examples to the advertised duties.
  • Losing your submission. Keep a copy of every application, since some sites do not let you view it afterwards.
  • Skipping the contract. Read pay, duties and insurance terms before you sign.

Rules change: confirm with the official bodies

This is general information, not legal or immigration advice. Visa categories, language requirements and platform features change, and each employer sets its own conditions. Confirm visa questions with HiKorea or your local immigration office and employment questions with the Ministry of Employment and Labor or the employer.
