The best way to guide a wedding photographer in Korea is to put your wishes in writing before the day: a short shot list, a list of the people who matter most, and the contract terms for what is included and what happens if plans change. In Korea, photography is often sold inside a studio, dress and makeup package, which the Korea Consumer Agency (KCA) survey reported by Seoul Economic Daily prices at 2.94 million won on average. This article covers how to brief a photographer and which contract points to check. It does not describe studio styles or prices beyond what those sources report.

Know what you are buying

Seoul Economic Daily's report of the KCA survey of 351 venues lists a studio, dress and makeup package at 2.94 million won, next to a median venue rental of 3.5 million won. The Korea Times, in its cost forecast, notes that it did not itemize photography, dress and makeup separately. In practice, that means you should not assume that a package price tells you how many hours of shooting, how many edited images or which albums are included. Ask for each item in writing.

  • Hours of coverage on the day, and whether any pre-wedding shoot is separate.
  • Number of photographers, and whether the person you meet is the person who shoots.
  • Number of edited images, and the delivery date and format.
  • Album, prints and frames: included, or extra.
  • Rights to share the images online and to print them freely.

Write a short shot list

A photographer can only capture what they know matters. Prepare one page, and send it a week or more before the wedding:

  1. The key moments in order: arrival, ceremony, family photos, bouquet or other customs you plan to include.
  2. Family groupings you want, written as names and relationships, such as both sets of parents, siblings and grandparents.
  3. Any customs from either side that should be photographed, with a short note on what happens in them.
  4. People who are not to be photographed, or moments that are private.
  5. One or two example images of the style you like, and one of what you do not.

For an international couple, add the guests' names in both spellings if useful, and ask a friend who speaks Korean to share the list with the photographer in advance. A short bilingual sheet avoids misunderstandings on the day.

Build a simple day timeline

Share a timeline with the photographer that lists the start time of each moment, who needs to be present for each group photo, and who will help gather family. A relative or friend who knows the guests by sight can call people to the photo area, which saves time. Keep the family photo list short, so the group shots do not take over the schedule.

Plan for the venue

The photographer works inside the venue's schedule. Ask the venue, in writing, how long the ceremony slot is, where family photos can be taken and whether outside photographers are allowed or charged an extra fee. The sources used here do not describe venue photography rules, so ask each venue directly instead of assuming they are standard. A rehearsal visit with the photographer, even a short one, can reveal lighting and space problems.

Contract terms that protect you

The KCA's English edition of the consumer dispute standards includes a wedding photography category. It lists a refund of the deposit plus 10% compensation if the business is at fault and cancels before shooting, and the business keeping 10% of the total fee if the consumer is at fault. That edition is dated, and current standards may differ, so ask the KCA which version applies.

Financial News reports that the Korea Fair Trade Commission's standards say that even if a wedding contract is cancelled because of the consumer's fault, the deposit should in principle be refunded when notice is given at least 150 days before the wedding. That article concerns wedding venues, and it notes similar issues with attire services, so do not assume the 150-day point covers your photography contract. Ask the studio what refund schedule it applies, and compare it with the consumer standards.

After the wedding: delivery and complaints

Write down the promised delivery date and the number of images. If the studio is late or the photos do not match the agreement, keep your messages and contract, then raise the problem with the studio first. Financial News reports that wedding-related relief applications rose sharply, so disputes are not rare. The Korea Consumer Agency is the body to ask about consumer counseling and dispute resolution.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Accepting a verbal promise. Get image count, hours and dates in writing.
  • Sending no brief. A shot list takes an hour and prevents missed family photos.
  • Assuming venue rules. Ask about outside photographers and time limits.
  • Confusing venue and photography refund terms. They are separate contracts.

Rules change: confirm with the official bodies

This is general information, not legal advice. Prices, consumer standards and venue policies change. Confirm the current consumer dispute standards with the Korea Consumer Agency or the Korea Fair Trade Commission, and read your contract carefully before paying a deposit.
