University admission requirements for international students in Korea are set by each university, but two patterns repeat: you must prove your education and your language ability, and the documents must be authenticated and translated to the university's standard. Seoul National University and Yonsei University publish enough detail to show what a typical set looks like, and the differences between them are a reminder to read your own target university's guide, not a general checklist.

Who counts as an international applicant

Seoul National University's admissions FAQ describes two tracks. International Admissions I requires the applicant and parents to be non-Korean citizens with high school completion. International Admissions II accepts applicants with Korean or foreign citizenship who completed all their education outside Korea. The FAQ says that a dual citizen applying under the first track and their parents must all renounce Korean nationality before the application deadline.

Yonsei University's international application guide, in the version for a March intake, similarly says that applicants and both parents must hold non-Korean nationality and that applicants with dual citizenship including Korean nationality are not eligible. Eligibility is the first thing to check, because it decides which admissions office you apply to.

Language proficiency: Korean or English, depending on the programme

SNU's FAQ says applicants need either Korean or English proficiency, not both. For Korean it accepts TOPIK level 3 or higher, and for English it lists TOEFL iBT 80, TEPS 551 and IELTS 6.0 or higher.

Yonsei's guide takes a wider approach for most majors, letting applicants choose from a language test score, a certificate of completion from a Korean language institution, or official evidence of coursework in Korean or English. For pre-medicine, pre-dentistry, nursing and pharmacy, it requires a TOPIK report at level 5 or above, or an equivalent Yonsei Korean-language certification. The lesson is that the same score can be enough for one programme and not for another.

Documents you should expect to prepare

From the Yonsei guide, a typical undergraduate set includes:

  • The completed application form and an academic history record sheet.
  • A personal statement, which the guide says is now mandatory.
  • A high school diploma or graduation certificate with authentication.
  • Official transcripts for the final three high school years.
  • Proof of the parents' non-Korean nationality and family relationship documents.
  • Language proficiency certificates.

SNU's FAQ says that graduation certificates and transcripts are acceptable if they qualify you for university admission in your home country, that passports of the applicant and parents work without notarisation, and that other documents need a notarised translation. Check the same details in your target university's guide, since the list differs between schools.

Authentication: apostille and translation

Yonsei's guide says academic documents need either an apostille stamp or consular authentication through a Korean embassy, that Chinese graduates need verification through CHSI, and that documents in languages other than English or Korean need a notarised translation plus authentication. SNU's FAQ adds that the apostille is required only after preliminary admission is announced, and suggests checking whether your country belongs to the Hague Convention. Because timing differs, ask whether the university wants authenticated documents at application or only after an offer, so you neither order too early nor miss a deadline.

Fees and what follows admission

SNU's FAQ lists application fees of 70,000 won for undergraduate and 90,000 won for graduate programmes and says no fee waivers are available. Other universities set their own amounts, so check the guide.

After admission, you need a student visa. According to study-abroad.org's visa guide, D-2 applications usually require a valid passport with at least six months of validity, photos, the certificate of admission, a bank statement showing a stated balance held for a month or more, apostilled transcripts and degrees, language test scores and a tuberculosis test for some nationalities. The Yonsei guide gives its own financial threshold for the certificate of admission, a bank balance certificate of at least 22 million won in the version we read. Because amounts differ between universities and embassies, ask your university and the Korean embassy in your country which figure applies to you.

A checklist you can adapt

  1. Confirm which admissions track applies to you and your parents' nationality.
  2. Check the programme's language requirement and the exact accepted tests and scores.
  3. List every document the guide asks for, with the date it must be issued and its format.
  4. Confirm whether authentication is needed at application or after an offer.
  5. Order notarised translations where required.
  6. Pay the application fee through the official portal and keep the receipt.
  7. After an offer, prepare the visa documents and financial proof for your embassy.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Using a requirement list from a different university or an old intake.
  • Assuming TOPIK level 3 is enough for every programme.
  • Authenticating all documents before checking when they are needed.
  • Sending only an untranslated document when the guide asks for a notarised translation.
  • Using one financial figure from a blog without checking your embassy's requirement.

Rules change: confirm with the official body

This is general information, not immigration or legal advice. Admission rules, fees, language scores and visa requirements change from year to year and differ by university. Confirm with the university's admissions office, the official Study in Korea portal and the Korean embassy in your country.
