Most phone and app preparation for a Korea trip comes down to five things: a way to get mobile data, a map app that works well locally, a transit card, a payment method, and the online entry paperwork. Sorting these out before you fly means you can leave the airport with a working phone and a clear idea of how you will pay and get around. This checklist covers each item and points out where you should confirm details yourself.

Step 1: Decide how you will get mobile data

Korea has a lot of free Wi-Fi, but you will still want your own connection for maps, ride-hailing and booking tickets on the move. LG U+, one of the local carriers, describes reliable connectivity as important for booking intercity travel and checking business hours before visiting shops. The carrier also gives rough weekly data guidelines for planning: about 1 GB per week for light use, 3-5 GB per week for moderate use, and 10 GB or more for heavy use.

Those figures come from a carrier's own travel FAQ, so treat them as a starting point rather than a rule. A simple way to choose:

  • Light use (messages, occasional map lookups): lean on Wi-Fi and a small data allowance.
  • Moderate use (navigation, photos, video calls now and then): plan for a mid-sized plan.
  • Heavy use (streaming, hotspot for a laptop): look for a large or unlimited plan.

Before you buy any SIM, eSIM or rental, check that your own phone model is supported and unlocked. That is a question for the seller or your home carrier, because it depends on your device.

Step 2: Know what free public Wi-Fi can and cannot do

According to a Stripes Korea guide, the government and major telecom companies operate a "Public Wi-Fi" service that is available on trains, buses, inside traditional markets, community centers and other places. The guide says you can locate networks through the wififree.kr website or its app, and that secured networks may ask for a login that the guide lists as "wifi" for both username and password.

The same guide gives a clear warning. It advises avoiding Wi-Fi networks from unknown providers because they may collect personal information, and it recommends against sensitive transactions such as financial services or corporate business on unsecured networks. It also notes that an encrypted connection may reduce speed. In practice, that means public Wi-Fi is fine for browsing and checking schedules, but not for logging in to your bank.

Step 3: Install local map and transit apps

LG U+ lists several apps for getting around: Naver Map and Kakao Map for finding your position, navigating to destinations and discovering nearby places; Kakao Metro and Kakao Bus for transit schedules and real-time bus locations; Ddareungi, which it describes as Seoul's bike-sharing app; and Kakao Taxi and T-map Taxi as ride-hailing alternatives.

Set these up at home rather than at the arrivals gate. Create any required accounts, switch the app language if the option exists, and save your hotel name and address in Korean. The sources used for this article do not describe how well global map apps perform in Korea, so if you rely on one, test its walking and transit directions for your own route before you travel.

Step 4: Sort out the transit card

According to VISITKOREA, the T-money card can be bought at convenience stores such as GS25, CU and 7-ELEVEN and at information centers in Seoul subway stations on Lines 1-8. It lists the card price as 3,000-5,000 won and says it works on all public transportation and at affiliated stores showing the card logo, including expressway toll gates and vending machines.

For recharging, VISITKOREA says you can use ticket vending machines inside subway stations, with a maximum reload of 500,000 won. On refunds, it says balances over 50,000 won must be processed at Tmoney Town near Seoul Station Exit 10, while smaller amounts can be refunded at convenience stores. That matters if you plan to load a large sum, so top up in small amounts.

VISITKOREA also describes alternatives. The WOWPASS is a foreigner-focused card priced at 5,000 won, sold at kiosks and requires a passport. The Seoul-specific Climate Card has a 3,000 won physical card, with passes listed from 5,000 won for one day and 20,000 won for seven days, covering Seoul buses and subways but not the Sinbundang Line or GTX. Compare these against how many days you will actually spend in Seoul.

Step 5: Plan how you will pay

LG U+ describes Korea as largely cashless: most establishments accept credit cards, and mobile payment apps such as Apple Pay and Alipay work in most stores. That is the carrier's summary, not a guarantee for every shop, so keep a backup payment method and a small amount of cash for places that do not take your card. Tell your bank you are traveling if your card issuer requires it, and keep banking apps for use on mobile data rather than public Wi-Fi.

Step 6: Complete the online entry steps

Korea uses an online arrival card. VISITKOREA says travelers can submit the e-Arrival card on the e-arrivalcard.go.kr website, on PC or mobile, up to three days before entering Korea, and that the card expires if you do not enter within 72 hours of submission. It lists exemptions for holders of a residence card, a valid K-ETA, or an e-Group visa. A Korea.net article adds that holders of valid K-ETA and travelers on group electronic visas are among those who do not need to submit it.

Separately, VISITKOREA states that visitors aged 17 or older go through fingerprint and facial recognition registration at immigration. Screenshot your e-Arrival confirmation so you can show it even if your signal is weak after landing.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Buying data before checking your phone. Confirm that your device is unlocked and supports the product you are buying with the seller.
  • Using public Wi-Fi for banking. The Stripes guide specifically warns against financial transactions on unsecured networks.
  • Loading too much onto a T-money card. Large balances need a trip to a specific refund location.
  • Submitting the arrival card too early. The window is three days before entry, and the submission lapses after 72 hours.
  • Relying on one payment method. Carry a backup card and some cash.

Rules change: confirm with the official body

This article is general information, not telecommunications, legal or financial advice. Data plans, card prices, refund rules and entry procedures can change. Check current details with your chosen carrier, with VISITKOREA, and with the Korea Immigration Service before you travel.
