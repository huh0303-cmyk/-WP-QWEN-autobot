The cheapest nightly rate rarely tells you which place is best for a month in Korea. What matters is the total price for your exact dates, the type of accommodation (which decides how it is licensed and what it includes), the cancellation terms, and whether your permitted length of stay actually covers the booking. This article walks through those checks in the order that saves the most trouble.

Start with whether your stay length is allowed

A booking can be perfect and still fail at the border. According to Medical Korea, citizens of 114 countries can enter visa-free for up to 30 days, although the duration varies by bilateral agreement and some nationalities receive 90 days. A one-month booking may therefore sit right at the edge of what your passport allows.

Before you pay a deposit, confirm three things with official sources for your nationality:

  • How many days you may stay without a visa.
  • Whether you need a K-ETA. The Korean government extended the temporary K-ETA exemption for visa-free nationals through December 31, 2026, according to VisitKorea, and travelers from exempt countries may still apply voluntarily. Check the official K-ETA website for the current country list.
  • Whether your check-out date leaves you a day or two of margin if a flight changes.

Price the whole month, not a sample night

Enter your real check-in and check-out dates and the correct number of guests, then record the final total shown at checkout. A rate displayed for a short example stay is not a quote for thirty nights. Write down what that total includes and what it does not.

  • Taxes and service fees: Is the total shown after all fees, or are some added at the last step?
  • Cleaning and utilities: Are cleaning, electricity, gas and internet included, or billed separately?
  • Deposit: How much is held, when is it returned, and in what form?
  • Payment currency: Is the price in won or your home currency, and who applies the exchange rate?

Take a screenshot of the quote with the date you checked it. If the listing changes later, you have a record of what was offered.

Compare accommodation types, not just prices

VisitKorea lists the main lodging types for visitors: hotels, motels, hanok stays, guesthouses, pensions and glamping. They differ in ways that matter over a month.

  • Hotels range from luxury properties to budget business hotels. VisitKorea notes that staying in a hotel for the amenities has become popular.
  • Motels are described by VisitKorea as Korea's most economical option, often in central locations.
  • Hanok stays are traditional houses that offer cultural immersion with modern amenities such as beds.
  • Guesthouses are budget-friendly, with dormitories, private rooms and female-only options. VisitKorea says many host social events, and that you should check noise level, whether the bathroom is private or shared, and parking availability before booking.
  • Pensions are a Korean style of lodging, from home-like to resort-style, often found outside the big cities.

For a month, ask yourself practical questions. Do you need a kitchen or a washing machine? Will you work from the room and need a desk? Is a party-oriented guesthouse realistic when you need quiet evenings? A slightly higher monthly price can be better value if it removes laundry fees and daily eating-out costs.

Check that the listing is a legal, registered lodging

Short-term rentals in Korea are regulated. The Korea Herald reports that the culture ministry eased rules for the "urban homestay" category for overseas tourists: local governments may now approve homes older than 30 years if they are judged safe under the Building Act, and hosts can meet the foreign-language requirement through effective communication, including translation apps. The same report says about 5,000 home-sharing businesses in residences under 230 square meters were registered in the prior year, and that Airbnb began requiring Korean hosts to submit business registration proof, which was expected to remove roughly 30,000 unlicensed listings.

Practical steps drawn from a registration-check guide by Koursea, which cites Government24 tourism-business information:

  1. Ask the host which accommodation category the property is registered under.
  2. Ask for the registration or business details, and do not treat the platform listing as proof.
  3. Confirm the address matches the listing.
  4. Get check-in and emergency procedures in writing.

Warning signs include a host who will not name the property category, an address hidden until after payment, a claim that registration is not needed for short stays, or a request to pay outside the platform.

Read the cancellation and change terms line by line

For a thirty-night booking, the cancellation policy often matters more than the discount. Look for the last date of free cancellation, the percentage charged afterwards, and whether the policy treats the whole month as one booking or nightly. Also check what happens if you leave early: some hosts refund unused nights, others do not.

Keep every message with the host or platform. If anything goes wrong, a written record of the listing, the payment terms and the conversation is the most useful evidence you can have.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Booking exactly 30 nights without confirming that your visa-free period covers it.
  • Comparing a nightly rate on one site with a monthly total on another.
  • Assuming a platform listing means the lodging is registered.
  • Skipping the bathroom, noise and parking questions that VisitKorea flags for guesthouses.
  • Paying outside the booking platform.
  • Forgetting to save screenshots of the price, inclusions and cancellation terms.

Rules change, so confirm with the official bodies

This article is general information for trip planning, not legal or immigration advice. Entry rules, K-ETA exemptions and lodging regulations can change. Confirm your permitted stay and K-ETA status with the official K-ETA site or the Korean embassy that covers your country, and confirm a property's registration with the host and your local district office if you are unsure.
