If you want cherry blossoms without the heaviest crowds, look at places where the bloom comes later or where the setting is quieter. The Korea Tourism Organization's VisitKorea guide to spring blooms names Gaesimsa Temple in Seosan, Samcheok Maengbang Village and Icheon's cornelian cherry village as less crowded alternatives to the biggest festivals, and the Korea Times highlights Seokchon Lake, the Gyeongui Line Forest Path and Namhae. Timing matters as much as location, because the bloom moves from south to north over several weeks.

How the bloom moves across the country

Korea.net, the government's official site, reported a forecast from the private forecaster K-Weather that blooms would arrive three to six days earlier than the 1991 to 2020 average. Its regional first-bloom dates were March 21 for Jeju, March 25 to 29 for the southern regions, March 30 to April 5 for central areas and April 2 for Seoul, with peak bloom about a week after the first bloom. That forecast covered one particular spring; the useful lesson is the order, with Jeju and the south first, then the centre, then later spots such as temples and higher ground.

Festivals follow the bloom. Korea.net reported that the Jinhae Gunhangje Festival was moved earlier, to begin on March 22 in that year, which it described as the earliest date in its history, and that the Yeouido festival was rescheduled to March 27 to 31. Festival dates are set each year, so check the organiser before booking.

Less crowded options named by official and news sources

  • Gaesimsa Temple, Seosan (Chungcheongnam-do): VisitKorea says double cherry blossoms and rare green cherry blossoms bloom here from late April to early May, about two weeks later than typical cherries, and it lists the temple among less crowded alternatives that extend the season into May. The site is more than 1,300 years old and holds nationally designated cultural treasures.
  • Samcheok Maengbang Village (Gangwon-do): VisitKorea describes a 60,000-square-metre field of canola flowers with a 2.5-kilometre cherry blossom road and the East Sea as a backdrop, with bloom in early to mid-April and a canola festival that ran from April 3 to 19 in 2026.
  • Democracy Park, Busan: double cherry blossoms in mid to late April on the slopes of Bosusan, with elevated paths and sea views, according to VisitKorea.
  • Namhae (South Gyeongsang): the Korea Times describes a 5-kilometre route with more than 1,100 trees arching over coastal roads, with canola flowers underneath, designated among the 100 most beautiful roads in Korea.
  • Seokchon Lake, Seoul: a 2.5-kilometre path with more than 1,000 trees near Jamsil Station, with views of Lotte World Tower and night illumination, per the Korea Times. Listed festival dates were April 3 to 11 in 2026.
  • Gyeongui Line Forest Path, Seoul: a 6.3-kilometre former railway corridor in Mapo and Yeonnam with cherry tunnels, cafes and bookstores nearby, according to the Korea Times.

Note that "less crowded" is the sources' description of relative crowds, not a measured crowd figure. Any site close to a major subway station can fill up on a clear weekend.

The best-known sites, for comparison

VisitKorea lists Jinhae Gunhang Village in Changwon, with about 360,000 cherry trees and a 1.5-kilometre tunnel along Yeojwacheon Stream, and Yeouido Cherry Blossom Road in Seoul, with about 1,800 trees along 1.7 kilometres by the Hangang River. VisitKorea notes that traffic is restricted on Yeouido during its festival, and advises using Yeouinaru or National Assembly stations. These are the places many other visitors choose, so they work best on weekday mornings or outside the festival week if you have the choice.

Plan a route around the bloom

  1. Start with the forecast. Check the Korea.net or a forecaster's update close to travel, since dates shift each year.
  2. Pick one region per trip. The south blooms first, the centre later and temples or highland sites after that.
  3. Check the festival dates and any traffic restrictions with the organiser.
  4. Pick a backup. Choose one spot with a later bloom, such as Gaesimsa, in case the main spot has finished.
  5. Plan how you will get there. VisitKorea gives subway stations for the Seoul sites and notes that Icheon is about an hour from Seoul.
  6. Save the 1330 Korea Travel Helpline, which VisitKorea lists with multilingual support.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Using last year's dates. Festival and bloom dates change each spring.
  • Mixing up flowers. Cornelian cherry (sansuyu) blossoms are yellow and bloom earlier than cherry blossoms; VisitKorea lists Icheon and Gurye as sansuyu villages.
  • Assuming a quiet place stays quiet. Weekends and festival days draw crowds anywhere.
  • Ignoring road closures. Roads such as Yeouido and Jeju's Jeonnong-ro close to traffic at peak bloom, according to VisitKorea.
  • Forgetting that the peak is short. Korea.net describes peak bloom as about a week after first bloom.

Dates and details change: confirm with the organisers

Festival dates, road closures, entry rules and bloom forecasts are set by local governments, festival organisers and forecasters, and they change each year and sometimes at short notice. Confirm current details with the festival organiser, the local tourist office or the Korea Tourism Organization before you travel. This article is general travel information.
