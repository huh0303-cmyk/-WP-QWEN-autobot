Korea's incentives for foreign direct investment come in three main forms: tax reductions or exemptions, cash grants, and support with land and rent. None of them is automatic. According to Invest KOREA, many are tied to the industry, the size of the investment and the location, so the first job for an investor is to find out which category a planned project falls into and then confirm the exact terms with the authorities.

Tax reductions that Invest KOREA lists

Invest KOREA's incentives page groups tax support under several headings. Foreign-invested companies that run registered businesses may qualify for reductions or exemptions on acquisition tax and property tax for acquired property. For imported capital goods used in eligible businesses, the page says customs duties, individual consumption tax and value-added tax are exempted under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act.

The Invest KOREA investment guide gives more detail on the capital goods benefit: for foreign-invested companies in designated sectors it describes a 100% reduction for five years, with an extension of two years allowed. The foreign investment zone overview similarly states tariff exemptions on capital goods for five years from the import date.

The incentives page also mentions credits for integrated investment, employment increases and small and medium-sized enterprise status, and relief in specific zones such as enterprise cities and special research zones. The page does not list rates, periods or thresholds for these items, which is a signal to ask for them rather than assume.

Support for foreign employees

Invest KOREA says income tax reductions are available for foreign engineers and that special taxation applies to foreign workers and regional office staff. The investment guide refers to a 50% income tax reduction for 10 years for foreign engineers and K-Tech Pass holders under updated eligibility criteria. Eligibility depends on qualifications and the type of work, so an employer should check each person's case with the National Tax Service before telling staff what to expect.

Local taxes and foreign investment zones

Investing inside a foreign investment zone can add local benefits. Invest KOREA's overview of the zones says local tax exemptions can extend up to 15 years in accordance with local government ordinances. It also says value-added tax exemptions are available for individual-type zones only. Local ordinances differ, so the municipality where you will locate is the body to ask.

The same overview describes rent support in zones: it gives the example that a manufacturing operation valued at USD 2.5 million with 200 or more full-time employees can receive a 100% rent reduction, while one with 70 or more workers can receive 75%.

Cash grants

Cash support is separate from tax relief. According to Invest KOREA, eligible items include purchase costs or rental fees for land or buildings, construction costs, purchase costs for capital goods and research equipment, installation costs for infrastructure, and employment and education and training subsidies. The incentives page says grant limits were raised, and the investment guide says the central and local government funding split for regions outside Seoul moved from 60:40 to 70:30. Because grant ceilings and temporary boosts change, always ask what limit applies at the time of your application.

For research and development, the incentives page explains that qualifying business-affiliated research institutes may register with the Korea Industrial Technology Association. The sequence it describes is prior consultation, online registration, review by the association, issuing of a certificate and follow-up management.

A practical order of questions

  1. Which industry category does the project fall under, and is it a designated sector?
  2. What investment amount and employment level will be committed?
  3. Will the site be inside a foreign investment zone, and of which type?
  4. Which taxes are reduced, for how long, and which office approves the reduction?
  5. Is a cash grant sought, and what are the application dates and limits?

How the ordinary tax system still applies

Incentives reduce a bill; they do not change who is taxed. Invest KOREA's taxation guide says foreign corporations pay corporate tax only on domestic-source income, while foreign-invested domestic corporations are taxed on worldwide income like Korean companies. It lists corporate income tax brackets that begin at 9% on income up to KRW 200 million. A foreign-invested company therefore needs ordinary tax planning as well as an incentive application.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Assuming an incentive applies because the investor is foreign. The pages describe conditions tied to industry, scale and location.
  • Planning cash flow around a grant before it is approved.
  • Mixing up zone-specific benefits: for example, the VAT exemption mentioned for foreign investment zones is limited to the individual type.
  • Ignoring local ordinances when counting on local tax exemptions.
  • Treating a reduction period as permanent. The pages give fixed periods.
  • Relying on an old brochure. The investment guide itself notes that figures were updated, so check the date of anything you read.

Rules change: confirm with the official bodies

This article is general information, not legal, tax or financial advice. Incentive rates, periods and eligibility are revised through tax law amendments and government notices. Confirm current terms with Invest KOREA/KOTRA, the National Tax Service and the local government concerned, and consult a licensed tax accountant or lawyer before making an investment decision.
