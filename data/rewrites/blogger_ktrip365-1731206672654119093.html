A 7-day autumn route through central and southern Korea can link five places that are documented as worth the detour: Jeonju Hanok Village, Naejangsan National Park, Suncheon Bay, the Gyeongju Historic Areas, and the UNESCO-listed village of Hahoe. The order below runs from the southwest to the southeast and then north, so each leg moves in one direction. Check the official foliage forecast and transport timetables before you book anything, because color timing and schedules change every year.

How to use this itinerary

This is a framework, not a timetable. The sources I could read describe the places themselves but not train or bus times, so those are left for you to confirm with the operators. The VisitKorea site, the official Korea Tourism Organization portal, lists a "2026 Fall Foliage Forecast" under its news section, dated 8 September 2026. Open that page first and move the days around if the colors peak earlier or later in one region than another.

Days 1 and 2: Jeonju Hanok Village

According to Wikipedia, Jeonju Hanok Village has more than 800 hanok (traditional Korean houses) and was designated an International Slow City in 2010. Within it, Gyeonggijeon is a hall built in 1410 that houses the portrait of Yi Seong-gye, founder of the Joseon dynasty, which is listed as a National Treasure. Other sights it names are the Jeondong Catholic Church (completed 1914), the Jeonju Hyanggyo Confucian school, and Pungnam Gate, the only surviving city gate.

  • Day 1: arrive, walk the village lanes, visit Gyeonggijeon and Pungnam Gate.
  • Day 2: visit Jeondong Catholic Church and the Hyanggyo, then sample local food.

The same article notes that traditional handicraft workshops have increasingly been replaced by souvenir shops, so ask at a workshop what is actually made there if craft is what you are after.

Day 3: Naejangsan National Park

Naejangsan National Park lies across North Jeolla Province and the Jeonnam-Gwangju area. Wikipedia says it covers 81.45 square kilometers, was designated South Korea's eighth national park on 17 November 1971, and takes its name from the 763-meter Naejangsan mountain, with 919 plant species and 1,880 animal species recorded. The page does not describe foliage, so the autumn-color reputation is something to confirm in the forecast and through the Korea National Park Service, which runs the park's official English page. Check trail conditions and any closures there before you go.

Day 4: Suncheon Bay

Wikipedia describes Suncheon Bay as a coastal wetland in South Jeolla Province made up of a stream, tidelands and reed fields. It reports a reed field of 230 hectares and a total wetland area of 35.50 square kilometers, and says it was the first Korean coastal wetland listed as a Ramsar wetland, on 20 January 2006. Notable migratory birds named include hooded cranes, Eurasian spoonbills, whooper swans and Chinese egrets. Which of these you see depends on the season, so check bird and visiting information locally.

Days 5 and 6: Gyeongju Historic Areas

UNESCO says Gyeongju was the capital of the Silla dynasty for nearly a thousand years (57 BCE to 935 CE), and that the Gyeongju Historic Areas were inscribed in 2000. It lists five component areas:

  1. The Mount Namsan belt, with temple ruins, stone statues and pagodas.
  2. The Wolseong belt, including Anapji Pond and the Cheomseongdae Observatory.
  3. The Tumuli Park belt of royal tombs.
  4. The Hwangnyongsa belt, site of the largest temple ever built in Korea.
  5. The Sanseong Fortress belt.

Spend one day on the Wolseong and tumuli areas, which are the most compact, and a second on Namsan, which UNESCO says covers 2,650 hectares. That is large enough that you should pick one or two trails rather than try to cover it all.

Day 7: Hahoe village near Andong

UNESCO inscribed Historic Villages of Korea: Hahoe and Yangdong in 2010. It describes two clan villages established in the 14th and 15th centuries as exceptional examples of Joseon-dynasty settlement, with the residences of head families, pavilions, academies, and commoner homes with mud walls and thatched roofs. The listing says the two villages are about 90 kilometers apart, so decide at the start which one you will visit. Both have been protected cultural heritage since 1984.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Fixing hotel dates before reading the foliage forecast.
  • Assuming every site is open every day. Confirm opening days and any seasonal closures.
  • Trying to see all of Namsan or all of Naejangsan in one visit.
  • Booking hanok stays without checking cancellation terms.
  • Relying on this page for transport times. Check with the rail and bus operators.

Rules change: confirm with the official body

Schedules, entry rules and fees change. Confirm current details with the Korea Tourism Organization (VisitKorea, 1330 Korea Travel Helpline), the Korea National Park Service, and the individual sites before you travel.
