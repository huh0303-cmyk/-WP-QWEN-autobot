If you hold a passport from a visa-exempt country, you do not currently need a K-ETA to enter Korea for a short visit. The Korean government has extended a temporary K-ETA exemption through December 31, 2026. You still have entry steps to complete, most importantly the online e-Arrival card, and what applies to you depends on your nationality, so check the official lists before you book.

What the K-ETA exemption actually covers

According to VISITKOREA, "nationals of countries or regions that are exempt from visa requirements may enter Korea without applying for a K-ETA until December 31, 2026." The K-ETA site's notice, dated December 23, 2025 and issued by the Ministry of Justice, describes the same extension: the exemption runs from January 1, 2026 to December 31, 2026 (KST).

Two limits are worth reading carefully. First, the exemption applies to citizens of countries or regions that are already exempt from visa requirements. It is not a general waiver for all nationalities. VISITKOREA says the list of eligible countries is maintained on the official K-ETA website. Second, it is temporary, with an end date. If your trip falls near or after that date, confirm whether it was extended again.

Why some exempt travelers still apply

The notice explains that exempt travelers may apply for a K-ETA voluntarily to receive specific benefits, including exemption from completing an arrival card on entry. A standard application fee applies to voluntary applications and the fees are non-refundable. People who already hold K-ETA approval can keep using it until it expires, without reapplying or seeking a refund.

Whether the voluntary route is worth the cost is a personal decision. If you only want to avoid filling in the arrival card, compare the K-ETA fee listed on the official site against the few minutes the online card takes.

The e-Arrival card for everyone else

VISITKOREA says the e-Arrival card system launched on February 24, 2025 and that inbound passengers are expected to use it. Key points from its announcement and from VISITKOREA's immigration page:

  • Submit through the e-Arrival card website, e-arrivalcard.go.kr, on a PC or mobile device.
  • You can submit up to three days before arrival (the day of arrival, one day before or two days before). If you do not enter within 72 hours, the submission expires.
  • A family member or another person completes the form for travelers under 14.
  • One person can submit for multiple people, including adults, at the same time.

Korea.net reports that holders of a valid K-ETA, people who have completed foreign resident registration, travelers on group electronic visas and airline crew do not need to submit the entry declaration. It also says the requirement applies to foreign visitors staying up to 90 days.

A simple pre-trip checklist

  1. Check your nationality. Look up whether your passport country is on the visa-exempt list. If it is not, you need a visa or other authorization, which is a different process from K-ETA.
  2. Check the exemption dates. Confirm the K-ETA notice on the official site covers your travel date.
  3. Decide on voluntary K-ETA. Weigh the fee and the benefit of skipping the arrival card.
  4. Submit the e-Arrival card. Do this within the three-day window if you are not exempt, and keep the confirmation on your phone.
  5. Prepare for biometrics. VISITKOREA says foreign visitors aged 17 or older must go through fingerprint and facial recognition registration, using both index fingers, with no sunglasses or hats for the photo.
  6. Know your customs limits. VISITKOREA lists duty-free allowances per person including USD 800 in total purchases, alcohol up to USD 400 (2 bottles, 2 liters), 200 cigarettes and 100 ml of perfume. Travelers under 19 cannot claim the alcohol or cigarette exemption.

Passport validity and proof of funds

Many guides mention minimum passport validity and proof of funds. The official pages used for this article do not state a specific passport validity rule or a proof-of-funds requirement for visa-free entry, so this article does not give one. Airlines and immigration officers can apply their own checks, so ask your airline and the Korean embassy or consulate in your country what documents they expect. Carry your onward ticket and accommodation details as a sensible habit, but treat that as precaution rather than a stated rule.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Assuming the exemption is permanent. It has an end date, December 31, 2026.
  • Assuming every nationality is exempt. Only visa-exempt countries or regions qualify.
  • Submitting the arrival card too early. The window opens three days before arrival and the submission lapses after 72 hours.
  • Paying twice. A voluntary K-ETA fee is non-refundable, so confirm you actually want it first.
  • Using unofficial sites. Use the official K-ETA and e-Arrival card addresses and be wary of third-party services that add fees.

Rules change: confirm with the official body

This is general information, not legal or immigration advice. Entry rules, exemption periods and fees change. Confirm current requirements with the K-ETA site, the Korea Immigration Service, or the Korean embassy or consulate for your country. If your situation is complicated, ask a qualified immigration professional.
