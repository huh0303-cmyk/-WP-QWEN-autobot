If you are applying for the Global Korea Scholarship for undergraduates (GKS-U) this cycle, the embassy track window has already closed, but the university track is still open at many schools. According to the 2027 GKS-U application guidelines, online applications for the embassy track ran from 15 September 2026 (11:00 KST) to 30 September 2026 (18:00 KST), while the university track runs from September to November 2026 and the exact dates vary by institution. If you missed the embassy deadline, your next step is to check which participating universities still accept direct applications and what each one asks for.

Where the two tracks differ

The guidelines describe two ways in. In the embassy track you apply online through the Study in Korea website, choose up to three universities (at least one must be a Type B university) and upload scanned documents. In the university track you apply directly to one university and one department, and you follow that university's own document rules.

The guidelines also say you cannot apply to several tracks or programmes at the same time, and that an embassy-track applicant who passed the first round cannot apply to the university track. Pick one track and one route before you start collecting papers.

Check eligibility before spending time on documents

The guidelines list these conditions for the bachelor's programme:

  • You must be under 25, born after 1 March 2002.
  • You must hold the citizenship of an invited country, and your parents must hold non-Korean citizenship. Dual citizenship with Korea disqualifies an applicant.
  • You must have graduated, or be expected to graduate, from high school or an associate degree programme, and you cannot already hold a bachelor's degree.
  • You must meet one academic condition: 80 or above on a 100-point scale, a place in the top 20% of your class, or a minimum CGPA of 2.64/4.0, 2.80/4.3, 2.91/4.5 or 3.23/5.0.

The same document lists automatic disqualifications, including graduating from a Korean high school, receiving a Korean government scholarship for a degree programme before, and submitting false documents. Read that list in full on the official page rather than relying on a summary.

Prepare the paperwork in this order

  1. The forms. The guidelines name an application form, personal statement, study plan, recommendation letter, applicant agreement, medical assessment and a consent form for personal information.
  2. Citizenship proof for you and your parents, with proof of the family relationship.
  3. School records: your graduation certificate (or certificate of expected graduation) and transcript.
  4. Authentication. The guidelines say documents need an apostille or consular confirmation. They state that simple photocopies of apostilled documents and notarised copies without government authentication are not accepted.
  5. Optional evidence: a TOPIK score report or English test score, and up to five awards or certificates.

Apostille and consular confirmation take time. Start these first, because the other items depend only on you.

How selection works and what the scores reward

For the embassy track the guidelines describe three rounds: embassy screening (results by 16 October), NIIED review (results in late November) and university review (results by 23 December). The university track has two rounds, with a university screening in November and an NIIED round with results by mid-December. The final announcement is expected on 7 January 2027.

The competency review considers academic performance, language proficiency, academic activities, contributions to society and international exchange, and future potential. For language, the guidelines convert TOPIK levels to a share of the total score, from 100% for level 5 and above down to 60% for level 1 and 50% for no official score. A TOPIK level 3 or higher also earns a small bonus. Those figures are scoring rules for this cycle, so read the current guideline before relying on them.

What the award covers and the Korean language year

The guidelines state the bachelor's programme runs 5 to 7 years: one year of Korean language programme plus four to six years of degree study. Scholars with TOPIK level 5 or 6 can be exempt from the language programme. The guidelines say that if you hold only provisional certificates, you may not receive a certificate of admission or be able to obtain a study visa, so confirm your certificate requirements early.

Study in Korea's scholarship page lists GKS benefits as airfare, Korean language training fees, tuition and monthly allowances. Its page also lists other options such as university scholarships (30 to 100 percent of tuition depending on merit) and foundation programmes. If the GKS does not work out, ask each university what it offers.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Waiting for the last day. The embassy window was about two weeks long, and university deadlines differ.
  • Applying twice. Applying to more than one track or department can lead to disqualification.
  • Submitting unauthenticated copies. Notarised copies alone are not accepted.
  • Leaving the graduation date off a certificate. The guidelines say certificates without a specified graduation date are not accepted.
  • Not checking the withdrawal rules. The guidelines say a renounced scholarship cannot be reversed, and that withdrawing within the first three months after enrolment means returning the full amount.

Rules change: confirm with the official body

This article is general information, not legal, immigration or financial advice. Eligibility, dates, benefits and scoring are published each cycle by the National Institute for International Education (NIIED), embassies and universities, and they can change. Confirm everything on the Study in Korea portal, with the Korean embassy in your country, and with the university you are applying to. If you are selected, ask the embassy and HiKorea about your visa requirements.
