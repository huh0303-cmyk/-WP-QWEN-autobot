Early autumn is a good time to plan a Korea itinerary around the weather and the leaf-color calendar rather than around a fixed list of sights. September is still warm and wet, October turns cooler and drier, and the colors reach different mountains on different dates. This checklist helps you pack for that shift, time your stops, and keep transport simple.

What the weather data says about September and October

Climate-Data.org gives Seoul an average temperature of 20.3°C in September, with about 131 mm of rain over roughly six rainy days and humidity near 75%. For October it lists an average of 13.6°C, around 55 mm of rain over four rainy days and humidity near 70%. The site describes the pattern as rainy summers and very dry winters, with September still carrying summer moisture and October marking the shift to the drier autumn season.

These are monthly averages for Seoul, not a forecast, and other regions differ. The gap between the two months is the useful part: a trip that spans late September and October can need both a rain layer and a warm layer. VISITKOREA's travel basics page links a weekly weather forecast, so check it in the days before you leave.

Pack in layers, not in extremes

Because averages swing by about seven degrees between the two months, plan clothing as layers you can add or remove:

  • A light top layer for warm, humid afternoons.
  • A sweater or jacket for mornings, evenings and higher ground.
  • A compact umbrella or rain jacket, especially in September when rainfall averages are higher.
  • Comfortable walking shoes that can handle wet pavement and uneven trails.
  • A small day bag so you can carry layers when the temperature rises.

Mountains are usually cooler than city centers, so a warm layer is sensible if hiking is part of your plan. That is a general travel point rather than something stated in the sources below.

Use the foliage forecast to order your stops

According to the Korea Tourism Organization's 2026 fall foliage forecast on VISITKOREA, first color is expected on Seoraksan on October 3, Bukhansan on October 23, Jirisan on October 28 and Hallasan on October 31. Peak color is forecast for Seoraksan on October 25, Bukhansan on November 4, Jirisan on November 5, Naejangsan on November 11 and Hallasan on November 13.

The practical lesson is that color moves from north and high ground toward the south and lower ground. If you are in Korea in mid-October, Seoraksan is nearer its peak than Hallasan. If you arrive in early November, Bukhansan near Seoul is closer to its best. VISITKOREA adds that actual dates may change depending on the weather, so treat them as a planning guide and look for updates as your trip approaches.

For city-based days, the forecast page names Gyeongbokgung Palace, Seoul Forest, Olympic Park and N Seoul Tower in Seoul, plus Nami Island and The Garden of Morning Calm nearby. For longer trips it lists the Ginkgo Forest in Hongcheon, Jangtaesan Mountain in Daejeon, Baegyangsa Temple in Jangseong and Bulguksa Temple in Gyeongju.

Match each stop to its season window

A separate VISITKOREA article on fall foliage destinations includes details that affect timing. It says the Ginkgo Grove in Hongcheon, with approximately 2,000 ginkgo trees, is open to the public in October only. It lists Hwadam Botanic Garden in Gwangju, Gyeonggi-do, as best from October to early November and about 1.5 hours from Seoul by public transit. It suggests visiting Gwanbangjerim Forest in Damyang early in the morning for fog and color, and says the Secret Garden in Inje is a restricted military area with designated photo zones.

Write down each stop's opening rules and best-visit window before you book transport, because a single-month opening such as the Hongcheon grove can decide your whole date range.

Keep local transport simple

VISITKOREA's transportation overview groups travel into getting to Korea, intercity travel by trains, express or intercity buses and domestic flights, and urban travel by city buses, subways, taxis and bicycles. For city days, a transit card is the easiest option. VISITKOREA says the T-money card sells for 3,000-5,000 won at convenience stores and at Seoul subway station information centers, can be reloaded at station vending machines up to 500,000 won, and works across public transportation and at affiliated stores. Its Seoul-only Climate Card is listed at 3,000 won for the physical card, with a one-day pass at 5,000 won and a seven-day pass at 20,000 won, excluding the Sinbundang Line and GTX.

If your itinerary leaves Seoul for mountains or temples, build in extra time. Intercity legs and local connections add up, and several of the forecast's destinations are outside the capital.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Packing only for summer. The October average in Seoul is about seven degrees lower than September's.
  • Treating foliage dates as fixed. VISITKOREA says dates may change with the weather.
  • Missing a limited opening. Check opening periods, such as the Hongcheon ginkgo grove's October-only access.
  • Overloading a day. Count transfers, not just distances.
  • Loading too much onto a transit card. Refund steps differ by balance.

Rules change: confirm with the official body

This is general travel information, not legal advice. Entry requirements, opening hours, foliage forecasts and fares change, and weather varies year to year. Confirm current entry rules with the Korean embassy or the Korea Immigration Service, and check attractions' own pages and VISITKOREA for the latest details. The 1330 Travel Helpline, which VISITKOREA lists, is another place to ask.
