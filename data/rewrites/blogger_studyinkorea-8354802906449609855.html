The quickest way to find a student community from your own country is the "International Student Association in Korea" page on the Study in Korea portal. It lets you filter associations by country, read their posts and follow their social media. If your country has no listed group, university buddy programmes, city support centres and language-exchange apps offer other ways to meet people. This article shows how to use each, based on pages that were open when this was written.

What the Study in Korea directory offers

Study in Korea is the official portal operated by the Korean government's National Institute for International Education. Its association page says the directory "helps you connect with fellow students" and lets you join student association activities and access study abroad information. At the time of reading, it reported 236 search results.

The page has two tabs, Posts and SNS, and a country selector. The list of countries is long and includes Vietnam, Kazakhstan, Indonesia, Malaysia, India and the Philippines among many others. Choose your country, then read recent posts to see whether the group is active. The page does not publish a membership process for each association, so what you do next depends on the group: some posts link to social media accounts or event notices.

What the posts show about association activities

The most recent posts on the page give a realistic picture of what these groups do. They include a festival and a sports-related event organised by an Indian student group, a memorandum signing and a multicultural football tournament posted by a group from the Democratic Republic of the Congo, a photowalk, a talk to connect Malaysians and a course and career talk posted by Malaysian groups, and an orientation for new international students posted under China. Posts like these tell you what to expect: social events, career talks and orientation sessions, not only chat groups.

Notice also that post titles appear in more than one language: most of the recent ones are in English, while the orientation notice is in Korean. If a group's posts are mostly in a language you do not read, use the SNS tab or ask a classmate from the same country to explain what the group does. This is worth doing before your first event, because a group's activity level and language tell you more than its name does.

Check the date on the latest post before you rely on a group. A page with posts from this term is a better sign than one with nothing recent, and the portal lets you read posts by country before you contact anyone.

Other ways to connect on and around campus

A Korea JoongAng Daily article on support for international students describes several routes beyond country groups:

  • University counselling centres. Hanyang University's Erica campus runs a counselling centre in Korean, English and Chinese, and Kyung Hee University's Global Education Support Team connects students with English- and Chinese-speaking counsellors.
  • Campus buddy programmes. The article lists peer mentoring at various universities.
  • Municipal support. The Seoul Foreign Resident Center offers up to five free one-on-one counselling sessions in Korean, English and Chinese, and the Danuri Call Center runs 24 hours a day in 15 languages at 1577-1366.
  • Apps. The article mentions Karrot, which has local meetup groups for language exchange, HelloTalk and Maum.

For career and study support, the Korea Times reports that the Seoul Global Student Center opened in May near Sinchon Station on Subway Line 2. According to the article, it offers résumé and cover letter workshops, business Korean classes, TOPIK preparation, academic Korean instruction, work experience programmes and a mentorship day, and it works with 32 four-year universities and 10 junior colleges in Seoul. Check its own announcements for registration details.

A simple plan for your first month

  1. Open the association page, select your country and read the last three posts.
  2. Follow the group's social media link if one is shown and watch for an event notice.
  3. Ask your university's international office whether there is a campus club for your country or a buddy programme.
  4. Join one event before deciding whether the group fits you.
  5. If you want to practise Korean as well, add a language-exchange meetup, and keep your study time in your plan.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Treating the directory as a membership service. It is a place to find groups, and each group sets its own way to join.
  • Trusting an inactive group. Check the date of the latest post.
  • Sharing documents in a group chat. Do not post passport or alien registration details in open chats; confirm questions with your university or immigration office.
  • Following visa or work advice from peers. Rules differ by status, so confirm them with your university's international office or the immigration authorities.
  • Waiting until you feel stuck. The counselling and support services in the JoongAng article exist for early use as well as for crises.

Rules and services change: confirm with the official bodies

Group listings, support centre hours and programmes change often. This article is general information, not legal, immigration or medical advice. For anything that affects your visa, health or finances, confirm the current position with your university's international office, the immigration authorities and the service providers named above.
