Seoul Foreign School (SFS) admits students from Pre-K through Grade 12, but only if the family meets one of three passport or residency categories and the child shows they can succeed academically in English. Applications then run through five steps, from an inquiry form to an emailed decision, and the school publishes its fees in Korean won plus a US dollar component. This article summarises what the school's own pages say so that you can plan the paperwork and the budget.

Who is eligible: the three categories

According to the SFS admissions policy page, the school serves ages 2 to 18 and above, from Pre-K to Grade 12. A student must satisfy one of three conditions:

  • Category A: at least one parent holds a non-Korean passport.
  • Category B: both parents hold Korean passports, and the family can show either more than 1,095 days outside Korea or six semesters of schooling abroad. The policy notes that Category B admissions are capped to comply with government-mandated enrolment limits.
  • Category C: one or both parents are naturalised Korean citizens and the child cannot continue in the local Korean school system because of language or cultural adjustment issues. The page says this requires school deliberation and approval from the local school principal.

Beyond citizenship, the policy states that previous academic history or admissions testing must indicate that the student can reach outcomes comparable to classmates, and that English proficiency must meet the standards in the school's language policy for English as an Additional Language (EAL) students. Parents must also agree to support the school's mission and rules. Students who need learning support are evaluated to see whether the school can meet those needs.

The five application steps

The SFS application page lists these steps:

  1. Review the eligibility requirements and check your passport and residency documentation.
  2. Submit the inquiry form to check seat availability.
  3. Submit the online application, create an account and pay the processing fee.
  4. Provide the required documents, including academic records, recommendations and assessments.
  5. Receive the admissions decision by email.

Because step 2 is about seat availability, do not wait until you have every document before you ask. Grade levels can be full while you are still gathering papers.

Documents to prepare

The page says all applicants need the online application form, a birth certificate with an English translation or notarisation (or a certificate of family relations), copies of the passports of the applicant, mother and father, and copies of residence cards where applicable. Additional requirements vary depending on parental passport status and the student's citizenship circumstances.

For students aged 6 and over, the school asks for report cards from the current year and the previous two academic years, translated or notarised. Teacher recommendations and counsellor references are required for ages 11 and above. If your child's records are in Vietnamese, Korean or another language, plan time for certified translation, since this is likely to take longer than filling in the form.

The sources do not describe a separate grade-placement rule, so ask the admissions office how the school decides between two possible grade levels for a child whose birthday falls near a cut-off date, and how EAL support is arranged after enrolment.

Costs and timeline

The SFS application page states that applications for the 2026-2027 school year are currently accepted, with the first day of school on August 10, 2026, and that applications for 2027-2028 become available in early November 2026. The application fee is 450,000 won per applicant and is non-refundable.

The tuition page lists annual tuition as a won amount plus a dollar amount. A few examples for 2026-2027:

LevelTuition listed
Kindergarten to Grade 5 (full day)27,285,000 won plus 13,500 US dollars
Middle school (Grades 6-8)27,930,000 won plus 14,625 US dollars
High school (Grades 9-12)33,370,000 won plus 14,955 US dollars

For new students, the same page lists one-time non-refundable fees of 650,000 won for registration and 5,500,000 won for entrance, in addition to the 450,000 won application fee. Recurring extras include an annual round-trip bus fee of 4,200,000 won. The page points to a downloadable fee schedule for payment terms and conditions, which you should read before accepting a place.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Assuming a foreign passport is enough. One parent's non-Korean passport satisfies Category A, but the school also looks at academic history and English proficiency.
  • Counting only the won figure. The tuition lines combine won and dollars, so add both components and the one-time fees.
  • Leaving translations until last. Records for the previous two academic years must be translated or notarised.
  • Missing the application window. Check the dates for the intake you want, since the next cycle is listed as opening in early November.
  • Using this page for other schools. Every international school sets its own rules; compare at least two before deciding.

Rules change: confirm with the school

Fees, deadlines, categories and required documents are set by the school and can change each year. This article summarises what the school's pages said when they were read and is general information, not legal or immigration advice. Confirm every figure and requirement with the SFS admissions office (the application page gives admissions@seoulforeign.org) and check visa questions with the immigration authorities.
