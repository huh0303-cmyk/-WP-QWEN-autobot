A foreign company choosing a site in Korea should first decide between an ordinary industrial complex and a foreign investment zone, because the rent, lease length and eligibility rules differ. According to Invest KOREA, Korea designates four types of industrial complex, and foreign-invested companies may also qualify for reduced-rent land in foreign investment zones if they meet minimum investment and ownership conditions.

The four types of industrial complex

Invest KOREA's industrial sites guide lists these categories, each with a different purpose:

  • National industrial complexes: designated to promote key national industries and high-tech industries, or to develop underdeveloped areas.
  • General industrial complexes: designated to spread industry more evenly and revitalise regional economies.
  • Urban high-tech industrial complexes: located in urban areas for knowledge, cultural, telecommunications and other high-tech businesses.
  • Agricultural and industrial complexes: designated to bring industry to rural areas and raise incomes of farmers and fishermen.

The guide adds that local government heads apply for designation, while the designating authority depends on the type. National complexes are designated by central government bodies. In practice, this means a complex's stated purpose tells you which industries the authorities want there, and that is a useful first filter for whether your business fits.

Foreign investment zones: three formats

Invest KOREA describes three kinds of foreign investment zone:

  1. Complex-type: inside an existing industrial complex, aimed at smaller foreign investors. Invest KOREA's overview says there are 30 of these.
  2. Individual-type: for large investors who choose their own location. The overview lists 76 of these.
  3. Service-type: for categories such as research, finance, knowledge services and tourism. The overview lists 3.

The industrial sites guide gives different thresholds for each format. For individual-type zones it states investment thresholds that vary by industry, in a range from USD 2 million to USD 30 million. The Invest KOREA overview page quotes higher minimums for manufacturing, tourism and logistics. Because these two pages do not read identically, confirm the current figure for your sector directly with Invest KOREA rather than relying on any single number.

Eligibility conditions for foreign-invested companies

For complex-type zones, Invest KOREA's pages name conditions that appear repeatedly:

  • Registration as a foreign-invested company under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act.
  • Foreign ownership of at least 30%, with 50% for logistics operations.
  • New foreign direct investment made after the contract is signed.
  • A business in a sector the zone accepts, such as businesses with advanced technologies.

The sector point deserves attention. Invest KOREA's complex-type page lists eligible sectors, including businesses holding technologies for new growth-engine industries and categories with advanced technologies. A general trading office or retail operation is unlikely to match, so check the sector list before spending time on a site visit.

Rent and lease terms

According to the industrial sites guide, complex-type foreign investment zones are leased long-term, for 50 years with renewal every 10 years. Annual rent is calculated as at least 1% of the acquisition value, compared with about 5% for typical public land, and reductions of 75-100% may apply to qualifying investments. Invest KOREA's complex-type page gives examples: manufacturers investing a set minimum may receive 75% or 100% reductions depending on employment levels, and technology companies investing at least USD 1 million may receive a full exemption.

The same page notes a rental security deposit of at least 5% of acquisition value, payable in cash or through guarantee insurance, and a higher rent tier (at least 5%) for companies that relocate after failing to meet their investment commitments. Read that last point as a warning: the reduced rent is tied to promises on investment and employment.

The guide also says KICOX, the Korea Industrial Complex Corporation, manages national industrial complexes and operates most complex-type foreign investment zones. It handles occupancy contracts, rent collection and zone administration, so it is the practical contact for lease questions.

Other support worth asking about

Invest KOREA's "Other Support" page describes reduced-rent leasing of state-owned property to foreign investors, with leases of up to 50 years and reductions between 50% and 100% depending on the criteria. It also says the government designates a project manager through KOTRA for foreign investors, who helps with data, interviews and administrative support, including housing and school enrollment for executives' families. Ask early whether a project manager can be assigned to your case, because that person can answer site questions that general websites cannot.

A selection checklist

  1. Write down your industry and check which complex type is intended for it.
  2. Confirm that your foreign ownership percentage meets the zone's minimum.
  3. Ask for the exact investment and employment thresholds that determine your rent reduction.
  4. Ask what happens to the rent if you fall short of the commitments.
  5. Check the deposit amount and whether guarantee insurance is accepted.
  6. Ask who administers the zone and which office signs the lease.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Treating all industrial complexes as equivalent. Each type has a stated purpose and a different designating authority.
  • Quoting a rent figure without checking whether the reduction depends on your investment and hiring.
  • Assuming a minimum investment figure from one document applies to your sector without confirming it.
  • Signing before confirming that the investment will be recognised as new foreign direct investment.
  • Ignoring the consequences of missing the stated commitments.

Rules change: confirm with the official bodies

This article is general information, not legal or financial advice. Zone lists, thresholds, rent rates and reduction rules are revised over time and vary by site. Confirm current terms with Invest KOREA/KOTRA and KICOX, and consider a licensed legal or tax adviser before committing to a lease.
