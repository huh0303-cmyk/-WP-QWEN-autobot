Before you ask a Korean university or scholarship office about language conditions, sort your records into formal test results and everything else, and note the date each one was issued. A TOPIK result is valid for two years after the results are announced, according to Wikipedia's summary of the exam, and the GKS undergraduate guidelines accept only TOPIK certificates from a stated range of exam sittings. A course attendance certificate or a self-study log is a different kind of evidence and should not be presented as a test score.

List every record you hold, exactly as issued

Make one table with these columns: name of the test or course, issuing organisation, date taken, date issued, result, and whether you have the original or only a copy. Copy names and scores exactly as printed. Do not convert or round a score yourself.

Group the entries into three kinds:

  • Formal test results, such as a TOPIK score report. TOPIK I covers levels 1 and 2 (200 points) and TOPIK II covers levels 3 to 6 (300 points). The cut-offs listed by Wikipedia are 80 and 140 points on TOPIK I and 120, 150, 190 and 230 points on TOPIK II.
  • Course records, such as an attendance or completion certificate from a language school or an online programme. These show participation. They do not state a TOPIK level unless the document says so.
  • Personal notes, such as a study log or app progress screenshot. Keep them for yourself, not for the application file.

Check how long each result stays valid

Wikipedia states that TOPIK results are valid for two years after the announcement. Write the announcement date next to each score and count forward two years. If a score will expire before your application deadline or before you enrol, you may need a new result, so ask the receiving office how it treats scores close to expiry.

The 2027 GKS-U guidelines add a practical limit on which TOPIK certificates they accept: paper-based certificates from the 96th to the 109th exam and internet-based certificates from the 4th to the 16th exam. The same guidelines list "TOPIK certificates older than specified exam ranges" among documents not accepted. Check the exam number printed on your report against the range in the guidelines for the cycle you are entering.

Match each requirement to your evidence

Open the current admissions or scholarship page and copy the language wording into a second column of your table. Typical wording to look for is a minimum level, a named test, an alternative English-language test for an English-taught programme, or an exemption. The GKS-U guidelines show how much this varies: TOPIK is optional at application, but a score affects the evaluation, and TOPIK level 5 or 6 can mean exemption from the one-year Korean language programme.

For the guidelines' scoring, TOPIK levels convert to a share of the total score, from 100% for level 5 and above to 60% for level 1, and 50% for no official score. A TOPIK level 3 or higher also earns a small bonus. These are rules for one cycle of one scholarship, not a general rule, so do not assume another programme scores language the same way.

Know what a provisional or missing result means

The GKS-U guidelines warn that with provisional certificates alone, you may not receive a certificate of admission at a Korean university and may not be able to obtain a study visa. If you are waiting for a result, record the exam date, the expected announcement date and the document you will receive. Ask the office whether it accepts a receipt or registration confirmation while you wait, and do not assume it does.

If you have no formal score yet, say so plainly in your inquiry and ask what the office accepts instead. The scoring rules above show that "no official score" is a recognised category in that scholarship, not a reason to stay silent.

Write the inquiry so it can be answered

  1. State the programme and intake you are applying for.
  2. List your formal results with issuing body, level or score and announcement date.
  3. List any course certificates separately and describe them as course records.
  4. Quote the requirement you are asking about and the page where you found it.
  5. Ask one clear question, such as "Does this result meet the language condition for this programme?"
  6. Keep the reply, with the date and the name of the person who sent it.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Calling a course certificate a test result. The two answer different questions.
  • Forgetting the validity window. A score older than two years after announcement may no longer count.
  • Submitting a copy when an original is asked for. The GKS-U guidelines ask for the original TOPIK score report.
  • Using an old requirement page. Requirements are set for each intake.
  • Relying on a verbal answer. Get it in writing.

Rules change: confirm with the official body

This article is general information, not legal or immigration advice, and it does not promise any admission or scholarship result. Language requirements, accepted tests and score validity are set by each university, scholarship body and the test organiser, and they change between intakes. Confirm them on the official TOPIK site (topik.go.kr), the Study in Korea portal and the admissions or scholarship office you are applying to. For visa questions, check with the Korean embassy or HiKorea.
