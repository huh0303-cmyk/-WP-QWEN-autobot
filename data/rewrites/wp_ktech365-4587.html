Samsung's HBM4 is the company's newest high bandwidth memory for AI accelerators, and its position in the market depends on two things: the specifications it has published and how many chips customers actually buy. Samsung says it began shipping HBM4 in February 2026 with a sustained speed of 11.7 Gbps and up to 3.3 TB/s of bandwidth per stack. Industry reports, however, say rival SK Hynix holds the larger share of Nvidia's HBM4 orders. This article separates what Samsung states from what outside reports claim.

What HBM is, in practical terms

High bandwidth memory is stacked DRAM placed close to an AI processor to move data quickly. Samsung's HBM4 announcement describes 12-layer stacking with capacities of 24 GB to 36 GB, and says 16-layer stacking, reaching up to 48 GB, is planned for the future. It also says the interface pin count doubles from 1,024 to 2,048 compared with the previous generation. A wider interface is one of the reasons bandwidth rises so sharply.

The specifications Samsung published

According to Samsung's global newsroom, dated February 12, 2026, HBM4 delivers a consistent transfer speed of 11.7 Gbps, which Samsung says is 46% above the industry standard of 8 Gbps, and can reach 13 Gbps at maximum. The company compares this with the 9.6 Gbps maximum of HBM3E, a 1.22x increase. Total memory bandwidth is given as 3.3 TB/s per stack, which Samsung describes as a 2.7x improvement over HBM3E.

Samsung also lists efficiency gains versus HBM3E: 40% better power efficiency, 10% enhanced thermal resistance and 30% improved heat dissipation. These are manufacturer figures, measured under conditions the company defines, so independent testing by customers is what ultimately matters.

How it is built

The newsroom says the product combines Samsung's 1c DRAM, described as a sixth-generation 10nm-class process, with a 4nm logic base die. Samsung quotes an executive saying the company adopted the most advanced nodes rather than relying on proven existing designs. That is a technology-first choice. The other path, described by Asia Business Daily, is SK Hynix's approach of prioritizing volume and stability, using its existing 1b DRAM process with a more mature 12nm base die from TSMC.

The trade-off is the center of the competition. Asia Business Daily writes that Samsung's advanced 1c process carries higher technical difficulty and cost, with lower initial maturity that could affect early yields, and that the open question is whether each supplier can achieve both high performance and mass production stability.

Timing and customers

KED Global reported that Samsung planned to begin HBM4 mass production in the third week of February 2026, shortly after the Lunar New Year holiday, for Nvidia's Vera Rubin architecture. It added that Samsung was in discussions with Broadcom and Google about customized versions, and that the move was part of an effort to regain leadership in AI-focused DRAM after earlier setbacks with previous-generation HBM. Samsung's own newsroom says mass production was underway with shipments to customers.

Samsung's newsroom also gives a roadmap: HBM4E sampling is expected in the second half of 2026, custom HBM samples in 2027, and it projects HBM sales to grow threefold from 2025 to 2026. Roadmaps like these are plans, not delivered products.

Market share: what is reported and what is not

Asia Business Daily, citing industry sources, said SK Hynix had been allocated more than 50% of HBM4 volume while Samsung held about 20%, and that SK Hynix was expected to start supply in March 2026 after revising specifications to meet Nvidia's requirements. KED Global similarly reported that SK Hynix held the larger share of Nvidia's HBM4 orders and that Samsung was planning a 70% capacity increase. These are reports attributed to industry sources rather than company disclosures, and allocations can change as qualification progresses.

For readers outside the industry, the main point is that two supplier strategies are being tested at once. One favors the newest process nodes for higher performance and efficiency, and the other favors a proven process for steadier supply. Which one wins customers depends on measured results from the buyers, such as qualification outcomes and delivered volumes, and those are not fully public. Follow company filings and customer comments for confirmation over time.

How to read HBM news

  1. Identify the source. Is it a company release, a customer statement or an anonymous industry report?
  2. Separate specs from shipments. A speed or bandwidth figure does not show volume.
  3. Check the generation. HBM3E, HBM4 and HBM4E are different products.
  4. Note the date. Production plans and allocations shift quickly.
  5. Look for the customer. A named customer and a stated shipment are stronger evidence than a plan.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Comparing percentages without a baseline. Samsung's 46% figure is relative to an 8 Gbps standard it cites.
  • Assuming first to ship means largest supplier. The reports above describe different volume allocations.
  • Treating sample timelines as sales. Sampling is a testing stage.
  • Quoting a single stack's bandwidth as system performance. Total performance depends on the accelerator design.

Figures change: confirm with the official body

This is general information, not investment advice. Specifications, volumes and customer allocations are revised often. Check Samsung's newsroom and regulatory filings for company statements, and use the Ministry of Science and ICT or similar official bodies for policy information.
