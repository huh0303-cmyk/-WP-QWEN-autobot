Planning treatment in Korea comes down to four decisions made in a sensible order: confirm the hospital or clinic and its consultation terms, sort out your entry status or medical visa, arrange how you will pay and what insurance you hold, and know where to turn if something goes wrong. According to VisitKorea, foreign patients who are not residents of Korea are not eligible for Korea's national insurance coverage, so payment planning needs to start early.

Step 1: Consultation before you book travel

Medical Korea, the information portal for international patients, lists services that cover visa guidance, information on medical institutions, reservation assistance and dispute consultation. It operates information centers where staff speak several languages. The center at Seoul Station (B2, Seoul Station Airport Railroad) is open 9:00 to 18:00 daily with English, Chinese, Japanese and Korean service, and the center at Incheon International Airport Terminal 1 (Gate 7, 1F) is open 9:00 to 21:00 daily, with Russian service also listed. Check the portal for current contact details before you call.

When you contact a clinic, ask in writing for:

  • The proposed procedure, the doctor in charge and the planned length of treatment and recovery.
  • An itemized estimate, with what is and is not included.
  • Interpreter availability during consultation and treatment.
  • Aftercare arrangements once you return home.
  • The document the clinic can issue to support a visa application, if you need one.

Step 2: Entry status and medical visa

Medical Korea describes two medical visa types. The C-3-3 is the short-term visa, for cases where treatment and travel total 90 days or less, such as cosmetic surgery. The G-1-10 is valid for up to one year and is designed for extended treatment and rehabilitation. Medical visas are available to patients who need hospitalization at specialized clinics or rehabilitation facilities, plus immediate family members who provide care.

The Gyeonggi Province English site adds that companions receive multiple-entry visas with one-year validity.

Applications can be made directly or through an agency. In a direct application the patient applies at a Korean consulate with documents such as a passport copy, a medical diagnosis and proof of financial support. In an agency application, a medical institution or facilitator applies through official channels and the patient later presents the certification at the consulate. Essential documents typically include the visa application, a passport copy, a diagnosis document and a document from a Korean medical institution confirming the treatment appointment.

Medical Korea also notes that citizens of 114 countries can enter visa-free for up to 30 days, with some nationalities receiving 90 days. For a short, simple visit, check whether you need a visa at all. If a K-ETA applies to your nationality, confirm that separately with the official K-ETA site.

Step 3: Travel logistics

  1. Confirm your permitted stay and match your flights to the treatment and follow-up dates, with margin in case recovery runs long.
  2. Ask the clinic when a follow-up visit is needed before you fly, and book your return after that date.
  3. If a companion is traveling, check their visa category too.
  4. Arrange lodging near the clinic and confirm whether the clinic has partner arrangements, rather than assuming.
  5. Carry your medical records, medication lists and the clinic's written estimate.

Step 4: Payment and insurance

VisitKorea's answer on medical tourism insurance is direct: foreign patients who are not residents of Korea are not eligible for insurance coverage, and Korea offers no medical-tourism-specific insurance for visitors. It advises contacting your own medical provider or insurance company about international or travel insurance and about what documentation will be needed for treatment.

The National Health Insurance Service's English page for foreigners describes mandatory enrollment for employed foreigners, and for self-employed foreigners and overseas Koreans who have stayed for more than six months. It does not address short-term visitors, which is consistent with VisitKorea's statement.

Practical payment questions to settle before treatment:

  • Which payment methods and currencies the clinic accepts.
  • The deposit amount, when it is due and when it is refundable.
  • Whether the estimate covers anesthesia, medication, tests and follow-up visits.
  • Whether your own insurer covers any part, and what paperwork it needs from the clinic.
  • Whether you will receive an itemized receipt in a language you can read.

Some news reports describe rules on tax refunds for cosmetic procedures changing. This article could not verify that point from an official page, so ask the clinic and the tax authority directly rather than assuming a refund.

If something goes wrong

The Korea JoongAng Daily reports that complaints to the Medical Korea Support Center rose from 12,313 inquiries in 2020 to 51,012 in 2024, that medical disputes grew from 22 to 214 cases, and that cases involving unregistered brokers collecting illegal referral fees also increased. It says cosmetic surgery and dentistry led dispute complaints and that the support center was small, with six contract workers across two branches.

The Korea Medical Dispute Mediation and Arbitration Agency (K-Medi) accepts applications from foreign patients. Those living in Korea can apply online, by email or by fax, and those living abroad by email or fax only. Its counseling center lists +82-2-1670-2545 and +82-2-6210-0080, and the email kmedi@k-medi.or.kr. Keep your estimate, receipts, medical records and messages with the clinic, since any complaint will depend on them.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Assuming Korean national insurance will cover a non-resident visitor.
  • Booking flights before follow-up dates are confirmed.
  • Paying an agent or broker without checking whether the agent is registered. Medical Korea maintains a list of registered facilitators.
  • Accepting a verbal estimate with no written breakdown.
  • Ignoring the visa-free limit if recovery could extend your stay.
  • Leaving without copies of your records for your home doctor.

Rules change, so confirm with the official bodies

This is general information, not medical, legal or financial advice. Visa categories, insurance rules and dispute procedures can change. Confirm visa requirements with a Korean consulate or the Korea Visa Portal, insurance with your own insurer, and procedure details with a licensed doctor. Medical Korea can direct you to the appropriate body.
