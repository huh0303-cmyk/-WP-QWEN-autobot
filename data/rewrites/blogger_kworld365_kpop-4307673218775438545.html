For most Seoul concerts and fan meetings, the subway is the default choice because it is color-coded, signed in several languages and has a published schedule, while buses fill gaps where no station is close. The decision that matters most is not which line to take there but how you will get home after the show, because there is no single last-train time for the whole city.

Subway first, bus second: how they differ

Visit Seoul describes the subway as one of the most efficient ways to get around the city, running from about 5:30 AM to midnight, with color-coded lines and signage in Korean, English and Chinese. It also says transfers between lines are free.

Buses are color-coded too. Visit Seoul lists four types: blue for main routes, green for residential areas, yellow for circulation and red for rapid service. SeoulStart describes the same system in terms of bus stops, with blue trunk buses between outer districts and downtown, green branch or village buses for neighborhoods, yellow circulators within districts, and red express buses from the metro area to downtown whose routes always start with 9.

A simple rule of thumb that follows from this: use the subway for the long leg of the trip, and use a green or yellow bus or a short walk for the last stretch if the venue is not next to a station.

Choosing an app

SeoulStart names three subway options beyond Naver Map:

  • Seoul Subway: the city's official app for foreigners, launched in 2023, with real-time timetables and wrong-train alerts.
  • Subway Korea: a third-party app covering five cities, with English, Japanese and Chinese support.
  • KakaoMetro: Kakao's app, described as Korean-first with potential English coverage gaps.

Pick one primary app, install a second as a backup, and test route searches at your hotel before event day. Searching from the venue crowd, on a congested network, is a bad time to learn the interface.

Reading bus information on the street

According to SeoulStart, bus stop signs carry a five-digit ARS ID. Entering or scanning that code in an app gives a live arrival countdown instead of relying on a printed schedule. It is worth saving the ARS ID of the stop nearest your lodging and the one near the venue.

Fare logic you need to know

SeoulStart says Seoul's integrated system allows up to four transfers, five rides in total, on one fare if you tag within 30 minutes of getting off, extended to 60 minutes between 9 PM and 7 AM. The critical rule is to tag your card both when boarding and when getting off; missing the exit tag breaks the transfer chain and can trigger a new base fare. A T-money guide on seoultourism.org agrees that switching between subway lines, or between subway and bus, within 30 minutes carries no extra base fare when using the T-money card.

That late-night 60-minute window is useful after evening events, but check the current rule in your app or at the station, since transit operators update fare policies.

The last-train problem

SeoulStart is direct about this: there is no single citywide last-train time. Seoul Metro publishes departure times by individual station, and transfer stations may close earlier than your starting station's last train. For concerts that end late, this is where most trips go wrong.

Plan the return before you go:

  1. Look up the last train from the venue station toward your lodging, including transfers.
  2. Subtract the time it will take to exit a crowded venue and reach the platform.
  3. Decide a cut-off time. If the show may run past it, arrange a fallback now.
  4. Fallbacks include a late-night bus, a taxi, or staying overnight nearby. Visit Seoul notes that regular taxis are the most affordable taxi type, and that international taxis with English-speaking drivers exist, but check fares in advance.

A decision checklist for event day

  1. Is the venue within a short walk of a station? If yes, subway only.
  2. If not, is there a green or yellow bus, and what is its ARS stop ID?
  3. How many transfers are required, and can you finish them within the transfer window?
  4. When is the last train from the venue station, and does your transfer station close earlier?
  5. Is your card balance enough for the trip home, and do you have a backup payment method?
  6. Does the event organizer publish its own transport or shuttle notice?

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Not tagging out, which can reset the transfer discount.
  • Assuming the last train at your hotel station matches the last train at the venue station.
  • Depending on one app that may lack English coverage for a route.
  • Choosing a bus without checking the stop's ARS ID and the live arrival time.
  • Skipping the organizer's own transport notices, which can differ from general guidance.

Schedules change, so confirm

Operating hours, fares and transfer rules are set by the transit operators and can change. This is general travel information, not an official service notice. Check Seoul Metro, Visit Seoul or your transit app for the current timetable close to your travel date, and follow the venue's own instructions on the day.
