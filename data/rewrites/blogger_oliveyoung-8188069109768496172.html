To judge K-beauty value, ignore the box and compare the stated volume (ml or g) against the price, then check the expiry date and whether the size can travel with you. Korean law requires cosmetics to carry basic information on the label, and airport rules limit how much liquid you can bring in the cabin, so the right size depends on both the price per milliliter and how you will fly home.

Read the printed volume, not the packaging

Packaging can make a product look larger than it is. A tall box, a thick jar or a gift set may hold a small amount of product plus extras such as samples, a spatula or a mini item. The number that matters is the volume or weight printed on the label of each item, usually in ml or g.

Use a simple method in the store:

  1. Find the printed volume of the main product, not the whole set.
  2. Divide the price by the volume to get the price per ml or per gram.
  3. Do the same for the alternative size or the set.
  4. Count the extras separately. If you would not use the samples or accessories, they should not count as value.

A bigger size can be cheaper per ml, but not always, and a limited-edition set can cost more per ml than the regular item. The only way to know is to calculate. Many phones have a calculator, so doing this at the shelf takes less than a minute per product.

What a Korean cosmetic label must show

According to a ChemLinked report on Korea's cosmetic labeling rules, Korean cosmetics are required to display the product name, the business name, the expiry date and an ingredient list, with some exceptions for small products. The same report says that an amendment promulgated on July 9, 2024 changed small-volume rules so that certain product types, namely external genital cleansers and eyelash perm wave products, must show both ingredients and precautionary information regardless of product size, with that part effective a year later.

For a shopper, the useful lesson is to look for the expiry date before buying, especially on sale items and multi-packs, and to check the ingredient list, which can be small on mini sizes. If a label is only in Korean, use a translation app on the ingredient list and ask staff about the expiry date if you cannot find it.

Match the size to how you will use it

Waste happens when you buy more product than you can finish before it expires, or when you buy a size you cannot carry home. Think about the following before picking a large size:

  • Frequency of use. A daily-use cleanser or sunscreen is easy to finish, while a specialty treatment used once a week may take much longer.
  • Sensitivity. If you have not tried the product, a small size or sample is a cheaper test than a large jar.
  • Expiry date. Compare the date on the label with how long you expect to use the product.
  • Sharing. A large set can make sense if you will split it with a friend or family member.

Flight rules shape which sizes make sense

Incheon International Airport states that liquids in carry-on bags must be in containers of 100 ml or less, based on container size regardless of how much is inside. They must fit in one transparent ziplock bag (20 cm by 20 cm), with one bag per person. The airport lists perfume, skin toner, lotion, shampoo, conditioner, toothpaste and sunscreen among restricted liquid items. A separate Incheon page says gels, cosmetics and toiletries are covered by the same 100 ml rule, with a 1 liter transparent bag, and that domestic flights have no such restriction.

Two practical consequences:

  • A 150 ml toner is over the carry-on limit even if it is nearly empty, because the rule is based on container size. Plan to pack it in checked baggage.
  • Hani Seoul notes that products soaked in liquid, such as toner pads and essence-soaked sheets, are treated as liquids, gels and aerosols. Bulk sheet masks may therefore need to go in checked baggage as well.

For checked baggage, Incheon's page says non-flammable sprays are permitted within limits of 500 g (500 ml) per item and 2 kg (2 L) in total. Hani Seoul's packing advice is to wrap glass bottles individually in soft clothing, seal caps with plastic wrap and place items in the center of the suitcase.

Items bought at airport duty-free are handled differently. Incheon says duty-free liquid purchases can bypass the 100 ml rule if kept in the sealed duty-free liquid item bag until your final flight. Check your connecting flights, because rules at transfer airports may differ.

A quick buying checklist

  1. Read the printed volume of the main product.
  2. Calculate price per ml or per gram for each size.
  3. Check the expiry date on the label.
  4. Decide how you will fly: cabin only or checked bag.
  5. Keep any liquid over 100 ml out of your cabin ziplock bag.
  6. Keep receipts for the products you plan to claim a tax refund on, and do not open them before customs. See VisitKorea's refund rules if you plan to claim.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Judging size by the box and not the printed ml or g.
  • Counting samples and accessories as part of the main product value.
  • Ignoring the expiry date on multi-packs.
  • Assuming the carry-on limit depends on how full the bottle is. Incheon says it depends on container size.
  • Forgetting that soaked pads and sheet masks may count as liquids.
  • Assuming rules at your connecting airport match Incheon's.

Rules change, so confirm with the official bodies

Labeling rules and airport restrictions can change. This is general shopping information, not legal or regulatory advice. For cosmetics regulation, check the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety. For carry-on rules, check Incheon Airport's official notice or your airline before you pack.
