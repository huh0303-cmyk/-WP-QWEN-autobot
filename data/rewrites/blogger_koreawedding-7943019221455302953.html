Registering a marriage between a Korean and a foreign national is a filing at the local district office (gu office), not a ceremony. The foreign partner generally needs proof of single status from their embassy, translated into Korean, plus passport or ID and two witnesses. Once the marriage is recorded, the next step is usually an F-6 spouse visa, so the order in which you collect documents matters.

Where the registration happens and what the office asks for

The Dongdaemun-gu Office's English page on international marriage lists the documents for a marriage registered in Korea first:

  • The foreign spouse's affidavit of eligibility (original) and its Korean translation, with the translator's details.
  • The foreign spouse's passport or proof of nationality.
  • The completed marriage registration form.
  • Both partners' personal ID (originals) and seals or signatures.
  • Two witnesses' names, resident registration numbers, addresses, and seals or signatures.

The same page says all submitted certificates must be originals and will not be returned, and that the office may ask for additional documents. Other district offices may vary slightly, so call the office you plan to use.

Proving single status through your embassy

The Korea Herald's legal column ("Ask a Lawyer") says foreigners must obtain an Affidavit of Eligibility for Marriage from their home country's embassy in Korea to prove they are unmarried, and that it must be translated before use. It adds that the two witnesses need not be present and that a foreign partner living abroad can authorize the Korean spouse to submit documents by mail. That article was published in January 2023, so treat its details as a starting point.

The U.S. Embassy's page gives an example for U.S. citizens. They bring proof of citizenship and photo ID to the Embassy, which notarizes the affidavit for a USD 50 fee; the notarized affidavit is then translated into Korean when the spouse is Korean and taken to the district office with two witnesses. The Embassy says the registration certificate is issued immediately when both spouses are U.S. citizens, or within 3 to 5 days when one spouse is Korean. Citizens of other countries are told to contact their own embassy.

If you married abroad first

Dongdaemun-gu states that for a marriage registered overseas first, you submit the original marriage certificate from the issuing country, its Korean translation, the foreign spouse's passport or proof of nationality, the registration form, and the registering party's ID and seal or signature. It also says the report must be made in Korea within 3 months of the overseas registration. Check this deadline with the district office as soon as your foreign certificate is issued.

Plan a realistic timeline

  1. Ask your embassy which documents prove single status and how long issuance takes.
  2. Have the affidavit or certificate translated into Korean, with the translator's signature and date.
  3. Choose two witnesses and collect their details.
  4. File at the district office and ask how many certified copies you will receive, and when.
  5. Request additional certified copies immediately, since later applications will need them.

The visa handoff after registration

Mr. Visa Korea's F-6 page, which cites HiKorea and the Ministry of Justice, says the applicant must be legally married to a Korean citizen, with the marriage valid under the laws of both Korea and the home country and recorded in the Korean family register. It describes a document list that includes a passport, an application form, the Korean spouse's civil registry documents issued within three months, proof of income, a relationship statement, and evidence of basic Korean communication ability, with a possible interview about the relationship.

The Korea Herald column says foreign spouses receive an F-6 marriage immigrant visa, initially valid for one year, and may apply for F-5 permanent residency after two years in F-6 status. Income thresholds and language rules are published in different figures by different websites, so use the immigration authority's current notice rather than a blog.

If any supporting document is in a language other than Korean, plan for translation and, where required, notarization or apostille through the issuing country's process. Check which one the immigration office expects.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Translating the affidavit without the translator's name, signature and date.
  • Missing the 3-month window after an overseas marriage.
  • Assuming your embassy issues the same affidavit as another embassy. Each has its own process.
  • Collecting only one copy of the registration certificate.
  • Relying on a third-party site for visa income or language rules.

Rules change: confirm with the official body

This is general information, not legal or immigration advice. Marriage registration and spouse visa rules are set by the Ministry of Justice, immigration offices and district offices and can change. Confirm the current requirements with your district office, your embassy, and HiKorea or the Korea Immigration Service before filing or applying.
