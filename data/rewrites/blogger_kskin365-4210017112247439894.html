The simplest way to keep a skincare routine intact while traveling is to bring a short list of familiar products in containers that meet airline liquid rules, protect your skin from the sun, and moisturize more than usual. Pack what you already tolerate, and save new products for after the trip.

Pack familiar products in travel sizes

The American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) advises bringing your own skin care products in travel sizes. Its reasoning is that hotel products often contain fragrance that can irritate sensitive skin, and familiar products lower the risk of an unexpected reaction. A trip is a poor moment to test a new active ingredient, because you cannot easily tell what caused a rash when everything around you has changed.

A practical minimum kit:

  • A gentle cleanser you already use.
  • A moisturizer you already use.
  • A broad-spectrum sunscreen.
  • Any prescribed topical treatment, kept in its original packaging.

Check the liquid rules for your flight

Creams, gels and lotions count as liquids at security. The U.S. Transportation Security Administration (TSA) says a quart-sized bag of liquids, aerosols, gels, creams and pastes is allowed in carry-on bags, with containers of 3.4 ounces (100 milliliters) or less per item, and that larger items belong in checked luggage.

If you are flying out of Korea, Incheon International Airport's carry-on page says each container must be 100 ml or less based on container size, regardless of how much is actually inside. It requires items to go in a transparent ziplock bag of 20 cm x 20 cm, one bag per person. The page names skin toner, lotion, perfume, sprays, and gels such as sunscreen as covered products, and says medications over 100 ml require a doctor's note or prescription. A half-empty large bottle still fails the rule.

Airlines and countries can differ, so check your departure and transit airports before packing.

Keep sun protection at the top of the list

The AAD recommends a broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen with an SPF of 30 or higher, reapplied every two hours or after swimming or sweating, along with seeking shade and wearing protective clothing. Sunscreen is a gel or cream under airport rules, so it needs to fit the 100 ml limit in carry-on. If your bottle is larger, put it in checked baggage or buy one at your destination.

Moisturize for dry cabins and new climates

According to the AAD, airplanes and different climates can dry the skin. It suggests applying moisturizer to damp skin after bathing and as needed, and using petroleum jelly to help lock in moisture on dry skin. The same page suggests petroleum jelly where skin rubs together if you will be walking a lot, to prevent chafing and blisters.

Other skin risks on the road

The AAD list goes beyond products. It recommends wearing socks in security lines and hotel rooms and flip-flops or shower shoes in bathrooms and pool areas to avoid athlete's foot and fungal nail infections. It advises inspecting hotel furniture for bedbugs on arrival, since they can hide even in nice hotels, and using bug spray during outdoor activities like hiking to reduce the risk of disease from unfamiliar insects. Insect repellent sprays are also covered by Incheon's liquid limit, so check the container size for those as well.

None of these steps needs special products. They mostly need a little planning before you zip the suitcase.

Keep product information handy

Keep original packaging or a photo of each label and ingredient list. If your skin reacts, you can tell a clinician or pharmacist exactly what you used. Labels also help if you need to replace a product abroad, since formulas and names often differ between countries.

A simple travel-day checklist

  1. Measure every container against the 100 ml rule, using the container size and not what is left inside.
  2. Put carry-on liquids in one transparent ziplock bag, within the size your departure airport states.
  3. Place larger bottles and anything you cannot decant in checked baggage.
  4. Keep sunscreen and moisturizer where you can reach them.
  5. Photograph product labels before you leave.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Assuming a partly used large bottle passes. Airport rules go by container size.
  • Trying a new serum or exfoliant just before a flight or a beach day.
  • Packing medication over 100 ml without the paperwork Incheon describes.
  • Applying sunscreen once and not reapplying.
  • Relying on hotel toiletries if your skin is fragrance-sensitive.

Rules change: confirm with the official body

This is general information, not medical advice. Airport security rules differ by country and can change, and skin conditions need individual advice. Check your airline and the airport authorities for current limits, and ask a doctor or pharmacist about any persistent reaction or prescribed treatment.
