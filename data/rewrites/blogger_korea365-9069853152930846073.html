If you plan to stay in Korea longer than 90 days, the first legal task is alien registration, and the second is keeping your address up to date. According to the Incheon city English guide, a foreigner who wants to stay more than 90 days from the date of entry must register within 90 days of entry, and must report changes of residence within 14 days. This guide covers those two steps plus the health insurance and help lines that most newcomers need in daily life.

Alien registration: who, when and where

The Incheon guide says registration is required for foreigners staying more than 90 days, with exemptions for certain diplomats and holders of A-1, A-2 and A-3 visas. You register at an immigration office. For Incheon, that is the Incheon Immigration Office; elsewhere, use the office that covers your address. Registration should be done as soon as you receive permission to stay or change status.

The documents listed are your passport, the foreigner registration form and a color photo of 3.5 by 4.5 cm. The guide adds that other documents vary by visa type. The residence card fee listed is 30,000 won. Ask the immigration office whether your visa needs extra papers such as an enrollment certificate or employment contract before you go.

Reporting a change of address

Registered foreigners must report a change of residence, personal details or passport information within 14 days of the change, according to the Incheon guide, to avoid penalties under the Immigration Act. The Seoul Jung-gu English page says the 14 days include weekends and holidays, and that after the period passes, reporting goes to the immigration office only, with potential fines.

The Jung-gu page lists four places to report a move: the local gu office, the immigration office, a dong community center, or the HiKorea website. Documents are the change of residence application, the passport, and proof of the new address such as a rental agreement. If someone files for you, power of attorney, the agent's ID and proof of relationship are needed. The page says approval is instant, and gives the immigration hotline as 1345.

Health insurance in daily life

The NHIS says it supports foreign residents in English, Chinese, Uzbek and Vietnamese through 1577-1000 extension 6. A guide on Seoulstart says foreigners on long-stay visas are subject to mandatory enrollment after six months of residence, while employees are enrolled by their employers from the start. It lists a 2026 workplace premium of 7.19% of monthly salary, split evenly between employee and employer. Sungkyunkwan University's international office says D-2 degree students are enrolled automatically at alien registration. Rules vary by visa, so confirm yours with NHIS.

After you register

Once your registration is processed, the next tasks follow from it. The SKKU international office says an insurance card and subscription guide are mailed to your Korean address for students, and that before the registration card arrives you can use a certificate of foreigner registration for services. For first-time registration in Seoul, the Jung-gu page names the Seoul Immigration Office (02-2650-6212, near Subway Line 5) and the Sejong-ro office (02-731-1799, near Jonggak Station). Phone numbers can change, so check HiKorea or call 1345 if a number does not connect.

Because notices and cards go to your registered address, report any move promptly. A missed letter about insurance or an immigration matter can cause problems that are slow to fix.

Where to get help in your language

  • Immigration questions: the 1345 hotline listed by Jung-gu, and the HiKorea website.
  • Health insurance: 1577-1000 extension 6, with 033-811-2000 as the direct line.
  • Marriage migrants and multicultural families: the Danuri Call Center, 1577-1366, weekdays 9:00 to 18:00, which the Korean government's foreigner services page lists with Korean classes, interpreting and counseling.

A first-weeks checklist

  1. Note the date you entered Korea and count 90 days from it.
  2. Gather your passport, photo and visa documents, then book or visit the immigration office.
  3. Keep your rental agreement, since it proves your address.
  4. After any move, report the new address within 14 days.
  5. Ask NHIS or your employer or school when your health insurance starts.
  6. Save the 1345 and 1577-1000 numbers in your phone.

If you are unsure which office covers your address, the HiKorea site and the 1345 hotline are the places to ask, since the Incheon and Jung-gu pages describe their own local offices only. Ask which office handles your area and whether you need an appointment before you travel there with your documents.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Waiting until the last day. Appointments can be limited, so register early.
  • Forgetting a move. A change of address inside the 14-day window is easy to miss.
  • Assuming insurance starts the same for everyone. Employees, students and others differ.
  • Using a blog as your only source. Check HiKorea or the immigration office for your visa type.

Rules change: confirm with the official bodies

This is general information, not legal or immigration advice. Visa rules, fees and deadlines change and depend on your status. Confirm current requirements with the Korea Immigration Service through HiKorea or the 1345 hotline, and with NHIS for insurance, before you act.
