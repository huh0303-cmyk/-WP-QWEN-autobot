The most useful thing you can do on moving day in Korea is create a dated record of the room's condition, send it to your landlord or agent, and keep the paperwork that proves you live at the address. A photo record protects you when the deposit is returned, and the address filings protect the deposit itself. This guide covers both, using what published guides and local-government pages say.

Why a condition record matters

SeoulStart's guide to getting a rental deposit back says that landlords may deduct for damage beyond normal use, such as holes, broken fixtures, excessive stains or missing items, but not for natural wear, faded paint, minor scuffs or damage already documented at move-in. It also says that unless the contract has a special clause shifting restoration costs to the tenant, landlords bear the cost of normal wear. The practical point is that a record made on day one is the evidence for what was already there.

The same guide recommends photographing every room, wall and appliance and sending the photos to the landlord on move-in day through a messaging app such as KakaoTalk, because the timestamp creates a record. Ask the landlord to acknowledge receipt in the same chat.

What to record, room by room

  • Walls, ceiling and floor: stains, cracks, marks, damp patches and any scratches on flooring.
  • Windows and doors: locks, handles, screens and seals.
  • Bathroom and kitchen: taps, drains, sink, cabinets and any visible mould.
  • Appliances and furniture supplied: list each item and its condition, and match the list against the lease.
  • Meters and keys: note meter readings and the number of keys or access cards you receive.

Take wide shots and close-ups, and include a few frames that show the date on the phone if the app does not. Keep the files in a folder you can find later and back them up.

Match the room to the agreement

Before you accept the keys, check that the unit and the included items match the written lease. If something is missing or broken, write it down and message it to the landlord at once rather than assuming it will be sorted out later. SeoulStart's housing timeline recommends keeping the contract, registry copies, building register, transfer receipts, photos and agent communications together as one set.

Filings after moving in

A condition record covers the room; these filings cover your legal position.

  1. Report the new address: SeoulStart's housing timeline says a registered foreigner must report a new place of stay within 15 days after moving, citing the Immigration Act. Mason Korea's international student page gives the same 15-day window for address changes.
  2. Register as a foreign resident if you have not yet: Jung-gu Office in Seoul says foreigners staying 91 days or longer must apply for alien registration within 90 days of entry, and that appointments are reserved through the HiKorea website. Mason Korea notes that students living off campus need a rental contract or residency certificate for the application.
  3. Confirmed date and lease report: SeoulStart says that once you have taken possession, completed the address report and obtained a confirmed date, priority to the deposit can arise from 0:00 the next day, and that covered leases above certain thresholds must be reported within 30 days.

Fees for alien registration are quoted differently by different sources (Jung-gu Office lists 30,000 won, while Mason Korea and SeoulStart list 35,000 won), so confirm the current fee with your immigration office when you book.

Utilities, access and practical details

Agree with your contact how parcels, building entry and shared areas work, and record the answers in writing. No source reviewed here covers utility account procedures in detail, so ask the landlord or agent which accounts are in whose name and what the building's management fee covers, and record the answer in the same chat as your photos.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Taking photos but not sending them anywhere, so there is no independent timestamp.
  • Photographing only obvious damage and missing small marks that could later be blamed on you.
  • Assuming that a photo record replaces the address report or confirmed date. It does not.
  • Including another person's belongings or private papers in photos that you share.
  • Agreeing to a special clause on restoration costs without reading it. SeoulStart says such a clause can shift costs to the tenant.

At move-out

SeoulStart's deposit-return guide advises giving written notice at least two months before the lease ends to avoid an automatic two-year renewal, cleaning, photographing every room again and returning all keys and access cards. If a deposit is not returned, it describes sending a formal demand by certified mail, applying to the district court for a tenancy registration order and, for deposits up to 30 million won, small-claims proceedings. Compare your move-out photos to the day-one record before the landlord inspects.

Rules change: confirm with the official body

This is general information, not legal advice. Notice periods, deposit rules, fees and reporting thresholds can change, and your contract may differ. Confirm the details with the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport, your local district office, the immigration office for registration, or a free legal consultation such as the Korea Legal Aid Corporation, which SeoulStart names.
