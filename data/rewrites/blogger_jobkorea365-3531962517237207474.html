An international student who graduates from a Korean university has a short window to decide between leaving, staying to look for work on a D-10 job-seeker visa, or going straight to a work visa if a job offer is already in hand. The key point is timing: according to Ajou University's international office, a D-2 student visa is valid for only up to 30 days after graduation, so the next step has to be filed before it expires.

Option 1: apply for D-10 to search for a job

The Korea Immigration Service's Visa Navigator describes D-10 as a job-seeker status covering general job seekers, tech start-up preparers and high-tech internship participants. The period of stay is six months (one year for the high-tech internship) and is extendable. D-10 is not a work visa: the guide says D-10 holders may do internship activities in fields that fit E-1 to E-7 status, and part-time work is allowed only for graduates who meet specific criteria, including TOPIK level 4 or higher.

Ajou University lists the steps and documents for a D-2 to D-10 change:

  • Apply before the D-2 expires, and submit two to three weeks ahead because processing takes time.
  • Apply online through HiKorea (e-application, change of status of sojourn) or book an in-person visit at the local immigration office through HiKorea.
  • Prepare the application form, passport and alien registration card, certificate of (expected) graduation, transcript, a job-seeking plan in Korean or English in free format, and proof of residence and living expenses. A TOPIK certificate is optional.
  • Pay the fee, which Ajou lists as KRW 130,000.

The university adds that D-10 is usually valid for six months and does not automatically permit work, so separate immigration permission is needed. Requirements can differ slightly between immigration offices.

Option 2: move directly to a work visa

If you already have an offer, the Visa Navigator says a change from D-10 to a professional employment status (E-1 to E-7) requires meeting the employment criteria for the occupation. E-7 covers special occupations: professional, semi-professional or skilled workers. Employers typically need to help with documents, and the immigration office decides each case.

The K-Work platform is aimed at this step. Korea.net reports that it was launched by the Ministry of SMEs and Startups and the Korea SMEs and Startups Agency, and offers AI-powered job recommendations from resumes, one-stop help converting a D-2 or D-10 visa to an E-7-1 work visa, and university database links to verify credentials. The article described it as in beta.

Where to look for information and jobs

The Korea Herald reports that the Study in Korea portal now acts as a permanent hub for university programs, scholarships, visas and employment, including local-government support policies and region-specific visa schemes. K-Work (above) is a job-matching service focused on small and medium enterprises. Always check the current status of any platform on its own site, because services are updated and, in K-Work's case, still developing.

After you start work

  • An E-7 holder who wants to change or add a workplace must obtain permission in advance, according to the Visa Navigator.
  • Foreigners staying more than 90 days must register within 90 days of arrival, and registered foreigners must report a new address within 15 days, according to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and the Seoul Immigration guide.
  • The Seoul Immigration guide says unpaid taxes or health insurance premiums can restrict extending your stay, so keep those in order.

A timeline worked backwards from the expiry date

Using the rules above, plan like this. Find the date your D-2 expires, which is up to 30 days after graduation according to Ajou. Aim to submit the change at least two to three weeks earlier, as Ajou advises. Collect the certificate of graduation and transcript before that date. If you get an offer after the D-10 starts, the six-month stay gives you time to complete the job search, and any move to an E-7 status will need the employer's documents and the immigration office's approval.

A decision checklist for the final semester

  1. Write down the date your D-2 expires and the last day you can file.
  2. Decide whether you need D-10 (still searching) or can go straight to a work visa (offer in hand).
  3. Order your certificate of graduation and transcript early.
  4. Draft a job-seeking plan that names target roles and employers.
  5. Check your Korean-language certificates if you may want part-time work later.
  6. Ask each employer who will handle the visa paperwork and the permission for work.

Common mistakes to verify

  • Letting the D-2 expire before filing for D-10.
  • Starting work or an internship without the permission the visa requires.
  • Assuming a TOPIK score is optional for every purpose. It is listed as optional for the D-10 application but TOPIK level 4 or higher is among the criteria for part-time work.
  • Ignoring address-change and registration deadlines.
  • Relying on a friend's experience: documents and fees can differ by office and over time.

Rules change: confirm with the official body

This article is general information, not legal or immigration advice. Fees, documents, and status rules change. Confirm the current requirements with the Korea Immigration Service (HiKorea or the 1345 Immigration Contact Center), your university's international office and, for labor questions, the Ministry of Employment and Labor.
