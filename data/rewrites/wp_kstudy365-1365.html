A Korean language course and a degree programme need separate calendars because they use different visas, different documents and often different offices. Build the calendar backwards from the start date of the exact programme you want, using the institution's current guide, and add visa processing time at the end. Published guides suggest starting 6 to 8 months before you travel for degree applications, and the language course route has its own steps.

Language course or degree: the visa decides the paperwork

According to study-abroad.org's student visa guide, the D-2 visa covers degree programmes from associate level to PhD, while language programmes at university-affiliated institutes that run longer than 90 days require a D-4-1 visa. The same guide says language study is a common route before moving on to a degree. Another guide on Edarabia adds that short summer programmes do not require a student visa, and that visitors on such programmes receive a short stay on arrival.

Write down which of the three situations you are in: a short course (no student visa), a long language course (D-4-1), or a degree (D-2).

Start with the programme record

Create one record per programme with the institution, campus, programme type and intake, and save the current admissions guide next to it. Use the Study in Korea portal to find institutions, then rely on the university's own instructions for documents and dates. Study-abroad.org's visa and arrival page says the major intakes are in March and September, with applications for March typically processed in January and February and for September in July and August. Individual institutions publish their own windows, so treat those as general patterns.

Work backwards from the deadline

Study-abroad.org's visa guide gives a general timeline. It suggests beginning a university application 6 to 8 months before arrival, allowing 3 to 5 months for the whole process from admission to visa, and expecting embassy processing of 2 to 4 weeks, or 3 to 6 weeks in peak season. It adds that some universities use a visa issuance number, which can shorten processing; its visa and arrival page mentions 1 to 3 days in some cases. These are estimates from one publisher, so ask your university what it handles and how long its own steps take.

A working calendar for a degree might look like this:

  1. Application deadline minus several weeks: language test results and transcripts ready.
  2. After submission: wait for the result and, if the university asks, order authenticated documents.
  3. After an offer: prepare the certificate of admission and visa documents.
  4. Visa stage: embassy appointment, processing and passport return.
  5. Arrival: alien registration within 90 days, as the same guide notes for the registration card.

Documents that differ between the two routes

For a D-4 language course, Edarabia lists the visa application form, passport copy and photo, the institution's registration papers, a standard acceptance letter from the institution's head, a diploma or enrolment letter, financial proof such as a balance statement issued in the past 30 days, a family relations certificate, and the training schedule and course details.

For degree programmes, study-abroad.org lists a passport with six months of validity, photos, the certificate of admission, a bank statement for a month or more, apostilled transcripts and degrees, language test scores and a tuberculosis test for some nationalities. Seoul National University's admissions FAQ says apostilles are required only after preliminary admission, while Yonsei University's guide says the certificate of admission needs the final education certificate and a bank balance certificate. Financial thresholds differ between publishers, with study-abroad.org giving one figure in US dollars and another guide giving a figure in won, so ask your university and embassy which applies.

How a language course can feed a later degree application

Yonsei University's application guide lists a certificate of completion from a Korean language institution as one accepted form of language evidence for most majors, but requires TOPIK level 5 or above, or an equivalent Yonsei certification, for pre-medicine, pre-dentistry, nursing and pharmacy. SNU's FAQ says it accepts TOPIK level 3 or higher for Korean proficiency. If you plan a language course first, work out which type of evidence your target degree accepts, and when your course will end relative to its application deadline.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Borrowing another student's checklist. Different routes need different papers.
  • Starting the visa process before the institution has issued the admission document it requires.
  • Ordering authentication too early or too late. SNU asks for the apostille after preliminary admission.
  • Planning a course that ends after the degree deadline you are aiming for.
  • Ignoring peak-season delays. Study-abroad.org says embassy processing can run longer in peak periods.
  • Assuming a short course and a long course need the same visa.

Rules change: confirm with the official body

This is general information, not immigration advice. Intake dates, visa documents, financial thresholds and processing times change and differ by university and embassy. Confirm the details with the institution's admissions office, the official Study in Korea portal, and the Korean embassy or consulate that will handle your visa.
