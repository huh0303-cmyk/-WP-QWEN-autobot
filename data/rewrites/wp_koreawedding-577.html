For a marriage report between a Korean national and a foreign national, the core documents are the foreign partner's proof of nationality, a home-country document showing they are legally free to marry, Korean translations, and a completed report form with two witnesses. The slowest item is usually the home-country document, which can take from a single day to ten weeks or more depending on the country, so it sets your timeline.

The documents, one by one

Songpa-gu Office says that for a marriage registered in Korea, the foreign national submits documents proving nationality (a copy of the family register, birth certificate, passport copy or identity register) and proof from a government office or embassy that they meet the marriage requirements of their home country.

Dongdaemun-gu Office lists the following when the marriage is reported in Korea first:

  • The foreign spouse's affidavit of eligibility for marriage, original plus Korean translation.
  • Passport or nationality documents.
  • The completed marriage registration form.
  • Original identification documents for both people.
  • Seals or signatures from both people.
  • Names, resident registration numbers, addresses and signatures or seals of two witnesses.

SeoulStart's step-by-step guide adds that the foreign spouse typically brings a passport and an alien registration card if they have one, the Korean spouse brings a national ID, and witnesses must be at least 19. It also says some neighbourhood centres do not handle marriage reports, so confirm which office to visit.

Authentication and translation

According to SeoulStart's guide to home-country documents, the certificate of no impediment must be authenticated either by apostille, for countries in the Hague apostille system, or by consular legalisation for countries outside it. Legalisation involves a ministry step and an embassy step, which takes longer and costs more. The Korean translation must be signed by the translator, and the guide says government certification of the translation is not legally required. Dongdaemun-gu's page also requires that translations include the translator's personal information and signature, and that submitted certificates are originals that will not be returned.

The same guide states that Korean law does not set a single fixed validity window for the certificate and that district offices apply their own window in practice. That is the reason to ask the office before you order: a document ordered too early may be rejected as stale, and one ordered too late delays the whole plan.

A working plan

  1. Several months ahead: contact your embassy about the home-country document and ask your Korean district office how recent it must be.
  2. Once the document is ordered: choose a translator who will sign with personal details, and decide on two witnesses aged 19 or older.
  3. Before the visit: check that names match across passport, alien registration card and the home-country document.
  4. Filing day: bring originals and the completed form. SeoulStart says same-day processing is standard when documents are complete, and Dongdaemun-gu notes that officials may ask for additional documents.
  5. Afterwards: request your marriage certificate from the family-registration counter or the court's online registry, which SeoulStart names as the efamily.scourt.go.kr service.

If you married abroad first

Dongdaemun-gu's page says you must report the marriage in Korea within three months of registering it abroad, with the original foreign marriage certificate, its Korean translation, passport or nationality documents, the report form and the reporting spouse's ID. SeoulStart cites the same three-month window and a fine of up to 50,000 won for missing it, while Songpa-gu's page mentions filing with a Korean embassy within one month. Because these differ, ask the office or embassy you will use which deadline applies.

Budget: what to expect

SeoulStart says there is no filing fee and that a marriage certificate costs about 1,000 won when issued separately. Costs come mainly from home-country fees (such as the 50 US dollar embassy example), translation, courier and travel. None of the pages reviewed here gives a single reliable total, and prices differ by country and translator, so ask for written quotes. Put aside extra for a second attempt if a document is rejected.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Ordering the home-country document before asking the office how recent it must be.
  • Unsigned translations or missing translator details.
  • Name spellings that differ between documents.
  • A witness under 19.
  • Filing at an office that does not take marriage reports.
  • Missing the deadline for a marriage registered abroad.

Rules change: confirm with the official body

This is general information, not legal advice. Document names, authentication routes and deadlines change, and offices apply their own practice. Confirm the current list with the district office where you will file, your embassy, and for visas the Korea Immigration Service (HiKorea).
