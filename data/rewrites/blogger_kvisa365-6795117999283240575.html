K-ETA is the Korea Electronic Travel Authorization, an online pre-approval that travelers from visa-free countries have to obtain before boarding a flight or ship to Korea. According to a Korean Ministry of Foreign Affairs notice, it costs 10,000 KRW per person and stays valid for multiple trips for three years or until the passport expires, whichever comes first. A temporary exemption currently lets many nationalities skip it, so the first job is to check whether your passport is on the exempt list.

Who needs a K-ETA and who does not

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs notice says K-ETA applies to nationals of visa-free countries before they travel to Korea. It also lists people who do not need one, including holders of diplomatic passports, people who already hold a visa, registered foreigners, dual nationals with a valid Korean passport, US Forces Korea service members, and aircraft and ship crew. The same notice states that travelers aged 17 and under and 65 and over were exempt; confirm that this still applies to you on the official site, because age rules can be revised.

A separate, time-limited measure also matters. VisitKorea reports that the government extended the temporary K-ETA exemption through December 31, 2026. During that period, nationals of visa-exempt countries can enter Korea without a K-ETA, and the official K-ETA website lists the eligible nations. Because the exemption is temporary and country-specific, do not rely on a friend's experience: look up your own nationality.

Why some exempt travelers still apply

VisitKorea notes that exempt travelers may apply voluntarily and that a benefit is exemption from completing an arrival card on entry. Standard fees apply to voluntary applications and, according to the notice, application fees are non-refundable. Previously issued approvals remain valid until they expire.

Without a K-ETA, a traveler is generally asked to complete the e-Arrival Card. A conference guide from EFMD describes the e-Arrival Card as a separate online form, submitted at www.e-arrivalcard.go.kr up to three days before arrival, with a paper version available on the plane. Treat that as a secondary description and check the official arrival-card site before you fly.

Fees, processing time and validity

  • Fee: 10,000 KRW per person, per the Ministry of Foreign Affairs notice. The EFMD guide adds that payment is by credit or debit card and is non-refundable.
  • Validity: multiple trips for three years, or until the passport expires if that comes first. If you renew your passport, the approval tied to the old passport number will not carry over, and the EFMD guide says you need to reapply with the new details.
  • Processing time: the ministry notice says the assessment can take more than 72 hours depending on the situation and suggests applying well ahead of departure. The EFMD guide describes typical processing as 24 to 72 hours and recommends allowing at least a week. Because these two sources differ, the safe approach is to apply early rather than the night before your flight.

What you need to prepare

According to the EFMD guide, you need:

  1. An email address, since status updates come by email.
  2. Your passport details: full name, passport number, nationality and date of birth.
  3. A clear passport-style photo.
  4. Travel details such as travel dates, purpose of visit and accommodation information.
  5. A credit or debit card for the fee.

The guide also says the passport should be valid for at least six months beyond arrival. That is a general travel precaution as much as a K-ETA point, so check your airline and the Korean embassy for the rule that applies to you.

A step-by-step application sequence

  1. Check your nationality against the K-ETA exemption list on the official K-ETA site.
  2. Decide whether to apply anyway, for instance to avoid the arrival card.
  3. Use only the official website, www.k-eta.go.kr, or the official mobile app named in the ministry notice.
  4. Enter passport data exactly as printed in the passport, upload the photo, and answer the travel questions.
  5. Pay the fee and keep the confirmation email.
  6. Check your email for the decision, and save or print the approval for your trip file.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Using an unofficial site. The ministry notice names k-eta.go.kr and the official mobile app as the application channels, so check the web address before entering passport details or paying.
  • Typing passport details from memory. A single wrong digit can mean reapplying and paying again, since fees are non-refundable.
  • Waiting until the last days. Processing times are described differently by different sources, and the ministry notice says some cases take longer than 72 hours.
  • Assuming the exemption is permanent. It currently runs through December 31, 2026, and the rules after that date should be checked with the official K-ETA site.
  • Forgetting the passport change. Approval is tied to the passport, so a renewed passport means a new application.
  • Confusing K-ETA with a visa. K-ETA is a pre-travel authorization for travelers from visa-free countries; the ministry notice lists people who already hold a visa among those who do not need it.

Rules change, so confirm with the official body

This is general information, not legal or immigration advice. Fees, exemption lists and processing times are set by the Korean government and have changed before. Before you apply or travel, confirm the current requirements on the official K-ETA website, with the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, or with the Korean embassy or consulate that serves your country.
