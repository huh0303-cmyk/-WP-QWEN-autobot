The first insurance check for a resident of Korea is whether your National Health Insurance (NHIS) enrollment and premium are correct, because that is the compulsory layer. According to the NHIS page for foreigners, employed foreigners are enrolled through their workplace, and any foreigner or overseas Korean who has stayed in Korea for more than six months is subject to mandatory enrollment (in force since 16 July 2019). Private policies come after that, and they are a separate contract with a separate company.

Step 1: Confirm which route you are enrolled through

The NHIS describes two broad routes. Employed foreigners and overseas Koreans at insured workplaces are enrolled as workplace subscribers. For self-employed foreigners, the page says the six-month residence rule applies. The page lists visa groups covered, including D-series (students, trainees, religious workers, investors), E-series, F-series (residents and marriage immigrants) and H-series visas.

The NHIS page also says an employed foreigner may be excluded if they have equivalent medical coverage through foreign insurance or an employer contract. That is an exemption to be requested and approved, not something that happens automatically, so ask the NHIS if you think it applies to you.

Step 2: Check what your premium should be

For workplace subscribers, the NHIS says the monthly contribution is the monthly wage multiplied by the contribution rate, split 50/50 between employee and employer. For the rate itself, the Korean Tax Expert site and the Seoul Start guide, which says it relies on the Ministry of Health and Welfare, report a 2026 workplace rate of 7.19% of monthly salary, which means 3.595% from the employee and 3.595% from the employer. Those are secondary sources, so compare them with the figure on your payslip and ask your employer to explain any gap.

Self-employed enrollees are assessed differently. The NHIS says their premium is calculated using a contribution score method, the same as for Korean residents.

Step 3: Look for reductions you may qualify for

The NHIS page lists several reductions for foreigners: a 30% reduction for religious workers on D-6 visas, reductions of 50-70% depending on period for students on D-2, D-4 and F-4 status, and a 50% reduction for residents of islands and remote areas. These depend on status and documents, so do not assume a reduction applies. Bring your alien registration card and enrollment documents to the NHIS office and ask directly.

Step 4: Dependents need documents

The NHIS says dependent family members must meet an annual income limit of under KRW 20 million and a property value base threshold of KRW 540 million, and have a valid family relationship. Foreign dependents must provide an alien registration card and government-issued family relationship documents verified by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. The Seoul Start guide adds that since April 2024 most dependents need six months of Korean residence, with an exemption for the spouse and children under 19 of a workplace subscriber. Because that is a secondary source, confirm the dependent rules with the NHIS before relying on them.

Step 5: Do not let premiums go unpaid

The Seoul Start guide reports that late fees apply to overdue premiums and that arrears can affect visa extension decisions. Treat the details as unverified until you have confirmed them with the NHIS and immigration authorities, but the safe approach is simple: set up automatic payment or a payment reminder and respond to any unpaid-premium notice quickly.

Step 6: Review any private insurance separately

A private policy is not part of the NHIS. If you hold one, read the key terms and note the renewal date. Financial News (quoting the Financial Supervisory Service) and Namdo Ilbo describe a cooling-off right: a customer can withdraw within 30 days of applying, but not after 15 days from receiving the insurance certificate, with the insurer refunding premiums within three days of a withdrawal notice. Certain policies, such as short-term policies of under a year and compulsory auto liability insurance, are excluded. These articles date from several years ago, so check the current rule with the FSS or your insurer.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Treating private insurance as a replacement for NHIS enrollment.
  • Assuming a premium reduction applies without applying for it.
  • Bringing incomplete documents for dependents, such as a family relationship certificate without the required verification.
  • Ignoring notices written in Korean. Ask the NHIS English line or a Korean-speaking colleague what a notice says, and keep every notice until the matter is settled.
  • Counting on a news article or blog for rates without comparing it to your payslip.

The NHIS lists a main call center at 1577-1000 and an English consultant line at 033-811-2000.

Rules change: confirm with the official bodies

This article is general information, not legal, medical or financial advice. Premium rates, eligibility rules and reductions are revised regularly. Confirm your own situation with the National Health Insurance Service and, for private policies, with your insurer and the Financial Supervisory Service.
