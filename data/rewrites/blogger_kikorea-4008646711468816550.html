Most foreign investment in Korea is handled by a notification, not an approval, but investments touching national security or designated technologies are screened by the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy (MOTIE). Free Economic Zones (FEZs) add a separate layer of tax, grant and regulatory benefits for investors who locate inside them. This article sets out how the screening works, what the FEZ incentives are, and how the basic incorporation steps run.

How foreign investment screening is organized

White & Case describes the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA) as the main legal framework. It is supported by the Act on Prevention of Divulgence and Protection of Industrial Technology and the Act on Special Measures for Strengthening National High-Tech Strategic Industries. MOTIE is the primary regulator, with a Security Review Expert Committee and MOTIE's Foreign Investment Committee deliberating on national security matters.

According to the same source, two technology lists matter most. National Core Technologies cover 76 designated technologies in fields such as semiconductors, displays, automobiles, shipbuilding, nuclear power, biotechnology, robotics and hydrogen. National High-Tech Strategic Technologies cover 19 technologies across six sectors: semiconductors, displays, secondary batteries, biotechnology, robotics and defense. Defense companies need explicit MOTIE approval.

Thresholds and timelines to know

White & Case lists the general notification trigger as 10% or more of voting shares, the right to appoint officers, or a minimum investment of KRW 100 million. Control transactions, meaning acquisitions of 50% or more of shares or becoming the largest shareholder with dominant management influence, are treated separately.

Transaction typeReported timeline
Routine FIPA notificationA few days
FIPA approval (defense)15 to 30 calendar days
National security review90 days preliminary, with a 30-day extension; 45 days final
Notification for core or strategic technologies15 days, excluding technical examination
Approval for core or strategic technologies45 days, excluding technical examination

Filings are made after the documents are signed but before funds are remitted. White & Case also notes a pre-confirmation process under which an investor can ask MOTIE within 30 days whether screening applies. Outcomes can be approval, conditional approval, or a suspension or prohibition order on national security grounds.

What Free Economic Zones offer

The Korean Free Economic Zone portal lists these benefits for foreign-invested companies:

  • A 100% exemption from tariffs for five years on imported capital goods.
  • Acquisition tax exemption of up to 15 years, and property tax reductions of up to 15 years, set by local ordinances.
  • Cash grants for foreign-invested companies with a foreign investment ratio of 30% or higher. They can cover land and building purchase or rent, construction and employment costs, and the amounts are negotiated based on technology transfer, job creation and company size.
  • Public or government land leases of up to 50 years at roughly 10/1,000 of the land price, with lease fee reductions of 50% to 100% under local ordinances.
  • Regulatory relief, including direct foreign payment transfers up to USD 100,000 and labor rule flexibility.

Developers have separate conditions. The portal says they must bring foreign investment above USD 30 million or a foreign ratio above 50%, within total development costs above USD 500 million. Investors who are not developing zones can mostly ignore that tier.

The incorporation steps after screening

Invest KOREA lays out the incorporation procedure for a foreign-invested company in sequence:

  1. Foreign direct investment notification to KOTRA or a foreign exchange bank, before the investment is made.
  2. Remittance of investment funds through a foreign exchange bank, listed as taking two to three days.
  3. Registration of incorporation at the court registry, also two to three days.
  4. Any needed authorization or permit, which varies by business type.
  5. Notification of incorporation and business registration at the tax office, three to four days.
  6. Opening a corporate bank account. Invest KOREA notes that opening additional accounts at another bank is restricted for 20 business days.
  7. Registration of the foreign-invested company with KOTRA or the bank where the notification was filed, within 60 days of the investment payment.

Invest KOREA puts the whole process at about two weeks and says a residence visa is issued after company registration.

Governance points investors often overlook

Screening is not only about the first investment. If a later share purchase or technology transfer crosses one of the control or technology thresholds, a new filing may be required, so keep a calendar of planned stake changes. Zone benefits also depend on conditions that continue after approval. Invest KOREA's guidance on cash grants, for example, excludes investments financed with long-term loans, and its separate zone rules require investment to be completed within a set period after the occupancy contract.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Remitting funds before filing. White & Case says filings come before remittance.
  • Assuming a technology is outside the lists. Ask MOTIE through the pre-confirmation route, which the source says has a 30-day response window.
  • Treating incentives as automatic. Cash grants are negotiated and local tax breaks follow local ordinances.
  • Counting only the stated days. Technical examination time is excluded from the listed core-technology timelines.

Rules change: confirm with the official bodies

This is general information, not legal, tax or investment advice. Thresholds, technology lists and incentive terms are revised from time to time, and local ordinances differ by zone. Confirm current terms with MOTIE, KOTRA's Invest KOREA, the Korean Free Economic Zone authority for your target zone, and a qualified Korean lawyer or tax adviser before committing capital.
