The smartest way to shop for skincare in Korea is to buy at a large multi-brand beauty retailer, carry your passport, and understand the tax refund rules before you open anything. Foreign tourists can often get a refund on purchases of 15,000 won or more under the official rules described by VisitKorea, but individual stores and third-party guides quote different minimums, so confirm at the register.

Why a multi-brand store makes sense for a first trip

A multi-brand beauty store lets you compare many brands in one place and use a single receipt for a refund. Olive Young's Myeongdong Global Town describes itself as the largest global specialty store in Myeongdong, and such flagship stores are built for international visitors. Whichever store you choose, the practical advantages are the same: one passport check, one receipt, and staff who are used to tourists' questions.

Who qualifies for a tax refund

According to the VisitKorea tax refund guide, the baseline threshold is 15,000 won or more per purchase. Eligibility rules in the same guide include:

  • Foreigners staying in Korea up to six months qualify. Long-term residents (six months or more), diplomats, US military personnel and people employed in Korea do not.
  • Overseas Koreans who have been away for two years or more and are in Korea for three months or less also qualify.
  • The purchased goods must leave Korea within three months.
  • Items that were opened or used before leaving the country are ineligible.

Two ways to get the refund

VisitKorea describes two methods:

  1. Immediate refund at the store. You show your passport at checkout, pay the price with tax already deducted, and then leave Korea. The guide gives a single-transaction limit of under 1 million won and a total limit of 5 million won.
  2. Airport refund. You pay the full price, keep the refund voucher, show the purchased items, voucher and passport to customs for export confirmation, then collect the refund at an airport counter or kiosk. The guide describes the same 15,000-won minimum for this route; check the current per-transaction limit with the store.

The guide lists staffed refund counters at Incheon Terminal 1 (near Gate 28 on the third floor, staffed 07:00 to 22:00) and Terminal 2 (near Gates 225, 249 and 274 on the third floor, staffed 07:30 to 21:30), with unstaffed kiosks operating around the clock at major airports. Check current hours before travel.

All methods require customs clearance and export confirmation of the purchased items. For the airport route, build in extra time. If you pack the items in checked baggage, ask staff before you check the bag how you will present them to customs.

Store thresholds may differ from the official minimum

A Hani Seoul guide to Olive Young refunds says a purchase must exceed 30,000 won per receipt for an immediate refund, with the single transaction under 500,000 won, and says you must show a physical passport or a mobile passport QR code at checkout. These numbers differ from the VisitKorea figures, which suggests that stores may apply their own conditions or that guides reflect different dates. Ask at the register what minimum and per-receipt limit apply, and whether receipts can be combined. Hani Seoul says multiple receipts cannot be combined.

The same guide says products must remain unopened for the customs check, though it notes that using items during the trip is generally accepted in practice if they are carried out of Korea. VisitKorea's rule is stricter: opened or used items are explicitly ineligible. When in doubt, follow the stricter rule and keep the items you intend to claim sealed.

A shopping plan that protects your refund

  1. Bring your passport on every shopping day.
  2. Ask the cashier whether the purchase qualifies for an immediate refund before paying.
  3. Keep receipts and any vouchers together in one folder.
  4. Do not open items you plan to claim. Open only the ones you will use during the trip, and keep them on separate receipts if you can.
  5. Check the carry-on liquid rules before you pack. Incheon Airport says containers must be 100 ml or less, kept in one transparent 20 cm by 20 cm ziplock bag, with one bag per person.
  6. Arrive at the airport early enough to complete customs and the refund step before security.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Assuming the minimum purchase is the same in every store.
  • Forgetting the passport at checkout, which pushes you to the airport refund route.
  • Opening products before customs inspection.
  • Packing everything in checked baggage and arriving at customs with nothing to show.
  • Buying liquids in large sizes and discovering they cannot go in the cabin.
  • Counting on a fixed refund percentage. Guides quote different rates, so treat any percentage as an estimate.

Rules change, so confirm with the official bodies

Tax refund conditions, limits and counter hours are set by the Korean authorities and can change. This is general information, not tax or financial advice. Confirm current rules with VisitKorea, the store staff and the customs or airport information desks. For carry-on rules, check Incheon Airport's official notices or call its customer service.
