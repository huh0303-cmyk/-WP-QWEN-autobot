An international couple in Korea who decide to marry should treat the legal marriage and the immigration steps as two separate processes. According to the U.S. Embassy in Korea, marriage in Korea is a civil procedure completed at the local district office (Gu Cheong), where both partners bring their documents and two witnesses sign the application form. A foreign partner who then wants to live in Korea as the spouse of a Korean citizen applies separately for a marriage migrant (F-6) visa, whose conditions are described below.

Step 1: the marriage report at the district office

The U.S. Embassy's guidance for American citizens gives a concrete example of the sequence. The foreign partner prepares proof of citizenship and photo identification, plus an affidavit of eligibility for marriage stating that they are free to marry; the embassy notarises the affidavit. If the other spouse is a Korean national, the notarised affidavit must be translated into Korean, with the translator's signature and the date. The couple then take the documents to the district office, where two witnesses sign the Korean application form. The embassy states that a certificate of marriage registration is issued immediately when both spouses are U.S. citizens and takes three to five days when one spouse is Korean. It also states that same-sex marriages are not recognised in South Korea and that fiancés who are neither Korean nor American should contact their own embassy.

The exact documents depend on the foreign partner's nationality. A guide published by Seoulstart, a commercial site, describes the usual elements as a certificate of no impediment or equivalent proof of legal capacity from the home country, an apostille or consular legalisation, a certified Korean translation, a passport or registration card and the marriage report form. Treat that list as a prompt to ask the district office and your embassy what they require, since requirements are not identical for every country.

Step 2: the pre-marriage guidance programme, if it applies

The government's foreigner services portal on gov.kr describes a mandatory international marriage guidance programme for Korean nationals marrying citizens of designated countries: China, Vietnam, the Philippines, Cambodia, Mongolia, Uzbekistan and Thailand. It involves registering online, completing a four-hour course and obtaining a completion certificate valid for five years. The certificate is required when applying for the F-6 visa at a Korean embassy or consulate. The page lists exemptions, such as a couple who maintained a relationship abroad for six months or more on long-term visas, a foreign partner who legally stayed in Korea for 91 days or more during the relationship, or a pregnancy, a child with the Korean national or humanitarian circumstances. Whether it applies to your couple depends on nationality and history, so check the portal.

Step 3: the F-6 marriage migrant visa

A law-firm summary of the F-6 visa lists three categories: spouses of Korean nationals (F-6-1), parents raising a minor child from a marriage with a Korean citizen (F-6-2), and people who cannot maintain the marriage because of a spouse's death or disappearance (F-6-3). It says the marriage must be genuine and voluntary, and that authorities examine the relationship and the background to the marriage. Other conditions it lists include:

  • Income: the Korean sponsor must meet an annual income threshold that depends on household size; the summary notes that 5% of assets held for six months or more can count as income. The threshold is revised, so confirm the current figure with the Korea Immigration Service or a Korean embassy.
  • Housing: a normal living space owned or leased by the sponsor or immediate family, with an exception for company-provided housing.
  • Communication: basic Korean proficiency (TOPIK Level 1 or more) or equivalent language ability, unless the couple has children.
  • Documents: a marriage certificate, invitation letter, income proof, employment certificate, property documents, health diagnosis, identity documents and proof of communication.
  • Reapplication: a six-month waiting period after a rejection, unless pregnancy or childbirth circumstances apply.

That summary is from a private law firm, so use it to organise questions rather than as the rule.

Step 4: registering after arrival and joining health insurance

A guidebook for married immigrants hosted on 129.go.kr says that after entering Korea, individuals must register as aliens within 90 days, and that married immigrants can enrol in the National Health Insurance system after registration, with spouses of employed people able to register as dependents. The guidebook is an older publication, so confirm current rules with HiKorea and the National Health Insurance Service. Local government pages also give 90 days from entry for foreigner registration and tell residents to reserve a visit through HiKorea.

Where couples can get support

The Danuri portal, operated by the Korea Institute for Healthy Family, offers information in 13 languages, a helpline at 1577-1366 and a locator for multicultural family support centers. The guidebook on 129.go.kr lists the Transnational Marriage and Family-Support Center helpline as 1577-5432 and the Health and Welfare Call Center as 129. Phone numbers can change, so confirm them on the portals before calling.

A checklist before you commit to dates

  1. Ask the foreign partner's embassy what proof of legal capacity to marry it issues.
  2. Ask the district office which documents, translations and witnesses it requires.
  3. Check whether the guidance programme applies, using the gov.kr page.
  4. Check the F-6 conditions for income, housing and language before arranging the move.
  5. Plan alien registration within 90 days of entry and reserve a visit on HiKorea.
  6. Look up the support helpline for your language.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Assuming a ceremony is the legal marriage. The district office registration is the civil procedure the embassy describes.
  • Assuming the marriage creates the visa. The F-6 visa is a separate application.
  • Using one country's document list for another. The U.S. example does not apply to every nationality.
  • Skipping the guidance programme check. It is a condition of F-6 for couples from the listed countries.
  • Relying on older guides. Income thresholds and phone numbers change.

Rules change: confirm with the official bodies

This article is general information and not legal or immigration advice. Marriage registration, visa conditions, income thresholds and exemptions are set by Korean authorities, including the Ministry of Justice and local governments, and embassies, and they change. Confirm your own situation with the district office, your embassy, HiKorea or the Korean embassy or consulate that will handle your visa, and consult a qualified immigration lawyer if your case is complicated.
