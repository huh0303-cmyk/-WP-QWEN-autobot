The Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (MFDS) regulates cosmetics in Korea, but it does not treat all products the same way. Functional cosmetics, which make specific claims such as whitening, anti-wrinkle or sun protection, need a review or a report before sale. General cosmetics are covered by ingredient lists, labeling rules and seller registration. Knowing the difference helps you read a label and understand what an "MFDS" claim does and does not tell you.

Who the regulator is and what it oversees

According to a regulatory guide from CIRS Group, the MFDS oversees the full chain of cosmetics regulation, including ingredient management, approval and filing of functional cosmetics, and label and advertising review. It says Korea's Cosmetics Act defines two categories, general cosmetics and functional cosmetics, and defines cosmetics as items applied to the body for cleaning, beautifying or maintaining skin and hair, with only mild effects on the human body.

The same guide says businesses that make or distribute cosmetics must register, and that the responsible seller must designate a responsible sales manager with the statutory qualifications. The MFDS English site lists related texts, including the Enforcement Rule of the Cosmetics Act, a guideline on assessment of new cosmetic substances, and regulations on labeling and advertisement.

What counts as a functional cosmetic

ChemLinked, a regulatory information service, says the MFDS designates eleven functional cosmetic types. They include whitening (two kinds), anti-wrinkle, self-tanning, sunscreen, hair dyeing, hair removal, hair loss relief, acne care (limited to rinse-off cleansing products), skin barrier restoration to relieve itching, and stretch mark appearance.

ChemLinked also explains the two pathways. Full evaluation is needed when a product uses a new functional ingredient not on the MFDS notification lists or deviates from established standards, with safety and efficacy data submitted to the National Institute of Food and Drug Safety Evaluation. Simplified reporting is available when a product uses only notified ingredients at specified concentrations, or repeats a formulation already evaluated for the same company.

Ingredient controls

ChemLinked's ingredient overview describes five lists: prohibited ingredients (it says over 1,000 substances), restricted preservatives, restricted hair dye ingredients, restricted UV filters, and other restricted ingredients. A positive list covers color additives and where they may be used, for example not in the eye area or only in rinse-off products.

It also notes that fragrance allergens must be individually listed if above 0.01 percent in rinse-off products or 0.001 percent in leave-on products, that animal testing has been prohibited since February 4, 2017, and that microbeads have been banned since July 2017 with sales banned from July 2018. These are summaries by a third party, so use the MFDS notices for the exact legal text.

Labeling you can check in a shop

ChemLinked says functional cosmetics must carry the Korean designation 기능성화장품 or an official MFDS logo, along with the approved efficacy claims and usage instructions. For hair loss, acne, barrier-restoration and stretch-mark products, it says a disclaimer must state that the product is not a medicine for preventing or treating disease.

When you are choosing a product:

  1. Look for the 기능성화장품 mark if the product claims whitening, wrinkle care or sun protection.
  2. Read the exact claim wording. A functional claim covers only the approved efficacy.
  3. Check the full ingredient list against anything you know you react to.
  4. For a medical skin problem, treat the cosmetic claim as non-medical and ask a doctor.

Where to read the official texts

The MFDS English page for cosmetics regulations lists a booklet titled Cosmetic Regulatory Framework in Korea, the Enforcement Rule of the Cosmetics Act, a guideline for risk assessment of cosmetic products, a guideline on assessment of new cosmetic substances, standards for cosmetic color additives, and regulations on demonstrating labeling and advertisement claims. The page shows these as English documents, with publication dates between 2016 and 2020, so check whether newer Korean-language versions exist before relying on a figure.

For a consumer, the labeling and advertisement regulation is the most useful of these because it governs what a product may claim. If you are a seller or importer, the registration and responsible-seller rules described by CIRS apply to you before the product reaches customers.

What "MFDS" does not guarantee

The sources describe a system of rules, reviews and filings, not a promise that a product will suit your skin. A product can meet every requirement and still irritate you. Regulation also does not make marketing words such as "clean" or "gentle" into verified claims. Patch testing a new product on a small area is a sensible habit, and a dermatologist should assess any reaction that persists.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Assuming every cosmetic is individually approved by the MFDS. The functional category is the one with review or reporting.
  • Treating a functional claim as a medical claim.
  • Ignoring the ingredient list because the front label looks reassuring.
  • Relying on a blog summary for exact limits instead of the MFDS notice.

Rules change: confirm with the official body

This is general information, not medical or legal advice. Cosmetics rules are revised by the MFDS, and the figures above come from secondary summaries. Confirm current requirements on the MFDS website, and talk to a healthcare professional about personal skin concerns.
