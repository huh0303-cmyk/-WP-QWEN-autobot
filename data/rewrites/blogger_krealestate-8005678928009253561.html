To protect a rental deposit in Korea, do three things on the way in and two on the way out. On the way in, record the unit's condition in writing and photos, report your address, and get the lease a fixed date (확정일자). On the way out, give notice on time and, if the deposit is not returned, apply for a tenancy registration order (임차권등기명령) before you move. Most disputes come down to proof, so the paperwork you build on day one decides how strong your position is later.

Record the unit's condition before you unpack

Deductions at move-out usually turn on what was already damaged. According to the Seoulstart deposit-return guide, landlords may deduct for damage beyond normal wear, such as holes, broken appliances, significant stains or missing fixtures, but not for natural wear and tear, pre-existing damage documented at move-in, or structural repairs. That makes your move-in record the key document.

  • Walk through every room with the landlord or their representative and check the unit against the contract, including the items the contract says are included.
  • Photograph walls, floors, windows, fixtures, appliances and the bathroom, with dated files.
  • List defects in writing, such as scratches or wallpaper stains, and send the list to the landlord by message so there is a timestamp.
  • Read any special clauses (특약). Seoulstart notes that a special restoration clause can shift wear costs onto the tenant.

Protect the deposit itself: address report and fixed date

KoreaSteps describes three layers of protection. The first is opposing power (대항력), which starts when you physically move in and report your address, and takes effect from the following day. For foreign residents it says the immigration address report substitutes for the resident registration filing, and it gives the deadline as within 14 to 15 days of moving. Confirm the current deadline with immigration or 1345, the multilingual information center it lists.

The second layer is the confirmed date stamp (확정일자) on the lease. KoreaSteps says it can be obtained at a community center (주민센터), a court, or online, and that it gives priority repayment over junior creditors if the home is auctioned. Do it in the same week you move in.

The third is small-deposit priority, which applies only to deposits below limits set by regional rules. Check whether your deposit qualifies rather than assuming so.

Check the landlord and property before signing

KoreaSteps says the landlord should disclose any fixed dates, rents and deposits already recorded for the home, and tax payment certificates, since unpaid taxes can rank ahead of a deposit. Foreign tenants can also request a foreign resident certificate showing earlier tenants' filing dates. If anything does not match what you were told, ask before paying.

Giving notice and moving out

Seoulstart lists the standard sequence: written notice at least two months before the lease ends (for leases signed or renewed after December 10, 2020), move out on the agreed date and return keys and access cards, let the landlord inspect, and receive the deposit. It states the landlord must return the deposit on the day the lease ends and you vacate, and cannot hold it back pending a new tenant's payment. It also says that if notice is missed, the lease renews for two years on the same terms, and that ending a renewed lease needs three months' notice.

Before the final inspection:

  1. Clean the unit and photograph it again, date-stamped.
  2. Compare your photos with the move-in set.
  3. Read any deduction the landlord proposes against the wear-and-tear rules above.
  4. Get any agreed deduction in writing before you hand over keys.

If the deposit is not returned

Seoulstart suggests this order: send a certified demand letter (내용증명) through Korea Post, file a tenancy registration order at the district court, then pursue small claims or a civil suit, and trigger any deposit insurance if you hold a guarantee. It says Korea Legal Aid Corporation (대한법률구조공단) offers free consultation.

The Supreme Court of Korea's public information page explains why the registration order matters: without one, moving out risks losing opposing power and priority repayment rights. Applying with the lease date, deposit amount, address-report date, move-in date and a resident record preserves those rights even after you move to a new address, and no landlord consent is needed. The page stresses that you must confirm the registration is complete before moving out. Seoulstart lists the filing cost as 43,400 won in the simple case of one landlord, one tenant and one property, and quotes delay interest of 5 percent annually until a lawsuit is filed, rising to 12 percent from the day after service of the complaint. Verify both at the court.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Moving in without a written list of existing damage.
  • Waiting weeks to report your address or get the fixed date.
  • Missing the notice window, which can renew the lease automatically.
  • Moving out before the tenancy registration is confirmed complete.
  • Signing special clauses without understanding who pays for wear.

Rules change: confirm with the official body

This is general information, not legal advice. Housing lease rules, deadlines, fees and interest rates can change, and your contract may contain special terms. Confirm details with the court, your local community center, immigration (1345), or Korea Legal Aid Corporation before acting.
