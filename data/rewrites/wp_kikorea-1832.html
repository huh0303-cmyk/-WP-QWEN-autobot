A foreign investor who wants to set up a company in Korea should handle five things in order: confirm the investment counts as foreign direct investment, notify KOTRA or a foreign exchange bank, bring the money in, register the company and business, and complete the foreign-invested company registration. According to Invest KOREA, the official process takes about two weeks once documents are ready, so most delays come from missing paperwork rather than from the procedure itself.

Priority 1: Check that your investment qualifies as foreign direct investment

The label matters because the incentives and the registration route attach to it. Invest KOREA's investment guide describes foreign direct investment as an investment of KRW 100 million or more together with acquiring 10% or more of the voting shares. The same guide says long-term loans from a foreign investor to a Korean subsidiary can also count, if the loan has a maturity of at least five years.

Before you plan anything else, write down three numbers: the amount, the percentage of voting shares you will hold, and the source of funds. These feed directly into the notification form in the next step.

Priority 2: Notify first, then remit the funds

Invest KOREA lists the notification of foreign direct investment as step one. It is made to KOTRA (through its Foreign Investor Support Center) or to a foreign exchange bank, and it is described as a pre-notification, so it comes before the money moves.

The documents named on the Invest KOREA procedure page are:

  • The notification form, two copies
  • A certificate of nationality
  • Proof of the source of the investment funds

Step two is the remittance of the funds through a foreign exchange bank (or customs, if currency is carried in by hand). Invest KOREA gives a timeline of two to three days. For companies with capital under KRW 1 billion, the page says a bank account balance certificate may be used instead of proof of full payment.

Priority 3: Register the company and the business

The next steps run through three different offices, and each one needs the documents produced by the one before:

  1. Court registry office: registration of incorporation, estimated at 2-3 days. Application forms are available through the Supreme Court's online registry (iros.go.kr).
  2. Permits, if needed: some businesses need authorisation from a district office, health centre or the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety. Invest KOREA says this applies where it is deemed necessary for the business, and timing varies by business type.
  3. Tax office: notification of incorporation and business registration, about 3 days. Invest KOREA lists the application form, articles of incorporation, lease contract, incorporation certificate, shareholder list and the representative's passport.

Notice that the tax-office step asks for a lease contract. That means a business address has to be settled before you get this far, not after.

Priority 4: Open the corporate account and finish the foreign-invested company registration

Invest KOREA lists the opening of a corporate bank account at a foreign exchange bank as step six and notes a restriction: opening an additional account at another bank is restricted for 20 business days. If you plan to bank with more than one institution, factor that wait into your schedule.

The final step is the registration of the foreign-invested company with KOTRA or at the location where the original notification was made. The page says this must happen within 60 days after the investment payment. It also states that a residence visa is issued after the company registration, so the founder's visa timeline depends on this step.

Priority 5: Understand what kind of taxpayer the new company becomes

Invest KOREA's taxation guide explains that foreign corporations pay corporate tax only on domestic-source income, while foreign-invested domestic corporations are taxed on worldwide income like other Korean companies. The same guide gives the corporate income tax brackets: 9% on income up to KRW 200 million, with higher rates in the brackets above that.

Whether a company qualifies for any reduction depends on its industry, location and size, so treat any incentive as something to confirm rather than assume. The investment guide notes, for example, that tax-reduction periods for imported capital goods apply to foreign-invested companies in designated sectors.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Sending money before the notification is filed. The procedure lists notification first.
  • Assuming the investment qualifies without checking the KRW 100 million and 10% voting share thresholds.
  • Leaving the lease for last, even though the tax office asks for the lease contract.
  • Forgetting the 60-day deadline for the foreign-invested company registration.
  • Planning a second bank account within the first 20 business days.
  • Counting on a visa before the company registration is finished.

Ask KOTRA's Foreign Investor Support Center which documents they need in your specific case, since the lists above are the general ones.

Rules change: confirm with the official bodies

This article is general information, not legal, tax or financial advice. Thresholds, document lists, deadlines and incentives are revised from time to time. Before you file anything, confirm the current requirements with Invest KOREA/KOTRA, your foreign exchange bank and the National Tax Service, and consider consulting a licensed lawyer or tax accountant for your situation.
