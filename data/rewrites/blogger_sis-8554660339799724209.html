TOPIK is the Test of Proficiency in Korean, and your result opens or closes several doors: university admission, some part-time work permissions for students, and points in certain immigration and employment criteria. The exam has two main versions. TOPIK I measures levels 1 and 2, and TOPIK II measures levels 3 to 6. This article explains how each version is built, what the level cut-offs are, how long a result stays valid, and where a score is actually used.

TOPIK I and TOPIK II: what is tested

The National Institute for International Education (NIIED) states that TOPIK I consists of Listening with 30 items and Reading with 40 items, while TOPIK II consists of Listening with 50 items, Reading with 50 items and Writing with 4 tasks. Both can be taken on paper or on the internet-based format. NIIED also lists a separate Speaking test of six tasks over 30 minutes.

The Wikipedia article on the exam adds timing details. For TOPIK I it gives 40 minutes for Listening and 60 minutes for Reading, with 100 points for each section and 200 in total. For TOPIK II it gives 60 minutes for Listening, 50 minutes for Writing and 70 minutes for Reading, with 300 points in total. Because Wikipedia is a secondary source, check the timing in the official exam notice for your session before test day.

The practical difference is the Writing section. TOPIK I has none, so a beginner can sit the test without producing written answers. TOPIK II requires four writing tasks, so learners aiming for level 3 or above need to practise writing by hand and under time pressure.

How scores become levels

According to the Wikipedia summary, the cut-off total scores are:

LevelVersionMinimum total score
1TOPIK I80
2TOPIK I140
3TOPIK II120
4TOPIK II150
5TOPIK II190
6TOPIK II230

Notice that the score scales differ. TOPIK I is marked out of 200 and TOPIK II out of 300, so a total of 140 is level 2 on TOPIK I but would be level 3 on TOPIK II, going by the cut-offs above. Check which version you sat before you read a score sheet.

How long a result lasts

Wikipedia states that results are valid for two years after the announcement of results. The Study in Korea portal repeats the two-year figure in its table on Korean proficiency standards for employment, noting that TOPIK scores are valid for two years as indicated on the certificate. The practical consequence is timing: a certificate that is valid when you take the exam may have expired by the time a later application is reviewed, so plan the test date around your deadlines.

Where a TOPIK level is used

NIIED lists several uses: university admission and graduation requirements, employment applications at home and abroad, qualification for permanent residency and employment visas, and eligibility for the Global Korea Scholarship. The government services portal for foreigners states that students generally need level 3 or higher for admission to a college or university and level 4 or higher for graduation.

Study in Korea adds two other areas where a level matters:

  • Part-time work for students. The portal's table of Korean proficiency standards ties the permitted weekly hours to the student's programme and Korean level. For example, a language-training student can apply six months after the qualification date with TOPIK level 2, and third- and fourth-year undergraduates are listed with TOPIK level 4. The conditions are detailed, so read the full table and ask your university's international office before you take any job.
  • Employment and immigration criteria. The portal reproduces a Ministry of Justice standard that gives 20 points for TOPIK level 5 or higher, 10 points for level 3 or higher and 5 points for level 2 or higher, with an additional route through the Social Integration Programme.

When to sit the exam

The portal lists paper-based sessions in January, April, May, July, October and November, internet-based sessions in February, March, June, September, October and November, and speaking sessions in March, June and October. Registration deadlines and exact dates are published by the organiser for each session, so check the official TOPIK pages as soon as you decide on a target session.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Comparing scores across versions. Levels 1 and 2 come from TOPIK I and levels 3 to 6 from TOPIK II.
  • Ignoring Writing. A learner with good listening and reading scores can still miss a cut-off if the writing tasks are left blank.
  • Letting the result expire. Count two years from the announcement date, not from the test date.
  • Treating level 3 as universal. The level 3 and level 4 figures are general, and the portal says requirements vary by university and programme.
  • Using unofficial score tables. Cut-offs and formats can be revised; confirm them on the official exam pages.

Rules change: confirm with the official bodies

This is general information, not legal, immigration or academic advice. Exam formats, cut-offs, validity rules and the standards used for part-time work or immigration points are set by NIIED, the Ministry of Justice and individual universities, and they can be revised. Confirm the current rules with NIIED, the TOPIK organiser, your university and the immigration authorities before you rely on a figure from this page.
