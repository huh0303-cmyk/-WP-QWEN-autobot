Korea groups its professional work visas into the E-1 to E-7 series, and the right one depends on what you will actually do at work. Most foreign professionals who are not teachers, researchers or artists end up looking at E-7, which covers special occupations, while recent graduates often start on D-10, a job-seeker status that is not a work visa.

The E-series at a glance

The Korea Immigration Service's Visa Navigator lists the professional employment statuses as follows. Holders may work within the occupational scope of their own status.

StatusCategory (as listed in the guide)
E-1Professor, teaching at institutions of higher education
E-2Foreign language instructor, language conversation instruction
E-3Researcher, natural science and high-tech research
E-4Technical instructor, specialized industrial instruction
E-5Professional in fields such as law, accounting and medicine
E-6Arts and entertainment, artistic or sports performance activities
E-7Special occupation: professional, semi-professional or skilled worker

The guide gives a stay period for each status and describes each as extendable. Check the current period for your own application on HiKorea.

Working out which category fits

Start from the duties in the job offer, not from the job title. A language teacher at a private institute and a software developer at a startup sit in different categories even if both are "foreign staff". If your role does not clearly match E-1 to E-6, E-7 is the group that covers professional, semi-professional and skilled roles. Ask the employer to confirm with the immigration office that the specific job qualifies before you resign from anything.

If you are a graduate: the D-10 step

The Visa Navigator describes D-10 as a job-seeker status with general job seekers, tech start-up preparers and high-tech internship participants. The period of stay is listed as six months, or one year for the high-tech internship, and is extendable. The guide says D-10 holders may do internship activities in fields that fit E-1 to E-7 status, and that part-time work is available only to graduates who meet specific criteria, including TOPIK level 4 or higher.

Ajou University's international office explains the change from D-2 to D-10: apply before the D-2 expires, since it is valid for up to 30 days after graduation. Its document list includes the application form, passport and alien registration card, certificate of (expected) graduation, transcript, a job-seeking plan in Korean or English, and proof of residence and living expenses. The office lists a fee of KRW 130,000 and says D-10 does not allow work automatically, so separate immigration permission is needed. Other universities may ask for slightly different documents.

From D-10 to E-7: what changes

According to the Visa Navigator, moving from D-10 to a professional employment status requires meeting the employment criteria for the specific occupation. In practice that means a signed job offer that matches an eligible occupation, plus the documents the immigration office asks for. Korea.net reports that the K-Work platform, run by the Ministry of SMEs and Startups and the Korea SMEs and Startups Agency, includes one-stop help converting a D-2 or D-10 visa to an E-7-1 work visa.

Longer-term: F-2 and F-5

The Visa Navigator also describes routes to longer residence. It says a professional employment status can lead to F-2 resident status through the point-based system or through five or more years of continuous residence that meets conduct and livelihood standards. F-2 holders have partial restrictions on employment depending on the subcategory. For F-5 permanent residence, the guide lists five years of F-2 residence, or three years for point-based F-2, together with completion of Social Integration Program level 5 or higher. These are routes to think about once you are settled, and the conditions are detailed on HiKorea.

Questions to ask the employer before signing

  1. Which visa category will you apply under, and has the company hired on that category before?
  2. Who prepares and submits the immigration documents, and who pays the costs?
  3. Does the job description match the duties allowed under that category?
  4. When is the earliest start date permitted by immigration?

Rules that apply after you start

  • The Visa Navigator says an E-7 holder must obtain permission in advance to change or add a workplace within the visa's scope.
  • The Seoul Immigration Office's guide says foreigners who work without authorization, including unauthorized part-time work, face a penalty of up to 30 million won or deportation.
  • The same guide says employers must report a foreign employee's resignation or contract changes within 15 days, and registered foreigners must report a new address within 15 days.
  • Foreigners staying more than 90 days must register within 90 days of arrival, per the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

Common mistakes to verify

  • Starting work on the strength of a job offer before the correct visa or permission is in place.
  • Assuming D-10 is a work visa.
  • Choosing a category from the job title instead of the actual duties.
  • Taking a second job without permission.
  • Using an old checklist. The Visa Navigator is an older edition, so confirm current requirements on HiKorea.

Rules change: confirm with the official body

This article is general information, not legal or immigration advice. Visa categories, occupation lists, fees and document requirements are revised from time to time. Confirm current rules with the Korea Immigration Service (HiKorea or the 1345 Immigration Contact Center) and with the Korean embassy or consulate in your home country, and consult a qualified immigration professional for your own case.
