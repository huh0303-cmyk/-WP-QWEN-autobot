Your first month in Korea depends on how long you stay. Visitors mainly need their entry paperwork in order, while anyone staying more than 90 days must register with immigration and report their address. According to KPMG, citizens of 24 countries are exempt from applying for a K-ETA through the end of this year, but all travelers not required to apply for a K-ETA must submit an e-Arrival card online within three days before arrival. This checklist splits the tasks by traveler, worker and student.

Before you land: entry paperwork

KPMG reports that the K-ETA exemption runs from January 1 to December 31, and covers citizens of 24 countries including the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Japan, Australia and most western European states. Exempt travelers can still apply voluntarily for benefits such as exemption from the e-Arrival card, for a non-refundable fee. KPMG says the e-Arrival card replaced the paper card on January 1 and must be submitted within three days, counting the day of arrival, before arrival. Because the exemption is temporary and your nationality may not be on the list, check your own requirements on the official K-ETA site before you book.

  • Confirm your visa or K-ETA requirement for your passport.
  • Submit the e-Arrival card if it applies to you.
  • Carry documents that match your purpose: school admission papers, an employment contract, or a return ticket.

Week one: the 90-day clock

The Incheon city guide says a foreigner staying more than 90 days must register within 90 days of entry. The registration documents it lists are your passport, the foreigner registration form and a 3.5 by 4.5 cm color photo, plus any extra papers your visa type requires. The residence card fee listed is 30,000 won. Visitors staying under 90 days do not need to register.

Do not leave this to the last week. Workers should ask their employer's HR team to help, and students should ask the international office, which often knows the local process.

Weeks one to two: your address

Registered foreigners must report a change of address within 14 days, according to both the Incheon guide and the Seoul Jung-gu page. Jung-gu lists the local gu office, the immigration office, a dong community center and HiKorea as places to report, and says you need the application, your passport and proof of address such as a rental agreement. Keep your lease from day one.

Weeks two to four: health insurance and phone numbers

Insurance timing depends on your status. The Seoulstart guide says employees are enrolled through their workplace and that long-stay residents are subject to mandatory enrollment after six months. It lists D-2 students, F-5, F-6 and E-9 holders among those enrolled without waiting. Sungkyunkwan University's international office adds that D-2 students are enrolled at alien registration and recommends personal insurance for the gap before registration is processed, which it says can take 6 to 8 weeks. Language students on a D-4 visa are listed there as enrolled six months after arrival.

Save these numbers: NHIS at 1577-1000 (extension 6 for English, Chinese, Uzbek or Vietnamese) and the immigration hotline 1345.

If you are staying less than 90 days

The registration duty described by Incheon applies to stays longer than 90 days. If you are a short-term visitor, your first month is mostly about your entry status and your permitted stay. Keep the passport page with your entry stamp, note your permitted stay length, and ask the immigration office before it ends if your plans change. Do not assume you can extend a visit automatically; confirm the options and fees on HiKorea or by calling 1345.

Keep a documents folder

Almost every step above asks for the same few papers: passport, photo, proof of address and proof of your purpose of stay. Keep paper and phone copies of each from day one, including your lease, employment contract or school admission letter, and any receipts from the immigration office. A single folder saves time when an office asks for a document you submitted weeks earlier.

A simple checklist by purpose

  1. Visitors: check K-ETA status, submit the e-Arrival card, keep your return ticket and leave before your permitted stay ends.
  2. Workers: register with immigration within 90 days, give HR your address, and confirm with them that workplace insurance has started.
  3. Students: register with immigration, report your housing address, and ask your school what insurance covers you from arrival.
  4. Everyone staying longer than 90 days: report any move within 14 days.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Assuming the K-ETA exemption is permanent. KPMG describes it as temporary and dated.
  • Missing the e-Arrival card. It is a separate online step.
  • Counting the 90 days from the wrong date. The Incheon guide counts from entry.
  • Not telling immigration about a move. The 14-day window includes weekends and holidays.
  • Buying insurance twice. Ask your school or employer before buying a private plan.

    • Rules change: confirm with the official bodies

Confirm current rules with the Korea Immigration Service through HiKorea or the 1345 hotline, the official K-ETA website and NHIS before you travel or act.
