The organisation named on the document in front of you tells you who to contact. A notice from the National Health Insurance Service (NHIS) is about the compulsory public scheme and goes to the NHIS. A policy, certificate or renewal letter from an insurance company is a private contract and goes to that insurer, with the Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) as the official body for complaints and disputes about financial products. Mixing them up is the most common reason an insurance question stays unanswered.

What the NHIS is, and what it handles

The NHIS English site describes Korea's health insurance as a system run by a single insurer that provides health insurance to all citizens living in Korea. Contributions are paid monthly and are set according to the level of household income and property, and they are subject to compulsory collection. For foreign residents, the NHIS guidance page explains enrollment, how premiums are calculated, reductions for certain visa holders, and dependents.

So questions about the following belong with the NHIS:

  • whether you are enrolled and under which category;
  • a contribution notice or a change in the amount;
  • adding or removing a dependent;
  • reductions for students or other groups;
  • the date your membership started or ended.

The NHIS lists a main call center at 1577-1000 and an English consultant line at 033-811-2000.

What a private insurer handles

A private policy is a contract between you and the insurance company. The policy terms (약관) define what is covered, what is excluded and how renewal works. Questions about a policy's benefit, a claim under the policy, a renewal notice or a premium change on that policy go to the insurer first. The NHIS cannot explain private contract terms, and the insurer cannot change your public insurance contribution.

Read the document before you call

  1. Find the issuer: look for the organisation's name and logo in the heading, plus the document date.
  2. Identify the subject: a contribution notice, membership information, a hospital bill or a private policy.
  3. Note reference numbers: the notice number, policy number or claim number.
  4. Write one specific question: for example, "Why did the amount on this notice change?" instead of "Is my insurance okay?"
  5. Prepare identification: have your alien registration card or passport ready, since both organisations will need to confirm who you are.

When a hospital bill is the issue, ask the hospital's billing desk to explain which part was charged to you and which part was handled through the public scheme. The NHIS English page notes, as an example of the system's rules, that a patient who seeks care outside the referral guidelines must pay the entire care expense. That example is a reminder that the bill itself can explain a cost, and the NHIS can confirm the rule that applied.

Your employer is a third contact point

For employees, the NHIS guidance page says the monthly contribution is the monthly wage multiplied by the contribution rate and split equally between employee and employer. That means a question such as "why is this amount deducted from my salary?" often starts with your employer's payroll or HR team, who can tell you which wage figure was reported. If the answer does not match the NHIS notice, bring both documents to the NHIS. Self-employed foreigners are assessed through a contribution score method, the same as Korean residents, so their notice will look different from a payslip deduction, and their questions go straight to the NHIS.

Where to go when the answer is not satisfactory

For private insurance, the FSS says it handles complaints and disputes about banking, insurance, financial investment, credit cards, savings banks, foreign exchange and unfair financial practices. Its website offers online complaint and consultation forms, a dispute mediation inquiry system and a Financial Dispute Mediation Committee that processes formal dispute cases, and it publishes settlement standards and case examples. The FSS page also describes a consultation line, 서민금융1332.

Start with the insurer's complaint channel, keep written records of the answer, and take the matter to the FSS if it is not resolved. Financial News, quoting the FSS, reports that a policyholder can withdraw an application within 30 days, but not after 15 days from receiving the insurance certificate, and that a contract may be cancelled within three months in cases of incomplete sales such as inadequate explanation of key terms. That article is from several years ago, so ask the FSS or the insurer for the current rule.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Calling the NHIS about a private policy, or the insurer about an NHIS notice.
  • Asking a broad question instead of a specific one tied to a document.
  • Throwing away envelopes and letters that show dates and reference numbers.
  • Assuming a phone answer is final. Ask for it in writing or by message when it matters.
  • Waiting too long. Withdrawal and cancellation rights for private policies have time limits.
  • Treating a general article, including this one, as a decision on your case.
