The best TOPIK online class is the one that matches the section structure of the test you are taking: TOPIK I (levels 1 and 2) tests reading and listening, while TOPIK II (levels 3 to 6) adds writing. Before paying for any course, decide which test you need, check how many practice questions and timed sections the class offers, and compare it against free options such as the Online King Sejong Institute, which describes itself as a free online Korean learning service.

Know which test you are preparing for

According to Wikipedia's summary of the exam, TOPIK I has two sections, reading (60 minutes, 40 questions) and listening (40 minutes, 30 questions), for a total of 200 points, all multiple-choice. TOPIK II has three sections: listening (60 minutes, 50 questions), writing (50 minutes, 4 short-answer questions) and reading (70 minutes, 50 questions), for a total of 300 points.

The same source gives the level cut-offs as 80 points for level 1 and 140 for level 2 on TOPIK I, and 120, 150, 190 and 230 points for levels 3 to 6 on TOPIK II. It says results are valid for two years after the announcement. Korea.net adds that the exam also has a separate speaking test, and its report on the 2026 plan counts six paper, six online and three speaking sessions.

The practical consequence: a class for a TOPIK II target must include writing practice with feedback, and any class aimed at TOPIK I can safely skip it. A single "TOPIK course" label does not tell you which of these it covers.

Check the format: paper or internet-based

Korea.net reports that the 2026 schedule has six paper-based sessions and six internet-based sessions, with online testing countries growing from 13 to 17, newly including Nepal, Laos, Bahrain and India. The Korea Times reports that the national exam is moving toward an AI-driven digital platform, with a pilot in 2028 and a digital launch in 2029, and that a home test for lower-level exams is planned. Those are plans, not current options, so do not choose a class on the assumption that you can take the test at home.

What matters now is the format of your actual test date. If you will sit the internet-based version, practise typing your answers on screen. If you will sit the paper version, practise handwriting the writing section within the time limit. Ask any provider whether its mock tests follow the format you will use.

Free options to try first

The Online King Sejong Institute describes three course formats: Lecture Assisted, Learning Assisted and Self-learning, plus K-Culture courses. It says the service is free, aimed at any foreign learner of Korean, runs on PC and mobile, and offers levels from introductory to advanced with multilingual lectures. It also mentions support from an online teacher even for independent learners. Its page does not state that it is a TOPIK exam-preparation programme, so treat it as a base for vocabulary and grammar, then add timed practice on past papers.

A short trial of a free course is a cheap way to find out how many hours a week you can actually study before committing to a paid class.

Questions to ask before enrolling in a paid class

  1. Which TOPIK level and test does the class target? Look for a clear TOPIK I or TOPIK II label and a level range.
  2. Is writing marked by a person? For TOPIK II, a class without feedback on written answers leaves a section of 100 points unchecked.
  3. Are there full timed mock tests? The section lengths above are the benchmark: 60 and 70 minutes for listening and reading on TOPIK II.
  4. What is the refund or trial policy? Get it in writing before paying.
  5. Does the schedule fit your time zone and exam date? Count back from your test session.
  6. Who teaches? Ask for the teacher's background instead of relying on a testimonial.

Build a weekly routine around the test

Whichever class you choose, keep the structure simple. Split your week between new material (grammar and vocabulary), listening practice, reading under time pressure and, for TOPIK II, one written answer reviewed by someone else. Take one full timed practice test every few weeks and log your score per section so you can see where you lose points. Because the level cut-offs above are based on total points, a weak section can be offset by strong ones, but only if you know which section is costing you points.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Buying a class before knowing the target level. Check what your university or scholarship asks for first.
  • Studying only by listening to lectures. The test is a timed exam, so passive study is not enough.
  • Ignoring score validity. Results are valid for two years after the announcement, so time the exam to your application.
  • Relying on old exam dates. Dates and overseas registration can differ by country or test centre.

Rules change: confirm with the official body

This article is general information, not legal or immigration advice, and it does not guarantee any exam result. Test dates, fees, formats and the way institutions use TOPIK scores can change. Confirm the schedule and registration on the official TOPIK website (topik.go.kr) or with your local test centre, and confirm the language requirement with the university, scholarship or visa office that will receive your score.
