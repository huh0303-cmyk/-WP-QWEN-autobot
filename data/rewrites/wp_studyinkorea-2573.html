MBA scholarships in Korea come mainly from the business schools themselves, and they differ a lot: some are awarded automatically at admission, some need a separate application, and most come with conditions you must meet to keep them. The government-funded Global Korea Scholarship (GKS) is a separate route. Before you choose a school, read its scholarship page for three things: how the award is decided, what percentage or items it covers, and what grade or work conditions apply afterwards.

Two routes: school awards and government funding

School scholarships are decided by the business school, usually when you are admitted. The GKS is run through Korean embassies and participating universities. SKK GSB's financial aid page lists the GKS as covering full tuition, a monthly living allowance, airfare and Korean language training for international students the school nominates, and it requires completion on the Korea campus. KAIST's MBA scholarship page describes the GKS in similar terms: full coverage of graduate and language-training tuition plus airfare, a monthly allowance and settlement fees, with one year of Korean language study followed by two years of master's study, applied for through KAIST or the Korean embassy in your home country.

Because the two routes are separate, check each school's page for how you would be nominated and whether you must apply to the school first.

Automatic award versus application-based award

This difference is easy to miss. According to SKK GSB, the SKK GSB Scholarship needs no separate application and is based on a review of your overall admissions performance, while the Future Leader Scholarship requires an application describing your business experience. Its four categories are Top Talent, Women Leaders, Family Business and Entrepreneurs, and Career-Break Professionals, and the Top Talent category needs a recommendation letter from a professor, CEO, HR head or team leader.

Yonsei says its scholarship decisions are made at the time of admission notification, and that need-based YSB Fellowships may require financial proof. If a school asks for extra documents for a fellowship, collect them when you assemble the main application rather than after you receive an offer.

Conditions for keeping the money

The same pages show that an award is rarely unconditional.

  • KAIST: recipients must keep a GPA above 3.3 on a 4.3 scale.
  • Korea University Global MBA: recipients must keep a GPA of 3.0 or higher and avoid academic dishonesty to retain funding across both semesters.
  • Yonsei: scholars must keep a minimum GPA of 3.0 on a 4.3 scale during each renewal period, fulfil a 10-hour work requirement, and are evaluated before each new semester.
  • SKK GSB fellowships: the Study Abroad and Kelley dual-degree fellowships list at least two years of work experience, the ability to maintain a 3.75 or higher GPA, and a language score of TOEFL iBT 100, IELTS 7.0 or TOEIC 900 or above.

Treat each condition as a budget risk. If you lose a partial award in the second semester, you would need to cover the difference yourself.

Admission requirements that shape your chances

Scholarship decisions follow admission, so the entry requirements matter. The Korea University FAQ says candidates with at least two years of professional experience are preferred but not required, that military service counts as career experience, and that major English tests (TOEIC, TOEFL, GMAT, TEPS) are accepted with no minimum score, although English proficiency is assessed in the interview. It also states that the Global MBA is a full-time day programme that cannot be pursued while working, and that it admits in three rounds (January, March and April). KAIST's page says it prefers candidates with more than two years of work experience.

A practical checklist before you apply

  1. List the two or three schools you are serious about and open each one's financial aid page, not a third-party summary.
  2. For each, write down: award name, percentage or items covered, whether it is automatic or needs an application, and the renewal conditions.
  3. Check the tuition for the current admission year, since the amount can change.
  4. Note which awards require extra documents, such as recommendation letters or financial proof, and who must write them.
  5. If you intend to use the GKS, find out from the school how nomination works and compare dates with the embassy or university track.
  6. Ask the admissions office to confirm in writing what your offer covers, including whether the award applies to both semesters or years.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Assuming "up to" means that amount. "Up to 50%" or "up to 20%" is a ceiling, not a promise.
  • Counting an award that is case by case. Korea University describes its award that way, so plan for the possibility of no award.
  • Ignoring the renewal GPA. A strict GPA floor can end funding after the first term.
  • Forgetting costs outside tuition. A tuition award does not mention housing or daily living costs unless the page says a stipend is included, as KAIST does for selected students.
  • Overlooking work obligations. Yonsei's 10-hour work requirement affects your schedule.

Rules change: confirm with the official body

This article is general information, not financial or immigration advice. Scholarship amounts, tuition and conditions are set by each school and by the government agency that runs the GKS, and they are revised from year to year. Confirm the current figures with the business school's admissions office and, for the GKS, with the Study in Korea portal and the Korean embassy in your country. If you will need a student visa, check the requirements with your embassy and HiKorea.
