Open the official page that matches your question, not the one that sounds most general. For a visa, start at the Korea Visa Portal; for the rules once you are in Korea, start at HiKorea; for choosing a programme and finding student support, start at Study in Korea. Travel planning has its own official source in VisitKorea. Each of these answers a different question, and mixing them is how readers end up with the wrong deadline or the wrong document list.

Question: which visa do I need?

According to the Korea Visa Portal (visa.go.kr), the government site offers electronic visa applications, application forms and document guidance, visa issuance certificates and application status tracking. It also provides a Visa Navigator that matches an applicant to a visa type, with visa categories organised by purpose such as tourism, employment, investment or family visits, plus fee information. The portal is available in Korean, English and Chinese and has FAQs and a visa application center section.

Use it first when you are still outside Korea or when you are unsure which visa category fits. Note down the visa type and the issuing office, because later steps depend on them.

Question: what must I do once I am living in Korea?

HiKorea (hikorea.go.kr) is described on its own front page as the e-government platform for immigration and foreign resident services. It offers electronic civil application submission, visit reservations at immigration offices, online landing permits and visa issuance acknowledgement letters. It also lets users track an application, check the validity of a residence card, check a stay expiration date, look up whether a foreign worker is eligible for employment and find designated medical institutions. The site provides visa navigation tools and stay-status manuals as well.

Several local government pages in English tell foreign residents to reserve a visit through HiKorea before going to an immigration office. A multilingual foreign resident hotline, 1345, is listed on the site; it operates on weekdays from 9 a.m. to 10 p.m. excluding holidays, and calls from within Korea are fee-based. HiKorea also warns against counterfeit electronic arrival card websites that ask for payment, which is a reminder to check that you are on the official domain.

Question: where do I find a programme and student support?

Study in Korea (studyinkorea.go.kr) is aimed at international students. Its English front page shows recent articles, notices, news and a "Study in Korea Life" section, and it lists an International Student Service Center with help in English and Chinese. Use it to discover programmes and institutions, then move to the university's own admissions guide for the current requirements, deadlines and intake details. Keep the programme, campus and intake together in your notes, since each application can differ.

Question: who can help with health insurance or marriage-related matters?

Two other official bodies come up often. The National Health Insurance Service (NHIS) has an English page on health insurance for foreigners. According to that page, employed foreigners are compulsorily enrolled, and self-employed foreigners with more than six months of residence must enroll. For families formed through marriage, the Danuri portal, operated by the Korea Institute for Healthy Family, offers information in 13 languages, a helpline (1577-1366) and a locator for multicultural family support centers.

The government's foreigner services portal on gov.kr hosts services such as the mandatory international marriage guidance programme registration for Korean nationals marrying citizens of designated countries. Whether it applies to you depends on nationalities and circumstances.

Question: where do I get general travel help?

VisitKorea, the Korea Tourism Organization's site, lists a 1330 Korea Travel Helpline at +82-2-1330 with multilingual assistance, and publishes practical pages such as transportation cards and seasonal flower guides. It is the right place for sightseeing and transport questions, but not for visa conditions or permitted activities. For permitted activities, return to HiKorea or the Korea Visa Portal.

A simple routing checklist

  1. Write the question in one line. "Which visa?", "Am I allowed to work?", "How do I register?", "Which programme?" or "How do I get to a site?"
  2. Pick the matching official portal from the sections above.
  3. Check the domain. The portals above are hikorea.go.kr, visa.go.kr and studyinkorea.go.kr. Be cautious of look-alike sites that charge fees.
  4. Save the page date and a screenshot of any rule you rely on.
  5. Confirm by phone if the situation is unusual. HiKorea's hotline is 1345; the Study in Korea service center lists English and Chinese phone lines.
  6. Keep one folder per application, with the visa type, the institution and the dates together.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Using an agent's summary instead of the portal. Requirements are set by the Immigration Service and the issuing office, not by third parties.
  • Applying the wrong visa category's rules. Permitted activities depend on the status of stay.
  • Paying on look-alike sites. HiKorea itself warns about counterfeit electronic arrival card sites.
  • Skipping reservations. Several local government pages say visits to an immigration office must be reserved through HiKorea first, with some exceptions, so check before you travel.
  • Assuming the university's page and the immigration page agree. If they differ, ask both in writing.

Rules change: confirm with the official bodies

This article is general information and not legal or immigration advice. Visa categories, document lists, fees and permitted activities are decided by the Ministry of Justice and its immigration offices, and other rules by the bodies named above, and all can change. Always confirm your own situation with the Korea Visa Portal, HiKorea, the issuing consulate or office, or your institution before acting.
