A photography brief for a Korean wedding works best when it has two halves: a short list of the people and moments that matter to you, and a written list of exactly what the photography price includes. The second half matters more than most couples expect, because Korea's Fair Trade Commission and the Korea Consumer Agency have both pointed to unclear photography, dress and makeup packages (often called "studio, dress, makeup" bundles) as a repeat source of disputes.

Start with the people and moments, not a shot list

A brief is a one-page note you send before the quote is accepted. It tells the photographer what you care about so that the quote matches your day. Keep it short enough to be read in a minute.

  • Must-have groups: name the family groupings you want, in order of priority, so the photographer can plan the time.
  • Must-have moments: for example the ceremony, an exchange of gifts, or a quiet portrait session. Three to five items is easier to deliver than twenty.
  • Language and names: if guests come from different countries, give the photographer a list of names with relationships. Spelling in both scripts helps with captions and album pages.
  • Practical limits: venue rules, time windows, and anyone who prefers not to be photographed.

The brief is not a contract. Its job is to make the later contract specific.

Turn the brief into itemised inclusions

According to a report in Seoul Shinmun on the Fair Trade Commission's standard contract for wedding preparation services, items that had been sold as optional extras, such as dress fitting fees and the cost of buying photo files, now belong in the base service. The same report says businesses must display base services and add-on options with a price for each, and must provide and explain the price schedule when a customer asks.

Use that as the structure for your questions. Ask the provider to write down, next to a price:

  1. Shooting time and whether it covers the ceremony, the studio session or both.
  2. Whether original and edited photo files are included in the base price, or sold separately.
  3. How many edited images are delivered, how they are delivered, and by what date.
  4. Whether an album, framed print or other product is a base item or an add-on, and its price.
  5. Any charge for early starts, additional locations or extra people.

An Economist (Korea) article on the commission's enforcement action describes how essential services had been split into twenty or thirty separately priced options, which made comparison between vendors difficult. It also reports that the commission objected to extra charges described only as "separate" without an amount or a price range. If your quote says "additional fees apply", ask for the amounts in writing.

Read the cancellation and refund section before the price

The Korea Development Institute's policy portal summarises the standard terms for wedding preparation services as requiring contracts to state refund and penalty criteria when a contract is cancelled or terminated. It also says providers must confirm the customer's statutory right to cancel within the cooling-off period and may not charge extra when a change in service is caused by the provider.

Cancellation is where most disputes arise. Asiae reported that consumer dispute-resolution requests about wedding services rose from 905 cases to 1,076, and that about 88.1 percent of complaints involved contract cancellation, penalty fees or withdrawal of offers. For an international couple whose plans may depend on visas, travel dates and family schedules, the refund rules deserve a close reading.

  • What share of the fee is refundable at different dates before the shoot?
  • Does the answer change once work has started, for example after a studio session?
  • Can the contract be transferred to another person if the wedding is called off?
  • Who is responsible if the provider uses a subcontractor and a dispute arises?

The Economist article lists clauses the commission treated as unfair, including unclear refund rules that ignore whether the service has begun, clauses that exempt the agency from disputes between customers and individual vendors, and restrictions on transferring the contract. Check whether any of these appear in the contract you are offered.

Compare prices before you sign

Both Asiae and Seoul Shinmun report that the Korea Consumer Agency runs a price-comparison site called Chamgagyeok (참가격) where couples can check prices by region, and that businesses using the standard terms for wedding preparation services disclose base prices and penalty criteria. Asiae also reports that a price display system now requires fees and refund standards to be shown, with penalties of up to 100 million won for violations and Seoul Shinmun says the display is now mandatory.

These articles describe rules aimed at wedding preparation businesses. If you book a freelance photographer directly, ask whether the same standard terms apply, and ask for the same itemised, written quote either way.

Checklist before you pay a deposit

  1. Send the one-page brief and ask the photographer to confirm in writing that the quote reflects it.
  2. Get the base package and each add-on priced separately, in a document you can keep.
  3. Confirm whether photo files (original and edited) are in the base price.
  4. Read the refund and penalty table and note the dates it depends on.
  5. Ask for a Korean-language contract and a plain-language summary in your own language, and have a Korean-speaking friend or an interpreter check that they match.
  6. Keep all messages, quotes and receipts together.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Accepting a headline price. Asiae and the Economist describe quotes where extras were added later. The Economist cites a survey in which 74.2 percent of customers considered the additional charges inappropriate, and reports that couples paid on average 1.44 million won more than first quoted.
  • Treating the brief as the agreement. Only the signed contract counts. Make sure promises from the brief appear in it.
  • Skipping the penalty table. A low price can come with steep cancellation costs.
  • Waiting too long to complain. Asiae notes that the Consumer Counseling Center (1372) and the Consumer24 website handle wedding service disputes.

Rules change: confirm with the official body

This is general information, not legal or financial advice. Standard terms, price display rules and penalty amounts can be revised. Before signing, check the current standard terms and your rights with the Korea Consumer Agency or the Fair Trade Commission, or call the consumer helpline at 1372.
