To choose a facility in Korea, start with official directories rather than advertising. Medical Korea, the international patient portal, keeps a searchable list of registered hospitals and facilitators, and a separate list of hospitals that hold the government's accreditation for serving foreign patients. Check that your clinic appears in the directory, look at what language services it lists, and only then compare doctors and prices.

Registered hospitals and facilitators

Medical Korea's home page presents directories of Registered Hospitals and Registered Facilitators, and states that cumulative foreign patients have reached 5.05 million. The registered hospital list is large: when checked for this article it showed 4,160 facilities across 18 regions. Categories include tertiary general hospital, general hospital, hospital, clinic, dental clinic and Korean medicine hospital, and each entry shows a registration ID, address, phone, email and website, along with contact details for the international healthcare center where one exists.

The directory page does not explain the registration criteria, so read it as a first filter rather than a quality rating. If a clinic or agent contacts you and cannot be found in the list, ask them to explain why before you send money.

The Korea JoongAng Daily reports a rise in cases involving unregistered brokers collecting illegal referral fees, which is a good reason to check any facilitator you are offered.

Accredited hospitals for international patients

Korea also runs a voluntary accreditation, the Korean Accreditation Program for Hospitals Serving Foreign Patients (KAHF). Korea Biomedical Review reports that the Ministry of Health and Welfare set it up in 2017 and evaluates criteria such as multilingual consultation, interpretation service and infection control, with the assessment conducted by KHIDI and KOIHA. Certification validity was extended from two to four years in a December 2022 policy revision, and a one-year conditional certification exists for institutions that show a commitment to improve.

The Medical Korea accredited hospital page lets you filter by region, specialty and language. It listed 24 accredited hospitals when checked, with interpretation in languages such as English, Chinese, Russian, Japanese and Arabic. It also mentions services that accredited institutions typically offer, such as interpretation, airport pickup, hotel reservation help and English-language medical documents; confirm each service with the hospital itself.

The number of accredited hospitals is far smaller than the number of registered ones. That is not a reason to reject a registered clinic, but it is a reason to ask what that clinic does for foreign patients, such as interpreters and written consent forms in your language.

Language and interpretation support

Medical Korea's useful-sites page points to two interpretation providers: the Korea Tourism Organization, which offers English, Japanese and Chinese, and BBB Korea, which covers more than 19 languages including Arabic. The information centers at Seoul Station and Incheon Airport also offer multilingual consultation.

Ask the facility whether the interpreter is an employee or arranged by an agent, whether the interpreter will be present at the consultation and during procedures, and whether consent forms are provided in your language.

Preparing for the treatment

  1. Gather your medical records, diagnosis, current medication list and allergies, with translations where possible.
  2. Send them to the clinic in advance so the doctor can plan the consultation.
  3. Ask for a written treatment plan and itemized estimate.
  4. Confirm your entry status. Medical Korea describes the C-3-3 short-term medical visa (treatment and travel of 90 days or less) and the G-1-10 for up to one year.
  5. Confirm insurance. VisitKorea says non-resident foreign patients are not eligible for Korean insurance coverage, so contact your own insurer about international coverage and required paperwork.
  6. Plan follow-up care at home, and ask the clinic to provide a summary of treatment you can give to your own doctor.

Questions to ask before choosing

  • Is the facility on Medical Korea's registered list, and is it KAHF-accredited?
  • Who exactly will perform the procedure, and are they licensed in the relevant specialty?
  • What are the risks and recovery time, and what happens if complications occur after you leave?
  • Is an interpreter present at every key step?
  • What does the estimate include, and what is billed separately?
  • How are follow-up questions handled after you fly home?

If you need to raise a problem

The Korea Medical Dispute Mediation and Arbitration Agency lists a counseling center at +82-2-1670-2545 or +82-2-6210-0080 and an email address for inquiries. Medical Korea also offers dispute consultation through its information centers. Keep the estimate, receipts, medical records and messages, since these are the basis of any complaint.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Choosing a clinic from social media or an advertisement without checking the registered list.
  • Treating registration and accreditation as the same thing.
  • Relying on an agent who is not on the registered facilitator list.
  • Skipping the written estimate and consent form.
  • Assuming interpretation is included in the price.
  • Booking a return flight before the follow-up visit is confirmed.

Rules change, so confirm with the official bodies

This is general information, not medical or legal advice. Lists, accreditation details and visa rules are updated by the authorities. Confirm facility status on the Medical Korea portal or through the Ministry of Health and Welfare, visa rules with a Korean consulate, and clinical questions with a licensed doctor.
