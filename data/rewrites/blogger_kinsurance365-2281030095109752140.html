For general questions about Korean National Health Insurance (NHIS), call 1577-1000. If you need help in English, Chinese, Uzbek or Vietnamese, dial 1577-1000 and press extension 6, or call 033-811-2000 directly. According to the NHIS English contact page, service hours are 9 AM to 6 PM. Callers from abroad use 82-33-811-2001.

The numbers and what each one is for

  • 1577-1000: the main NHIS line for general questions about enrollment, premium payment and coverage.
  • 1577-1000, extension 6: the foreign-language option. The NHIS contact page lists English, Chinese, Uzbek and Vietnamese.
  • 033-811-2000: the direct number for the same foreign-language service. The NHIS page lists 82-33-811-2001 for international callers.

The NHIS headquarters is in Wonju, Gangwon State, but you do not need to visit it. Local offices and the foreign resident centers described below handle in-person cases.

In-person help for foreign residents

The NHIS contact page says the agency works with organizations that assist international residents, including the Korea Support Center for Foreign Workers and Danuri, the portal for foreign residents. A guide published by Seoulstart lists five NHIS Centers for Foreign Residents, in Seoul (Guro-gu), Ansan, Suwon, Incheon and Uijeongbu. Check the NHIS website for current addresses and opening hours before you travel, since that guide is a secondary source.

University pages also give local numbers. Sungkyunkwan University's international office lists a Seoul center at 02-760-0026 and a Suwon center at 031-290-5026. Treat these as examples to confirm, not permanent contacts.

If your question is about premiums

The phone line can explain how your own premium is calculated, but it helps to know the basic structure first. The Seoulstart guide says employees pay 7.19% of monthly salary in total, split evenly between employee and employer at 3.595% each, and gives an example of 107,850 won each at a 3,000,000 won salary. For people enrolled as regional subscribers, it says the premium is a contribution score multiplied by a per-point value of 211.5 won, with the score based on household income, property and car ownership, and a minimum applying if the calculated amount falls below a set average. These are figures from a secondary guide for the current rate year, so ask NHIS to confirm the numbers that apply to you.

When you call, describe your situation in one sentence, for example "I am a D-2 student, registered on this date, and I received a bill I do not understand." Agents can then check your record faster than if you start with a general question.

Questions to ask on the line

The foreign-language line is most useful when you can state your situation clearly. Have the following ready:

  1. Your visa type and alien registration status.
  2. Whether you are an employee, a student, or neither. Employees are enrolled through their workplace, while others can be enrolled as regional subscribers.
  3. The date you arrived and the date you completed alien registration.
  4. Your Korean address, because insurance cards and notices are mailed there. The SKKU page says the card and a subscription guide are mailed to your Korean address.

How enrollment timing can differ

Timing depends on status. The Seoulstart guide says foreigners on long-stay visas become subject to mandatory enrollment after six months of residence, while employees are registered by their employers from the start. It lists D-2 students, F-5 permanent residents, F-6 marriage migrants and E-9 workers among groups enrolled without the wait. The SKKU page is more specific for students: D-2 students are enrolled from alien registration, while D-4 language trainees are listed as enrolled six months after arrival.

The SKKU page also advises students to arrange personal insurance for the gap before alien registration is processed, and says it can take 6 to 8 weeks. Because that is one university's guidance, ask NHIS or your school what applies to you.

Before you call: a short checklist

  • Call between 9 AM and 6 PM Korean time.
  • Keep your passport, alien registration number and any NHIS notice number within reach.
  • Write down the date, the agent's name if given, and what was said, so you can refer back to it.
  • If the issue involves money, ask how to get the figure confirmed in writing before paying.

If the call does not resolve your question, ask the agent what to bring to an in-person visit and to which center. Bringing your alien registration card, proof of address and any income or no-income declaration, as the Seoulstart guide lists for regional enrollment, usually saves a second trip. Confirm the list with NHIS before you go, since requirements can differ by case.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Dialing the foreign-language line without the extension. Press 6 after 1577-1000, or use the direct number.
  • Calling outside Korea with the domestic number. The NHIS page gives a separate international number.
  • Assuming your visa follows the same timing as a friend's. Enrollment dates differ by visa and status.
  • Ignoring mailed notices. A wrong address on file can delay cards and premium bills.

Rules change: confirm with NHIS

This is general information, not insurance, legal or medical advice. Phone numbers, enrollment rules and premium rates can change. Confirm current details with the National Health Insurance Service, using the contact page below, before you rely on anything here.
