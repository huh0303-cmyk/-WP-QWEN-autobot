Comparing financial products in Korea works best when you compare like with like and write the terms down in one table: the product type, the rate, every cost, the conditions for getting the advertised rate, and whether the money is protected if the institution fails. The Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) runs a comparison portal called FINE that puts savings, deposit and loan products from many institutions side by side, and the Korea Deposit Insurance Corporation (KDIC) states what its protection covers.

Start with the same type of product

A deposit, a fund and a trading account answer different needs, so a rate for one cannot be set against a projected return on another. KDIC's coverage page shows how different they are in one respect: protected products include bank demand, time and savings deposits, installment deposits and foreign currency deposits, while the page lists certificates of deposit, repurchase agreements, mutual funds, money market funds, bonds issued by financial institutions and performance-based trusts as not protected. If a fund and a savings account are on your shortlist, they are not offering the same promise.

According to the FSS page for 금융상품한눈에 on the FINE portal, the service compares savings, deposit and loan products across about 140 financial institutions, including banks and insurance companies, and shows interest rates and transaction conditions. The FSS operates the disclosure service, which sits within its consumer financial information portal. The portal is in Korean, so foreign residents may want help reading it.

Use it to build a shortlist, then confirm the final terms with the institution itself, since a comparison site shows disclosed information and not your personal offer.

Build the one-page comparison sheet

Create a table with one column per product and these rows:

  1. Provider and product type: bank deposit, installment savings, loan, fund, trading account.
  2. Stated rate: and what the rate is based on.
  3. Conditions for the rate: salary transfer, card use, minimum balance, term length.
  4. Costs: opening, ongoing, transaction and withdrawal or early-exit costs, entered only when the provider states them.
  5. Access: minimum deposit, term, how and when you can withdraw.
  6. Documents needed: identification, proof of residence or income, and whether a foreigner registration card is accepted.
  7. Protection: whether KDIC deposit protection applies.
  8. Source and date: where you read each figure and when.

Leave a cell blank when a number is not stated rather than filling in an estimate. A blank is a question to ask the provider.

Check deposit protection by institution

KDIC's page says the coverage is calculated per depositor, per institution, and not per type of deposit or per branch. The Korea Times reported that the cap would double from 50 million won to 100 million won, calculated per person at each financial institution, and KDIC's page states the current limit as 100 million won per depositor, covering principal and designated interest. KDIC's page also carries older wording of 50 million won in places, so read the current limit directly on the KDIC site and do not rely on a summary. The protection applies if an insured institution fails; it is not a guarantee of investment returns.

If you hold several accounts at one institution, they count together toward the limit. Spreading savings across institutions changes the protected total, though convenience and conditions matter as well.

Questions to ask the provider before you commit

  1. Which of the conditions for the advertised rate would apply to me, as a foreign resident or as a non-resident?
  2. What happens to the rate and to my money if I close the account early?
  3. Which fees are charged, when, and where are they written in the contract?
  4. Is this product covered by deposit protection, and where does it say so?
  5. Can I receive the full terms in a document I can keep, and in a language I can read or have translated?

Write the answers into the sheet with the date and the name of the person who gave them. A comparison is only as reliable as its sources, and a dated record makes it easier to raise a problem later.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Comparing a headline savings rate with a projected fund return as if both were guaranteed.
  • Reading only the top advertised rate and missing the conditions needed to receive it.
  • Assuming every product at a bank is protected. KDIC lists funds, CDs and several other products as not covered.
  • Counting protection per account rather than per depositor per institution.
  • Using an old article for rates or limits. Rates change often, and the protection limit has already been revised once.
  • Skipping the Korean-language terms. Ask for a written explanation of anything you cannot read.

If you believe a product was sold to you with an inadequate explanation, the FSS says it handles complaints and disputes involving banking, insurance, financial investment, credit cards, savings banks and foreign exchange, and operates a dispute mediation committee.
