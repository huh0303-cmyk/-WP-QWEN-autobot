For a K-pop concert or fan event in Seoul, the simplest setup is a T-money card loaded with enough balance for the day, a transit app that works in English, and a note of the last train from your venue station. T-money is a prepaid card used on subways and buses across Korea, and you can buy one without showing identification, according to a foreigner-focused guide by Lacha.

Buying and loading a T-money card

According to a T-money guide on seoultourism.org, cards are sold at convenience stores (CU, GS25, 7-Eleven, Emart24, MiniStop), at subway station vending machines, and at Incheon and Gimpo airports. The same guide gives a basic card price of roughly 2,500 to 4,000 won, with character editions costing more, and warns that the card price itself is not refundable. Only the balance you load can be recovered.

Loading rules reported by the two guides are consistent on the main points:

  • The minimum top-up is 1,000 won, and convenience stores usually load in 1,000-won steps.
  • The seoultourism.org guide gives a maximum of 90,000 won per top-up.
  • Convenience store clerks typically take cash. Lacha notes that buying the card at a convenience store usually requires Korean cash.

Payment options for foreign cards have been changing. The seoultourism.org guide says that as of March 2026, subway station kiosks accept major foreign cards, while Lacha reports that about 440 new machines accept overseas cards for single-journey tickets and short-term passes, not for ordinary T-money top-ups. Because the two descriptions differ, carry some cash as a backup and read the kiosk screen before relying on a card.

How transfers and fares work

Seoul's system is integrated, so the fare logic matters more than the exact fare. The Seoul-focused transit guide from SeoulStart says you can make up to four transfers, five rides in total, on one fare if you tag within 30 minutes of getting off, and that the window is extended to 60 minutes between 9 PM and 7 AM. Its key rule is that you must tag your card both when boarding and when getting off. If you skip the tag on exit, the transfer chain breaks and you may be charged a new base fare.

Fare amounts have been revised over time, and the Visit Seoul transport page itself dates its fare table to an earlier period, so check current fares in the station or on the operator's site before you budget.

Getting your money back

Before leaving Korea, you can refund the unused balance. Both guides say that balances up to 20,000 won can be refunded at a convenience store such as GS25 or CU for a 500-won fee. According to the seoultourism.org guide, larger balances are handled at T-money service centers, for example at Seoul Station and Myeongdong, and you should bring your passport. Lacha adds that balances below 1,550 won are refunded without the fee. Details vary by card brand, so confirm at the counter. Since the refund process takes a few minutes, do it on the day before your flight rather than at the airport gate.

Digital tools worth installing

SeoulStart lists several options with English support:

  • Seoul Subway: the city's official app for foreigners, launched in 2023, with real-time timetables and wrong-train alerts.
  • Subway Korea: a third-party app covering five cities with English, Japanese and Chinese support.
  • KakaoMetro: Kakao's subway app, which the guide describes as Korean-first with possible English gaps.
  • Naver Map: a general map and route planner mentioned alongside the other options.

For buses, SeoulStart says to look for the five-digit ARS ID on the bus stop sign, which gives live arrival information instead of a printed schedule.

A plan for event day

  1. The day before: check your balance and top it up, and download one English-capable transit app.
  2. Look up the venue's nearest station and the route from your lodging, and save two alternatives.
  3. Check the last train time from your venue station. SeoulStart says there is no single citywide last-train time. Seoul Metro publishes departures station by station, and transfer stations can close earlier than your starting station.
  4. Plan how you will get home if you miss the last train, such as a taxi or a late bus, and estimate the cost in advance.
  5. Tag in and out every time so your transfer discounts work.

Visit Seoul reports that the subway runs from about 5:30 AM to midnight, with color-coded lines and signage in Korean, English and Chinese. Treat these as typical hours and check the specific line and station.

Common mistakes and what to verify

  • Arriving without cash and assuming every counter accepts a foreign card.
  • Forgetting to tag when getting off, which can reset the transfer discount.
  • Assuming a single last-train time for the whole city.
  • Loading far more than you need, then facing a refund fee or a service-center trip.
  • Relying on one app only. If it fails or lacks English coverage, you need a second option.
  • Not reading the venue and event organizer's own transport notices, which can differ from general guidance.

Details change, so confirm

Card prices, refund fees, payment options and operating hours are set by the transit operators and can change. This is general travel information, not an official service notice. Check the T-money, Seoul Metro and Visit Seoul official pages, or station staff, for current details close to your travel date.
